We treat happiness as a thing to acquire, like a prize waiting at the end of some achievement. Hesse corrects this: happiness is a how, a way of living, not a what. It is a talent to be developed, like playing an instrument. Some people practice it daily and grow skilled at finding light in hard places; others wait for it to arrive and wonder why it never does. The good news is that talents can be learned. Begin small, practice noticing what is good, and happiness becomes less of a mystery and more of a habit.
