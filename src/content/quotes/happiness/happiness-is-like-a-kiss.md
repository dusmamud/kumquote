Meltzer compares happiness to a kiss: it only works when shared. Kept to ourselves, happiness shrinks. Shared — through kindness, laughter, encouragement, or simple presence — it grows. We often treat happiness as a private possession to protect, yet the people who seem happiest are usually the ones giving it away. A compliment costs nothing and returns warmth to the giver. A shared joy is doubled; a shared burden is halved. Happiness is not a scarce resource. Like a kiss, its value appears in the giving, and the giver always receives something back.
