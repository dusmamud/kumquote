Tolstoy packs a whole philosophy into six words. We often treat happiness as something that must be earned through effort, achievement, or the right conditions, and so we postpone it indefinitely. His blunt reply is that happiness is available the moment we stop deferring it. To be happy is not to solve every problem first; it is to decide that joy is allowed now, in the middle of an imperfect life. The sentence is almost a command: stop waiting for permission from the future. Simplicity is its strength.
