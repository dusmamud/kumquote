This line treats happiness as a decision rather than a stroke of luck. Circumstances shift constantly, and waiting for perfect conditions means waiting forever. Choosing happiness means deciding how to respond to what happens instead of letting events dictate your mood. It does not deny hardship or pretend problems away; it claims agency within them. A person can choose gratitude over complaint, patience over irritation, and hope over bitterness. That choice, repeated daily, becomes a habit of mind. Over time the habit shapes a life that feels content even when it is imperfect.
