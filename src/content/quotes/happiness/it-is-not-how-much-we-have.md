Wealth is usually measured by what we own, but this quote measures it by what we enjoy. A person can possess a great deal and still feel empty, rushing past every pleasure in search of the next one. Enjoyment is the quiet skill of noticing what is already here — a meal, a conversation, a morning walk — and letting it be enough. Spurgeon points out that happiness does not grow by adding more things to our lives. It grows when we learn to take pleasure in what we already have, turning ordinary moments into sources of real contentment.
