Chasing happiness directly often pushes it further away, like a butterfly startled by eager hands. The harder we grasp at it, the more it slips beyond reach. Hawthorne suggests a gentler approach: sit still, be present, and let joy come to you. When we stop demanding happiness and simply attend to the life in front of us, contentment often settles quietly on its own. The lesson is patience. A calm mind, open to small pleasures, is far more likely to be visited by happiness than a restless one forever hunting it down.
