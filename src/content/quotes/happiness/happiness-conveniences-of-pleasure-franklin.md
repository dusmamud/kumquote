Franklin argues that happiness is built from small, repeated pleasures rather than rare windfalls. A promotion or a lottery win may thrill us briefly, but such events are few and their glow fades fast. What truly sustains a good life is the daily supply of modest comforts: a warm breakfast, a friendly greeting, an evening walk, a quiet hour with a book. These small conveniences compound, day after day, into a steady sense of well-being. The wise person therefore invests in ordinary pleasures and tends them carefully, knowing that a happy life is the sum of many small good days.
