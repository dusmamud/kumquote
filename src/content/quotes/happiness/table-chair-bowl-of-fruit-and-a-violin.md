Einstein lists the smallest possible inventory for a good life: a place to sit, a place to work, food, and music. The point is not poverty but sufficiency, the discovery that happiness needs far less than advertising claims. A table and a chair mean shelter and purpose; fruit means simple nourishment; a violin means beauty for its own sake. Everything else is decoration. When we confuse decoration with necessity, we spend our lives chasing extras and calling the chase happiness. Einstein suggests the opposite: want less, notice more, and let a quiet room be enough.
