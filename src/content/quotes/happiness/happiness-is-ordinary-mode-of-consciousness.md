Murdoch finds happiness in an active, outward-looking mind. It is not found in luxury or in constant self-examination, but in a consciousness that is busy, lively, and free from obsession with itself. When our attention is engaged — by work, by people, by the world around us — we forget to ask whether we are happy, and happiness slips in quietly. Self-absorption, by contrast, magnifies every small discomfort. The happiest days are often the ones in which we were too interested in life to measure it. Happiness follows attention, not the other way around.
