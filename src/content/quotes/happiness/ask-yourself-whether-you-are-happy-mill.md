Like trying to watch ourselves fall asleep, constant self-checking pulls us out of the very experience we want to enjoy. Happiness lives in absorption, in being lost in good work, good company, or a beautiful moment, not in auditing our feelings. When we keep asking whether we are happy enough, we trade presence for performance and turn joy into a test we can fail. The wiser move is to look outward, engage fully, and let happiness be discovered later, in hindsight, rather than demanded in the moment.
