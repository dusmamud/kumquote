Gilbert refuses the idea that happiness simply happens to lucky people. She describes it as something earned through effort, a thing we fight for, strive for, and insist upon. That may sound unromantic, but it is actually liberating: if happiness depends on our own effort, then it is within our reach. The effort is not frantic chasing; it is the steady work of building a life aligned with what matters, choosing better habits, healing old wounds, and sometimes travelling far, literally or inwardly, to find ourselves. Happiness rewards those who treat it as a practice rather than an accident.
