Fyodor Dostoevsky argues that happiness begins with diagnosis. Unhappiness that we cannot name feels like weather — something that simply happens to us, beyond our control. But once we find its source — a fear, a habit, a relationship, a lie we tell ourselves — it becomes a problem we can work on. Knowing the cause does not instantly remove the pain, yet it changes our relationship to it. We stop being victims of a mystery and become students of our own lives. The greatest happiness, then, is not the absence of trouble, but the clarity that lets us face it.
