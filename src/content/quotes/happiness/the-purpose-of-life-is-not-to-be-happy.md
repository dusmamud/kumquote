Emerson pushes back against the idea that happiness is life's main purpose. A life aimed only at feeling good can become small and self-absorbed, while a life aimed at being useful naturally gathers meaning. Usefulness, honour, and compassion give our days weight: they connect us to other people and to something larger than our own comfort. When we know our presence made a difference, satisfaction follows more deeply than any pleasure. Happiness is not rejected here, only reordered. Put contribution first, Emerson suggests, and happiness becomes the quiet reward of a life that mattered, rather than its empty goal.
