Lincoln, a man who knew hardship intimately, reduces happiness to a decision. Circumstances matter, of course, but two people in the same situation can live very different lives depending on the attitude they choose. One dwells on what is missing; the other notices what is still good. The phrase "make up their minds" is the key — happiness is not an accident but a deliberate stance. It does not mean denying pain or pretending everything is fine. It means deciding, again and again, to look for what is worth appreciating. That decision is always available, in any season of life.
