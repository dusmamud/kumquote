Saroyan points to a paradox: the more desperately we need happiness, the more it escapes us. Needing it turns every ordinary day into a disappointment and every setback into proof of failure. But when we stop requiring happiness as a condition for a good life, its grip loosens. We can be at peace in difficult seasons and still notice beauty. This freedom is itself the greatest happiness — a quiet contentment that does not depend on circumstances cooperating, but on accepting life as it comes.
