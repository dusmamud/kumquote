Hoffer points to a painful paradox. Searching for happiness turns it into a test we keep failing: every present moment is judged against a future where we will finally feel right. The search keeps us scanning for what is missing instead of noticing what is already here. It also makes happiness feel like a possession to acquire rather than a quality of attention. The way out is not to abandon the wish for a good life but to stop treating it as a destination. Live the day in front of you, and happiness often arrives as a side effect.
