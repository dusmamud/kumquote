Aristotle placed happiness at the top of human aims because everything else is pursued for its sake. Wealth, honour, and pleasure are wanted as means to something further, but happiness is wanted for itself, complete and needing nothing added. This does not mean chasing easy pleasure; for Aristotle, happiness grows from living well, acting wisely, and fulfilling your nature over a whole lifetime. The idea invites a question whenever you choose: does this lead toward a life I would call good, or only toward a momentary thrill? Aim at the highest good, and the smaller goods fall into place.
