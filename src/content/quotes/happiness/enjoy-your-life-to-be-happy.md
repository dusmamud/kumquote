Hepburn's words cut through the noise of ambition and achievement to name what actually counts. We spend years chasing milestones, believing happiness waits at the end of some accomplishment, yet the quote insists that enjoying life is not a reward for success but the point itself. Happiness here is not frivolous indulgence; it is attention to the present, to people, to small pleasures. When enjoyment becomes the measure, choices simplify. A life organized around joy, connection, and gratitude leaves less room for regret, because the days themselves were the destination all along.
