Ovid pictures worry as a chain, heavy, invisible, and fastened by our own hands. We drag it everywhere, rehearsing disasters that never happen and replaying mistakes that cannot be undone. Breaking the chain is not a single dramatic act but a decision repeated daily: to set down what cannot be controlled and refuse to carry it further. The happy person is not someone with no troubles, but someone who has stopped volunteering for extra ones. Freedom begins when worry loses its grip, and peace is what remains when the weight is gone.
