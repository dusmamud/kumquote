Tennyson rejects the idea that happiness means a life without strong feelings or desires. Passions are not the enemy; being ruled by them is. A person who masters anger, fear, and appetite is free to choose rather than react, and that freedom is the ground of happiness. Absence of passion would be emptiness, not joy. The happy life is an engaged one, where desires are felt fully but governed wisely, so they serve our deeper purposes instead of dragging us from one impulse to the next.
