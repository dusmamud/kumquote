Conroy treats happiness as a rare gift rather than a guaranteed right. Most of life is ordinary effort, and joy arrives unannounced, like sunlight through a cloud, without being summoned or deserved. Calling it an aberration is a warning against taking it for granted: it can pass as quickly as it came. The proper response is attention, to notice the moment while it lasts and to let it sink in fully. Beauty is sharper when we remember it was never promised.
