Palahniuk argues that real happiness cannot be reached while we stay guarded. We protect ourselves with irony, distance, and careful plans, and those defences work, but they also keep joy out along with pain. To be cut open means to be seen honestly, to love without guarantees, and to admit what we actually want. It is frightening because openness can wound us. Yet the alternative is a life that is safe and numb. Happiness lives on the far side of vulnerability, and the risk is not optional; it is the entrance fee.
