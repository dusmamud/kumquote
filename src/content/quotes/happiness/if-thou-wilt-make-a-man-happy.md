Epicurus points to the arithmetic of happiness: it is the gap between what we have and what we want. Adding riches widens the having side, but desires tend to grow just as fast, so the gap never closes. Shrinking our wants is the surer path, because each desire we release is one less thing standing between us and contentment. This is not a call to own nothing, but to notice how many of our wants were borrowed from other people's lives. A person who wants little is rich in the only way that lasts — nothing is missing.
