Schweitzer reduces happiness to two ingredients most of us overlook: a healthy body and a short memory for slights. Good health is easy to ignore until it falters, yet it colors every single day. And a bad memory, in his sense, is a gift: the ability to let go of insults, mistakes, and old grudges instead of replaying them nightly. People who carry every hurt stay heavy; people who release them travel light. Eat well, move often, sleep enough, and forgive quickly. Happiness rarely needs grand purchases or achievements; it mostly needs a body cared for and a mind unburdened.
