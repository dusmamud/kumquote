Happiness rarely lives at the extremes. Too little leaves us restless and wanting, while too much dulls appetite, breeds boredom, and turns comfort into a cage. Pollock pictures happiness as a resting place in the middle, where needs are met but desire still has somewhere to go. The image suits ordinary life: enough work to feel useful, enough rest to recover, enough wealth to be secure without being owned by it. Chasing more of everything rarely helps, because the balance is the point. Find your middle ground, protect it, and happiness is more likely to stop and stay.
