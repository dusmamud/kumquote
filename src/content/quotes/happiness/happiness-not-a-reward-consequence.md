A consequence is different: it grows naturally out of the way we live, the way health follows good habits. Ingersoll's distinction matters, because it changes where we look for happiness. If it were a reward, we would wait for someone to give it to us. As a consequence, it is already within our reach, arising from kindness, honest work, and attention to the present. We cannot chase it directly, but we can live in the ways that produce it. Happiness, in this view, is less a destination than a result.
