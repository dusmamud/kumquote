Franklin points to a simple truth: happiness is shaped more by the mind than by circumstances. Two people can face the same day — the same weather, the same work, the same setbacks — and experience it entirely differently. One sees inconvenience everywhere; the other sees material for a decent day. Our inward disposition is the lens through which life arrives. We cannot always change what happens, but we can train how we meet it. A calm, grateful outlook does not erase hardship, yet it changes what hardship can do to us. Happiness begins within.
