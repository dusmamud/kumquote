Happiness begins in the mind before it appears anywhere else. Two people can live nearly identical lives, yet one calls himself happy and the other miserable, because happiness is partly a judgment we make about our own life. Publilius Syrus does not deny real hardship; he points out that our interpretation of events matters enormously. If we insist on seeing only what is missing, no amount of good fortune will satisfy us. Learning to recognize the good already present is the first step toward actually feeling happy.
