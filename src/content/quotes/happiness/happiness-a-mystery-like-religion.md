Chesterton warns against dissecting happiness until it disappears. We try to reduce it to formulas — habits, chemicals, circumstances — and in explaining it, we lose it. Like religion, happiness is best known by living inside it rather than analyzing it from outside. Mystery is not ignorance but respect for something larger than our explanations. We can still cultivate happiness, but we should not expect to master it. Some things are received, not solved, and happiness belongs among them. The happiest people are often those who stop demanding an explanation and simply let joy be what it is.
