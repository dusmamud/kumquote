Chesterton exposes the treadmill of wanting more. Accumulating endlessly never satisfies, because each new possession raises the bar for the next. The second way is quieter and far more powerful: want less. Desire is the real measure of enough, not wealth. When we train ourselves to appreciate what we have, scarcity disappears without anything being added. Contentment is not settling for less; it is recognising that less can be plenty. The richest person is not the one who owns most, but the one who needs least.
