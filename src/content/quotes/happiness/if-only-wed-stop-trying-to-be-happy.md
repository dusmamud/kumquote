Wharton turns the chase upside down. When happiness becomes a target, every ordinary moment gets measured against it, and most moments fall short. The harder we grip, the more it slips away, because joy rarely arrives on schedule or in the shape demanded. Her advice is not to give up on a good life but to stop policing it. Let a walk, a meal, or a conversation be enough as it is. A pretty good time often hides inside the very hours we dismiss while waiting for something better. Loosen the demand, and happiness can arrive unannounced.
