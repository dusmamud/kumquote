Like the violin, happiness improves with practice, not with wishing. Nobody picks up an instrument once and expects a concert; yet we often expect happiness to arrive fully formed without rehearsal. Lubbock's image tells us to train the skill daily: notice one good thing, speak kindly, take a walk, or sit quietly for five minutes. Small repetitions build a steadier mood the way scales build a musician's hands. Some days the practice feels clumsy, and that is fine. Mastery never comes from a single brilliant performance, but from showing up again and again, patiently and without applause.
