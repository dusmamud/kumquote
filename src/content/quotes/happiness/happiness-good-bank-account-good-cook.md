Rousseau gives happiness a deliberately plain recipe: enough money, good food, and a healthy body. The humor is in the modesty; he skips glory, fame, and grand philosophy for things that make an ordinary day pleasant. There is a serious point beneath the joke: happiness rests on simple foundations, and neglecting them in pursuit of higher aims leaves life uncomfortable. A sound body and a secure table free the mind for everything else. Sometimes wisdom sounds like common sense because it is.
