Camus wrote of a summer that no winter could defeat, a warmth carried inside even when the world turns cold. Hard seasons come to everyone: loss, failure, loneliness, and doubt. The quote does not deny those winters or pretend they are easy. It says that beneath them we can find something unbreakable — a capacity for joy, love, and renewal that survives the worst weather. That inner summer is not naive optimism; it is earned by enduring and still choosing to live fully. When we remember it, hardship becomes something we pass through, not a place we have to stay.
