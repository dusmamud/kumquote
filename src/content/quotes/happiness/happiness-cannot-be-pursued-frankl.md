Frankl warns that chasing happiness directly is a losing game, because happiness arrives as a side effect rather than a prize. It follows when we give ourselves to something larger than our own comfort, whether that is a cause, a craft, or another person. The moment we make happiness the target, it slips away, since a life measured only by how it feels becomes small and anxious. Instead, meaning comes first, and happiness ensues quietly, almost unnoticed, while we are busy doing work that matters. A purposeful life, then, is the surest road to the joy we seek.
