Bergman reduces happiness to two unlikely ingredients: a body that works and a mind that lets go. Good health is the quiet foundation beneath every pleasure; without it, even the finest things lose their colour. A bad memory is the surprising second half, not forgetfulness as a flaw but as a gift, the ability to release old slights, embarrassments, and failures instead of replaying them. People who carry every grudge stay heavy. Those who forget what should be forgotten travel light. Happiness, in this view, is less about adding joys and more about refusing to stockpile pains.
