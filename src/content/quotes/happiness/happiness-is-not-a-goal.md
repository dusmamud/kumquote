Chasing happiness directly often fails, because the harder we grasp at it, the further it seems to slip away. Roosevelt's observation explains why: happiness arrives as a side effect of a life well lived, not as a target we can aim at. It follows meaningful work, honest relationships, and service to others. But when happiness itself becomes the project, every ordinary day feels like a failure. The wiser approach is to tend the causes — purpose, connection, and growth — and let happiness come as their natural result, in its own time.
