J. Petit-Senn captures the paradox of chasing happiness: the harder we hunt for it directly, the more it slips away. We seek it in the promotion, the purchase, the perfect holiday — and arrive to find the feeling missing. Yet it ambushes us unannounced: in a quiet walk, an old song, a friend's unexpected laugh, an afternoon with nothing to prove. Happiness behaves less like a destination and more like a fellow traveller, noticed only when we stop scanning the horizon. Seek less, look around more, and it is usually already standing beside us.
