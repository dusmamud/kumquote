Jefferson separates happiness from riches and display. A quiet mind and useful work, he suggests, are worth more than a fortune, because they can be enjoyed every day without fear of loss. Wealth can be stolen and splendor fades, but a calm spirit and a task that matters belong to you. The lesson is practical: arrange your life so that your days have purpose and your evenings have peace. When work fills the hours and worry does not fill the mind, happiness has room to grow.
