Walters refuses to make happiness a hostage of circumstance. If happiness must wait for perfect health, perfect weather, or perfect people, it will never arrive, because outward life is never perfect. Determination changes the order: instead of waiting for conditions to improve, we decide first and let the feeling follow. This is not pretending that problems do not exist; it is refusing to let them set the terms of every day. A determined mind finds something to value even on hard days, and that small daily choice, repeated, becomes a habit of happiness.
