Russell noticed that wanting never ends: each desire satisfied makes room for another, and chasing them all leaves us exhausted. Happiness, he argues, requires a gap between what we want and what we have, and peace with that gap. Going without something teaches us that we can be whole without it. The person who needs less is freer, because fewer things can disturb their calm. This is not a call for poverty but for perspective — to enjoy what we have instead of suffering over what we lack.
