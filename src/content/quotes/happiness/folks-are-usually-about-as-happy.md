Lincoln's plain-spoken line places happiness squarely in the realm of decision. Circumstances matter, and hardship is real, but two people in the same situation often feel very differently. The difference is usually the mind's posture: one searches for reasons to complain, the other for reasons to be grateful. Deciding to be happy does not mean pretending problems do not exist; it means refusing to let them define everything. A settled intention to look for the good steadies us. Happiness grows when we treat it as a choice we renew each morning, not as a mood that arrives by luck.
