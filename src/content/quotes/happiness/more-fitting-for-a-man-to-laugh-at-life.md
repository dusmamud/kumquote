Seneca offers laughter as a philosophy, not just a mood. Life is full of absurdities, frustrations, and losses, and lamenting them endlessly only doubles their weight. Laughing at life does not mean denying its hardships; it means refusing to be crushed by them. Humour gives us distance, letting us see our troubles as smaller and stranger than they felt up close. A person who can laugh at misfortune keeps their freedom, because no event can dictate their inner state. Choosing laughter over lament is a quiet act of strength, a way of meeting the world with lightness instead of despair.
