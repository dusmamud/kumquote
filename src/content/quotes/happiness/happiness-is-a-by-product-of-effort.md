Happiness chased directly has a way of slipping through our fingers, because it was never meant to be a target. It arrives quietly, as a side effect of a life turned outward. When we make someone else's day a little lighter, we get purpose, warmth, and connection in return. A kind word, a shared meal, or an hour of real listening costs little and pays back generously. The paradox is simple: the more we try to hold happiness for ourselves alone, the less of it we keep. Give it away freely, and it comes back multiplied.
