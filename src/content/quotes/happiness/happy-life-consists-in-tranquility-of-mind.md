Cicero reduces happiness to something strikingly simple: a mind at peace. Not wealth, not fame, not endless excitement, but the quiet state of being untroubled within. A restless mind can turn even good fortune into worry, while a tranquil mind can find contentment in modest circumstances. Tranquility does not mean escaping life or avoiding all difficulty; it means meeting events without being pulled apart by fear, envy, or regret. We cultivate it by choosing what deserves our attention, letting go of what we cannot control, and keeping our conscience clear. When the mind settles, happiness has room to appear.
