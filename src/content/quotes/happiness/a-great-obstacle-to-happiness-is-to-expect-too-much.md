Expecting too much happiness is itself the barrier to it. When we demand constant delight, ordinary days feel like failures. Fontenelle suggests lowering the demand rather than chasing the feeling. A quiet evening, a small kindness, or a finished task can be enough if we stop measuring them against impossible standards. Happiness visits most often when we are not anxiously waiting for it. Moderate expectations make room for real contentment, which is steadier and truer than the intense happiness we imagine.
