Dostoevsky notices a habit of the mind: we keep a careful ledger of every worry and wound, yet let our joys pass uncounted. A single bad hour can fill the whole day in memory, while a dozen quiet kindnesses slip by unnoticed. The troubles are real, but the tally is rigged, because attention magnifies whatever it rests on. Counting our joys is not pretending life is easy; it is correcting the record. When we name the good things plainly, we discover that happiness was present all along, waiting to be noticed.
