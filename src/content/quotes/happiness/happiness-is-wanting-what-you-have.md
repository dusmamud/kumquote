Schachtel's insight reverses the usual formula. We spend our lives chasing what we want, believing happiness waits at the end of the chase. But wanting never ends — each satisfied desire is replaced by another. Real contentment comes from the opposite direction: learning to want what we already have. The home, the health, the people, the small routines of an ordinary day — these are riches we overlook while looking elsewhere. When we value what is present instead of longing for what is absent, happiness stops being a distant goal and becomes a daily companion.
