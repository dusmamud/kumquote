Marston insists that happiness is not something that happens to us; it is something we choose. Waiting for circumstances to make us happy hands our wellbeing to chance. A choice, by contrast, is always available: we can choose to notice the good, to act kindly, to meet the day with openness. Choosing happiness does not mean denying sadness or difficulty. It means refusing to let them have the final word. Every morning offers the same decision. Those who choose happiness again and again find that the choice slowly becomes a habit, and the habit becomes a life.
