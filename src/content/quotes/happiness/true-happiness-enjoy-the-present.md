Seneca names the habit that quietly steals happiness: anxious dependence on the future. We postpone enjoyment until conditions improve, yet the future never arrives in the form we imagined. True happiness, he argues, belongs to the present — the meal, the conversation, the walk we are already having. This is not carelessness about tomorrow but refusal to mortgage today for it. Enjoy what is here now, plan wisely, and let the future take care of itself when it comes. That is the freedom Seneca promises: a life enjoyed now, not deferred to a future that may never come.
