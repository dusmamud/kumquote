Happy moments are rarely long. Frost suggests the opposite: what happiness lacks in duration, it repays in depth. A single bright moment can lift a whole week, the way a tall tree changes a flat field. We do not need happiness to last forever for it to be real. Its intensity is the point. When we stop demanding that joy be permanent, we can appreciate it fully while it is here, and carry its height with us long after it has gone.
