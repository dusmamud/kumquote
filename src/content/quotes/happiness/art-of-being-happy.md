Happiness is often imagined as something rare and grand, reserved for special occasions. Beecher calls it an art, a skill anyone can practice: drawing joy from common things. A warm cup of tea, sunlight through a window, a familiar song — these small pleasures are available every day, yet we rush past them chasing something bigger. The art lies in slowing down enough to notice. People who master this skill are never bored and rarely empty, because their happiness is made from materials that are always at hand.
