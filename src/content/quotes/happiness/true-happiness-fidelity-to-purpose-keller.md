Keller, who knew hardship intimately, argues that pleasure alone cannot build lasting happiness. Self-gratification fades quickly and leaves us hungry again, always needing a larger dose. Fidelity to a worthy purpose works differently: it gives each day meaning, turning effort into something that outlasts the moment. Loyalty to a cause, a craft, or a person anchors us when moods change and troubles come. Happiness then grows from the knowledge that our life counts for something beyond our own comfort. It is the deep satisfaction of being faithful to what matters most.
