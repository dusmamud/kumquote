Oppenheim draws a sharp line between chasing happiness and growing it. The foolish person believes joy waits somewhere else — a new job, a new city, a new possession — and spends life running toward a horizon that keeps moving. The wise person tends the ground already beneath them: small pleasures, honest work, the people close by. Happiness grown at home is sturdier than happiness hunted far away, because it depends on attention rather than luck. Look down, not far off.
