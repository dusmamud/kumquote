Franklin reminds us that happiness is mostly assembled from small, repeated pleasures rather than rare windfalls. A quiet morning coffee, a friendly exchange, a walk in fresh air, these everyday conveniences quietly add up to a life that feels good. We tend to chase the big moments and overlook the ordinary ones, yet the big moments are few and far between. When we learn to notice and value the little comforts already around us, happiness stops depending on luck and becomes something we can practise daily, in the simplest of ways.
