We are often told that happiness hides in excitement, novelty, and constant change, yet Pritchett suggests the opposite. A steady, pleasant rhythm — work we enjoy, people we love, small rituals that repeat — can bring a deeper and longer-lasting contentment than any adventure. Monotony sounds dull, but when the routine itself is congenial, it becomes a source of calm rather than boredom. The point is not to settle for a grey life, but to build one whose ordinary days are genuinely good. Happiness, then, is less about escaping routine and more about choosing the right one.
