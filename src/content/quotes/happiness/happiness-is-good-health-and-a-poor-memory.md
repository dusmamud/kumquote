Schweitzer reduces happiness to two plain blessings, and the joke carries a serious point. Good health lets us enjoy the day instead of fighting our own body, and a poor memory, in his sense, means refusing to carry every slight and failure forward forever. We often replay old embarrassments and grudges until they colour the present, but happiness needs room to breathe. Forgetting what should be forgotten is not carelessness; it is a skill. A sound body and a light hold on the past leave space for joy.
