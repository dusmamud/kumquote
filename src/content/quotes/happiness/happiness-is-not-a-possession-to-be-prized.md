We often chase happiness as if it were an object to be bought, won, or stored away for later. Yet no possession can hold it, because happiness lives in how we interpret what we already have. Two people can share the same day and experience it entirely differently, one finding delight in small things, the other finding only faults. The difference is not circumstance but attention. When we train our thoughts toward gratitude and curiosity, ordinary moments begin to glow. Happiness, then, is less a prize than a skill of seeing, practiced daily.
