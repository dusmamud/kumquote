Hawn's words point to self-acceptance as the foundation of happiness. Many of us wait to feel happy until we are thinner, richer, more successful, or more admired. But happiness tied to conditions is always postponed. Being happy with who you are does not mean giving up growth; it means growing from acceptance rather than from self-rejection. When we stop fighting ourselves, a surprising lightness appears. The person we are — flaws included — is enough to build a good life on. Happiness starts with making peace with that person.
