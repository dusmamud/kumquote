Direct pursuit of happiness often fails because it turns attention inward, where every shortcoming looms large. Palmer observes that happiness arrives as a by-product — something gained while aiming at something else. When we try to make someone else happy, we step outside our own worries, and the warmth of that connection returns to us. Generosity creates the very feeling we were chasing. The surest route to a happier life is often to forget ourselves for a while and serve another person well.
