Bentham turns happiness from a private pursuit into a public measure. If the aim of laws and customs is the well-being of the most people, then every rule can be judged by a simple question: does it make life better for ordinary people, or only for a few? Happiness here is not a mood but a serious moral goal. A society that takes it seriously must care about health, fairness, and freedom, not just wealth or power. The quote reminds us that our own happiness is bound up with the happiness of everyone around us.
