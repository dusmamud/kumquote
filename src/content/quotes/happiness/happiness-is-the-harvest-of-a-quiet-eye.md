A quiet eye notices what a restless one rushes past: the light on a wall, a kind word, the simple fact of being alive. Happiness often hides in such small observations, waiting for someone patient enough to see them. When the mind is noisy with comparison and craving, even abundance feels empty, because nothing is truly noticed. Stillness changes the harvest. It lets us gather joy from ordinary moments instead of demanding grand ones. To cultivate a quiet eye is to practice looking slowly, listening fully, and letting enough be enough. Contentment, in this sense, is not passivity but attention.
