Cousteau finds happiness in something simpler than achievement: existence itself, seen clearly. A bee or a dolphin simply lives, and that is enough for it. We are given the extra gift of knowing we exist, and of wondering at the sea, the sky, and the strange fact of being alive at all. That wonder turns ordinary moments into something rich. When we stop treating life as a problem to be solved and start noticing it as a marvel to behold, happiness stops being a distant reward and becomes part of the day itself.
