Seneca says happiness is not a prize for becoming someone else; it is the result of living in agreement with your own nature. A fish is not unhappy for failing to climb trees, yet people spend years chasing lives that do not fit them, measuring themselves against strangers, and wondering why success feels hollow. Nature here means your real temperament, values, and limits, not your ambitions borrowed from others. When your daily life matches who you actually are, effort feels meaningful instead of exhausting, and contentment follows without needing applause.
