Aristotle pictures happiness as a soul coming to rest in the place where it belongs — like water finding its level, or a seed finding good soil. Much of our restlessness comes from living out of place: chasing roles that do not suit us, imitating lives that are not ours, measuring ourselves against strangers. When we find work, relationships, and rhythms that fit our nature, the striving quiets and contentment settles in. Happiness is not a prize to be grabbed, but a state of being rightly placed. Find where you belong, and the soul knows how to rest.
