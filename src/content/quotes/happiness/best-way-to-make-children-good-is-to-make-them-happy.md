Wilde turns the usual logic of raising children upside down. We often assume goodness must be drilled in through rules and correction, and that happiness will follow as a reward. He suggests the opposite: a happy child naturally becomes a good one. Joy softens the heart, builds trust, and gives a child the security to be generous and kind. A child who feels loved and delighted by life has little reason for cruelty or defiance. Happiness is not a distraction from character; it is one of its strongest foundations.
