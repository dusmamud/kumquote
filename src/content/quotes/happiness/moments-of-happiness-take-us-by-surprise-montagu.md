Montagu observes that our happiest moments rarely arrive on schedule. We plan grand occasions and chase peak experiences, yet joy often ambushes us in unguarded instants: a child's laugh, a sudden sunset, an old song on the radio. It cannot be seized by force, because the very act of grasping turns a living moment into a project. Happiness seizes us instead, arriving when our guard is down and our attention is free. The lesson is gentle: stay open, stay present, and let gladness find you, rather than hunting it down with plans and checklists that leave no room for surprise.
