Anthony points to a quiet paradox: misery can feel safer than happiness because it is familiar and predictable. Hope requires risk, since reaching for joy means opening ourselves to disappointment, while staying unhappy asks nothing new of us. Many people therefore cling to known sorrows rather than gamble on an uncertain gladness, choosing the comfort of certainty over the adventure of change. Yet this bargain costs dearly, trading a living hope for a dead security. Real happiness asks for courage, the willingness to risk feeling foolish, hurt, or let down, in exchange for a life that is genuinely alive.
