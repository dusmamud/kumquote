Zeno, the founder of Stoicism, defines happiness as a good flow of life — not a peak of excitement, but a steady, smooth current. A good flow means living in harmony with reason and nature, meeting events without violent resistance. When we fight every current, life feels exhausting. When we move with it — accepting what we cannot change and acting wisely where we can — the days pass with less friction. Happiness is not a prize at the end of the river. It is the quality of the flow itself, calm and unforced, carrying us forward.
