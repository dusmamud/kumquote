Hawthorne captures the strange way happiness behaves: chase it and it flees; sit still and it may land on you. Pursuit makes happiness a target, and targets move. We grow tense, watchful, and disappointed, which is the opposite of the openness happiness needs. Sitting quietly is not giving up but changing posture — becoming present, attentive, and at ease. In that stillness, small joys we overlooked become visible. Happiness often arrives unannounced, like a butterfly, to those who have stopped running after it.
