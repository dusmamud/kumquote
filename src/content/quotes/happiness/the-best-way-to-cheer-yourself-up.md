When we feel low, our attention usually turns inward, circling our own troubles until they grow larger. Twain offers a surprising escape: turn outward instead. Helping someone else lifts the giver as much as the receiver, because kindness shifts our focus from what is wrong to what we can still offer. A small act — a kind word, a favour, a moment of listening — reminds us that we are useful and connected. Our own worries do not vanish, but they shrink beside the warmth of having helped.
