Chekhov notices how happiness changes our attention. When we are content, the weather, the season, and the small discomforts of the day fade into the background; when we are miserable, every detail becomes evidence for our unhappiness. The seasons do not change, but our way of meeting them does. This is why two people can live through the same winter and describe it completely differently. Happiness is less about arranging perfect conditions than about a state of mind in which ordinary conditions stop mattering so much.
