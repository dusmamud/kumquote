Kant draws a sharp line between what reason can plan and what happiness actually is. Reason can calculate, compare, and organize, but it cannot manufacture the feeling of a life well lived. Happiness belongs to imagination because it is shaped by how we picture our lives, what we notice, and what we allow ourselves to want. Two people with identical circumstances can feel entirely differently. The lesson is practical: stop trying to reason your way into happiness with a better plan, and start feeding your imagination with gratitude, wonder, and small pleasures.
