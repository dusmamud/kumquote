Benjamin suggests that happiness begins with the courage to look at ourselves honestly — our flaws, our fears, our contradictions — without flinching. Most of us keep busy precisely to avoid that meeting, and the avoidance itself becomes a source of unease. When we can sit with who we truly are and not be frightened, a quiet peace follows, because the inner critic loses its power. Self-knowledge without fear is the foundation on which lasting contentment is built. Meet yourself honestly, and happiness will have a place to grow.
