Epictetus notices a paradox of appetite: the things we chase daily lose their shine, while rare pleasures glow. When something is always available, we stop noticing it, and familiarity quietly turns delight into background noise. Scarcity, on the other hand, sharpens attention and makes a single moment feel vivid. The wisdom here is not to hoard pleasures but to space them, to let absence do its work. A meal tastes better after a long walk, rest feels sweeter after honest work, and a reunion means more after time apart. Rarity is not deprivation; it is seasoning.
