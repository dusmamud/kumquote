Searching for a definition of happiness can become a trap. The more we analyse what happiness should feel like, the less we actually live. Life happens in the present moment — in a conversation, a meal, a walk, a task done well. Camus warns that treating happiness as a puzzle to solve keeps us standing outside our own lives, watching instead of participating. Meaning is not found by staring at it. It appears quietly while we are engaged in living. Stop hunting for the perfect feeling, and the feeling often arrives on its own.
