Jerrold warns against searching for happiness in other people's lives. The gardens of strangers always look greener, but they are not ours to tend. Happiness grows at home, in the small daily life we actually live: family, work, routine, and quiet moments. Envy distracts us from cultivating our own ground. The quote invites us to stop comparing and start tending what is already planted around us. A modest life, cared for with attention, yields more happiness than a grand one admired from a distance.
