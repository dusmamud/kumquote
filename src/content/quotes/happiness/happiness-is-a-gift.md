Expecting happiness on a schedule only breeds disappointment, because joy rarely arrives when ordered. Dickens treats happiness as a gift rather than a right: unexpected, unearned, and all the sweeter for it. When we stop demanding that life make us happy, we become free to notice the small delights already arriving unannounced. The trick is delight, not demand. A good morning, a kind word, a quiet evening — these are gifts, not guarantees. Receiving them with open hands turns ordinary days into something quietly wonderful and worth remembering.
