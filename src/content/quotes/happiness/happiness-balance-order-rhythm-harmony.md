We often imagine happiness as intensity — peak moments, excitement, ecstasy — and then wonder why ordinary life feels flat. Merton offers a quieter definition: balance, order, rhythm, and harmony. A happy life is not the loudest life but the most coherent one, where work and rest, giving and receiving, effort and stillness keep time with each other. Intensity burns out; rhythm sustains. When our days have a humane order, happiness stops being a rare event and becomes the steady background of a well-lived life.
