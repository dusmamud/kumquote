Addison gives happiness a practical shape: three essentials rather than a mystery. Something to do gives our days purpose and keeps our hands and minds engaged. Something to love gives our hearts a home and our efforts a reason beyond ourselves. Something to hope for gives the future light, so that even hard days carry meaning. When one of the three is missing, life feels flat; when all three are present, even modest circumstances can feel rich. Happiness, in this view, is not luck or wealth, but a life built around work, love, and hope.
