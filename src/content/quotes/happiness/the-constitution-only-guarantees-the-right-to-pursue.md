Franklin cuts through a comforting illusion: no law, no government, and no circumstance can hand us happiness ready-made. What society can promise is only the freedom to chase it, and the rest is personal work. That sounds stern, but it is also liberating — happiness is not something withheld by the world, but something we are free to build for ourselves. The pursuit itself, with its choices and its effort, is where the meaning lives. Nobody else can catch it for you.
