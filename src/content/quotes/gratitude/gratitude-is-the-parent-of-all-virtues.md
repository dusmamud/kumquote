Cicero gives gratitude the highest place among virtues — not only the greatest, but the parent from which the others grow. Think about it: a grateful person is more likely to be kind, generous, patient, and honest, because gratitude reminds us that we have received more than we deserve. It softens pride, quiets envy, and opens the heart to others. Without gratitude, virtues become duties performed coldly; with it, they flow naturally. That is why Cicero calls it a parent: gratitude does not stand beside the other virtues, it gives birth to them.
