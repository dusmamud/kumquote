Hubbard chooses the capacity to appreciate over the capacity to own, and the trade is wiser than it looks. Owning what we cannot appreciate is a kind of poverty: the shelves are full and the heart is empty. Appreciating what we cannot have is a kind of wealth: the world is full of beauty that costs nothing to enjoy. The first person guards possessions they do not love; the second person loves freely and never runs out. Wealth, Hubbard suggests, is not in the hands but in the eyes.
