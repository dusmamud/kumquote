Chesterton elevates gratitude above all other thinking, and then adds wonder to it. To be grateful is not merely to feel pleased; it is to recognise that the good things in life are gifts, not entitlements — and that recognition is itself a form of wisdom. When gratitude is doubled by wonder, even ordinary things become extraordinary: a morning, a meal, a friend's voice. Happiness, in this view, is not the result of having more but of seeing clearly what we already have. The highest thought, Chesterton says, is simply this: thank you, and how wonderful it is.
