Kennedy reminds us that gratitude is not only a feeling but a duty, and one that requires stopping. In busy lives, the people who help us most are often thanked last, because we assume there will be time later. But the difference they make deserves acknowledgement now, while it can still be heard. Finding time to stop is itself part of the thanks: it shows that the person matters more than the rush. A sincere thank you costs nothing and means a great deal; it tells someone their effort was seen and valued.
