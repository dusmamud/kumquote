Counting blessings is a good habit, but Maxwell pushes us one step further. Gratitude should not stop at a list of good things; it should change how we live. Our health, our friendships, our talents, and our opportunities are blessings, and they become meaningful when we use them well — helping someone, creating something, or giving back. A blessing kept to ourselves is only half enjoyed. The real test of gratitude is action: turning what we have received into something that blesses others. Count your blessings, yes — and then put them to work. That is gratitude made complete.
