Gratitude is so rarely felt in public life that it can seem strange, almost like a physical surprise, when it finally appears. Disraeli's joke works because it catches the embarrassment many of us feel about saying thank you plainly: we are used to irony, complaint, and cool detachment, while honest thanks feels unfamiliar and even a little awkward. But that awkwardness is worth pushing through. Naming gratitude out loud is far better than keeping it private. A sincere thank you, however odd it feels, is one of the few things that makes both giver and receiver feel better.
