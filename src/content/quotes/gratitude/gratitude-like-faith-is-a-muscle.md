Gratitude is often treated as a feeling that either appears or not, but Cohen describes it as a muscle that strengthens with use. Like any muscle, it grows weak when ignored and stronger when exercised daily. Each small act of thanks, whether spoken aloud or noticed, is one more repetition. Over time, the practice changes how we see the world: good things become easier to spot, and hard times feel less overwhelming. This idea turns gratitude from a mood into a skill, something we can train, and it promises that the effort itself reshapes the way we experience life.
