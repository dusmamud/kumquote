Gratitude does not grow on its own like a weed. It is a fruit, which means it needs planting, watering and patience before it appears. A coarse or careless character rarely produces it, not because such people are incapable, but because they have never cultivated the habit of noticing what they have received. The line treats thankfulness as a skill of attention that can be trained. The more we practise noticing gifts, the more naturally gratitude grows. Left untended, it withers; tended daily, it ripens into character.
