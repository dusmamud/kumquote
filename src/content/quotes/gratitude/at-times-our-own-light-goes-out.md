Schweitzer speaks of the times when our own energy fails — when enthusiasm dies, hope dims, and we cannot relight ourselves. In those moments, another person's kindness, encouragement, or example can rekindle the flame, often without them knowing it. We owe a deep debt to those people: the teacher who believed in us, the friend who listened, the stranger whose words arrived at the right hour. Gratitude here is not a polite formality but a recognition of rescue. Each of us has been relit by someone, and remembering who they were keeps us humble.
