Faulkner compares gratitude to electricity, and the comparison is exact: it only exists when it flows. A battery kept in a drawer powers nothing, and gratitude kept inside the heart changes nothing. It must be produced through attention, discharged through words and actions, and used up in daily life. Thank the colleague, call the friend, or write the note, and the current moves. Keep it stored and it fades into nothing. Gratitude is not a feeling to hoard but a power to spend, and the more we spend it, the more of it seems to flow.
