Thoreau practised gratitude as a daily discipline rather than a passing feeling. His thanksgiving was perpetual because he trained himself to notice what he already had: health, shelter, friendship, the changing seasons. Most unhappiness comes from comparing our lives with other people's and finding ours lacking. Gratitude reverses that habit by turning attention toward what is present instead of what is missing. The quote is not a call to settle for less, but a reminder that contentment begins with recognition. A grateful mind finds enough in the ordinary, and that enough is the foundation of a peaceful life.
