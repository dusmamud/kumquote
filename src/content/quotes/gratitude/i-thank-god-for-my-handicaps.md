Helen Keller, who lost sight and hearing as a small child, turns gratitude upside down: she is thankful not in spite of her handicaps but because of them. Through hardship she found herself, her life's work, and her faith — things a comfortable life might never have forced her to seek. It is the most radical form of gratitude, one that refuses to divide life into blessings and curses, and finds meaning in both. If Keller could be grateful for so much, the rest of us have little excuse.
