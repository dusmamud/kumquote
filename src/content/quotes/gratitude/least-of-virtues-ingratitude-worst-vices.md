Fuller makes a striking asymmetry: gratitude is the least of the virtues — modest, ordinary, easily practiced — yet ingratitude is the worst of vices. The point is that failing at something so small reveals something large: ingratitude is not a minor lapse but a corruption of character, the refusal to acknowledge what we owe others. A person can lack grand virtues and still be decent, but ingratitude poisons relationships at the root. The quote is a warning and an encouragement both: gratitude costs little, and its absence costs everything.
