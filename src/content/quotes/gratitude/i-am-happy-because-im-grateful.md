We often assume happiness comes first and gratitude follows, but Nelson turns that order around. Happiness grows out of the decision to notice what is good. Gratitude is not a feeling that visits by luck; it is a choice we can make each morning, whatever our circumstances. When we choose to be grateful, small things begin to shine: a meal, a friend, a quiet hour. That choice does not erase hardship, but it changes what we see, and what we see shapes how happy we feel. Joy, then, is less a gift of fortune than a habit of attention.
