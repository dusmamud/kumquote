Lindbergh observes that gratitude cannot be repaid directly — no thank-you ever equals the gift. But that is not a failure; it is how generosity works. We repay kindness not by settling the account but by passing it on, doing good for someone else, somewhere else in life. This turns gratitude into a chain rather than a transaction: each gift inspires another, and the original giver is repaid by a kinder world. The impossibility of exact repayment is what keeps generosity moving, from person to person, without end.
