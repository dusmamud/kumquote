Bonhoeffer, writing from prison, noticed a truth that comfortable life hides: we receive far more than we give. Our days rest on the work of countless others — farmers, teachers, builders, friends — and on gifts we did nothing to earn. Gratitude is the act of seeing it: of recognising how much has been given to us, and how little of it we repay. Life cannot be without gratitude, Bonhoeffer says, because a life that never notices its gifts is a life lived in blindness.
