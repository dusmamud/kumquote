Epictetus defines wisdom as a shift of attention: stop grieving for what you lack, and start rejoicing in what you have. The first habit is endless — there will always be something missing, someone richer, some goal unreached — and it guarantees a life of quiet dissatisfaction. The second habit is available right now, because everyone has something worth rejoicing in, however small. This is not a call to give up ambition, but to stop letting absence define the day. The wise person measures life by what is present, and finds that the present is already full.
