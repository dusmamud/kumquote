Oatman turns gratitude into a practical exercise: count, name, look. Blessings stay vague until we name them one by one, and naming them forces us to notice what we usually walk past. The second half of the line asks us to see what has been done for us, not merely what we have achieved ourselves. Counting is not about keeping score but about paying attention. When we are discouraged or overwhelmed, the list itself becomes evidence: a life that looked empty from a distance turns out to be full of small, specific gifts.
