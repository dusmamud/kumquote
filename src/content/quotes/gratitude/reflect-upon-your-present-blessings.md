Dickens urges us to reflect on present blessings, which every person has in abundance, rather than past misfortunes, which all people share. Misfortune is universal, but so is blessing, and attention decides which one shapes our mood. Dwelling on old injuries keeps them alive; counting present gifts brings them into focus. The quote is practical advice for the mind. You cannot change the past, but you can choose what to reflect on now. Choose the blessings. The past cannot be rewritten, but the present can be counted, and counting it is a choice.
