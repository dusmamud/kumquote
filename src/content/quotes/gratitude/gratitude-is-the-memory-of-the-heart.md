Gratitude is often described as a feeling, but Massillon calls it the memory of the heart, which suggests something deeper and more durable. The mind forgets kindness quickly, distracted by the next demand, but the heart keeps a record of who helped, who listened, and who stayed. When gratitude lives in memory, it shapes how we treat people long after the favour is done. This line reminds us that being thankful is not a polite reflex in the moment; it is a way of remembering well, of carrying other people's goodness with us and letting it guide our own actions.
