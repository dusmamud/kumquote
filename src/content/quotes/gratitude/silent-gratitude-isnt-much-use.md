G.B. Stern points out a quiet failure: gratitude that is felt but never expressed helps no one. The person we are grateful to cannot know what we feel unless we say it, and our own gratitude stays small when it is never spoken aloud. A thank you, spoken plainly, does real work — it encourages the giver, strengthens the relationship, and turns a private feeling into a shared good. We often assume people know we are grateful, but assumptions are a poor substitute for words.
