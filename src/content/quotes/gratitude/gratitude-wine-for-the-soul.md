Rumi celebrates gratitude as intoxication of the best kind — a warmth that loosens the heart and fills it with joy. Wine, in his poetry, stands for spiritual ecstasy, and gratitude is its truest form: drinking deeply of the gifts already present. The playful command "Go on. Get drunk" invites excess, not moderation — let gratitude overwhelm you, let it color everything. This is gratitude as celebration rather than duty, a joy that spills over. To be drunk on gratitude is to see the world gilded, and to carry that glow into every encounter.
