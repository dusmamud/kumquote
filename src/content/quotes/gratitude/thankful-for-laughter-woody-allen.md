Woody Allen turns thankfulness into a joke, and the joke into a small truth. Laughter is one of life's great gifts, worth being thankful for, yet even the best things come with undignified side effects. The humor works because gratitude need not be solemn; it can be light, self-mocking, and fully aware that joy is often messy. Being thankful for laughter, milk and all, means accepting happiness as it actually arrives, not as a polished ideal. The line reminds us that gratitude and humor belong together, since both begin with noticing the good in an imperfect, very human moment.
