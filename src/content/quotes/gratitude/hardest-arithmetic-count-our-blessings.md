Hoffer jokes that counting blessings is the hardest arithmetic — harder than any mathematics — because the mind resists it. We count troubles easily and automatically, but blessings require deliberate effort, and they seem to slip from the total. Mastering this arithmetic means learning to notice what usually goes uncounted: health, friendship, shelter, a quiet morning. The difficulty is not in the counting but in the seeing. Once learned, however, this skill changes every sum: a life that seemed poor turns out to be rich, counted correctly.
