Seneca places gratitude among the highest of human qualities, above wealth, power, and cleverness. A grateful heart is honourable because it remembers who helped, acknowledges what was given, and refuses to take kindness for granted. Ingratitude, by contrast, cheapens every gift and every relationship. The person who says thank you sincerely — and means it — shows character that no achievement can match. Gratitude costs nothing. In a world that celebrates getting more, Seneca reminds us that the noblest thing we can do is simply to be thankful for what we have.
