Kant judges ingratitude more harshly than most faults because it breaks something basic in us. To receive kindness and give nothing back, not even thanks, is to treat other people as tools and their generosity as a debt owed to us. Gratitude, in his view, is not politeness but a moral duty: it recognises that we depend on each other and honours that dependence. Ingratitude denies the bond entirely. A person can be flawed in many ways and still be decent, but Kant suggests that ingratitude reveals a deeper failure of character.
