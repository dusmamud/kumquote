Kennedy warns that gratitude can become cheap if it stays in words. Saying thank you is good, but the highest appreciation is lived: it shows up in what we do, and whether our actions honour the gifts and sacrifices behind them. A soldier's sacrifice is honoured not by a speech but by a life of service; a teacher's gift is repaid not by praise but by learning well. Words are easy and forgettable. Living by our gratitude — letting it change what we do — is harder, and it is the only thanks that truly counts.
