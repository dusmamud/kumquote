Cicero calls gratitude a parent, not just one virtue among many —
because the other virtues grow out of it. A grateful person notices
what was given, and noticing is the start of honesty, generosity,
patience, and humility. An ungrateful person, by contrast, assumes
everything was owed, and from that assumption grow entitlement,
resentment, and complaint.

The daily practice is almost embarrassingly small: name three
things you received today that you did not earn alone — a meal, a
message, a moment of quiet — and say who they came from. Done
every evening, this does not just make you feel better for a
minute. It retrains what you notice, and what you notice decides
what kind of person you are becoming.
