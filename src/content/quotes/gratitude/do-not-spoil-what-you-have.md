Epicurus warns against a common self-inflicted wound: ruining present happiness by longing for what we lack. The things we now enjoy — a home, a friend, a skill, a quiet evening — were once only hopes, and we rejoiced when they arrived. Yet desire moves the goalpost, and we begin to spoil what we have by resenting what we have not. The cure is memory: remember how much you once wanted what you now possess, and the old joy returns. Gratitude, for Epicurus, is the discipline of noticing that the present already contains yesterday's answered prayers.
