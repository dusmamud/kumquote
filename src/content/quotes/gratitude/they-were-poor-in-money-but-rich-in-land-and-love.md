Alcott writes of a family poor in money but rich in land and love. The line, from her Thanksgiving story, reframes wealth: what matters is not the bank balance but the ground beneath your feet and the people around your table. Poverty of money is real, but it does not have to mean poverty of life. The quote is a gentle lesson in gratitude. Measure your riches by love and belonging, and many poor households turn out to be rich indeed.
