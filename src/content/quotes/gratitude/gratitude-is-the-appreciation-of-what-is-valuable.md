Emmons, a psychologist who studied gratitude scientifically, defines it as appreciating what is valuable and meaningful to oneself. Gratitude is not vague optimism; it is attention directed at the good that is already present. It notices the value in people, health, work, and ordinary moments, and names it as a gift. The definition makes gratitude a practice, not a mood. Look for what is valuable, recognise it clearly, and let appreciation follow. That is the whole discipline. Name one valuable thing each day, and the practice will quietly remake how you see your life.
