Contentment has two parts, and Lin Yutang names them both. The first is enjoying what we already have, which asks for attention rather than effort: tasting the meal, valuing the friend, noticing the home. The second is harder, letting go of desire for what lies beyond our reach, because endless wanting poisons even good fortune. Together they form a quiet kind of freedom. When we stop measuring our lives against what we do not have, what we do have finally becomes enough. Gratitude is the practice that makes both parts possible, turning ordinary possessions into real and lasting satisfaction.
