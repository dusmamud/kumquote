Rousseau is honest about a hard truth: gratitude cannot be demanded, only given freely. We can hope for thanks, but the moment we treat it as a debt owed to us, we poison the gift. Real gratitude is voluntary, arising from a genuine recognition of kindness received. That is why it is both a duty and not a right: we owe it when we have been helped, but no one can collect it from us by force. Give freely, expect little, and when gratitude arrives on its own, it will be sincere, warm, and worth far more than any payment.
