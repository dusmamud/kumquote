Albert Einstein, a man whose name stands for individual genius, insists that his life rests on the labors of others — living and dead. Nothing we enjoy is truly self-made: the food, the roads, the ideas, the language we think in all came from hands we will never know. Einstein reminds himself of this a hundred times a day, not once, because gratitude fades so easily into entitlement. His conclusion is what makes the quote complete: gratitude is not only a feeling but a debt, and the honest response is to give back in the same measure as we have received.
