James Allen elevates thanks from politeness to duty — and the most urgent one. We treat gratitude as optional, something to offer when convenient, but Allen insists it comes first. Returning thanks acknowledges that we did not arrive alone: teachers, friends, strangers, and circumstances all contributed. Delaying thanks risks forgetting the debt entirely, and forgotten debts harden into entitlement. The urgency is practical as well as moral: thank people while the gift is fresh, while they can hear it, and while it still matters to say it.
