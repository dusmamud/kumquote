This stark line cuts through the most common excuse in human life: later. We assume there will be time tomorrow to say what matters, to begin the work, to make amends — and that assumption quietly steals the present. The Buddha's warning is not meant to frighten but to wake us up. Time is not guaranteed, and the things we postpone may never get their moment. If we truly accepted that today might be all we have, we would speak more kindly, start sooner, and stop waiting for perfect conditions.
