Groucho Marx takes a solemn proverb about fate and punctures it with a punchline. "No man goes before his time" sounds like destiny, but the boss leaving early turns it into office comedy — the only escape from work is the boss's early exit. The joke works because it catches two truths at once: we cannot control our time, and we spend much of it in absurd situations. Humor like this lightens a heavy subject, reminding us that even our most serious ideas about time deserve a laugh now and then.
