Tracy states the uncomfortable math of life: there will never be enough time for everything. The list grows faster than the hours, and anyone waiting for a clear schedule will wait forever. But the second half of the sentence rescues us: there is always enough time for the most important thing. The problem is rarely the clock; it is our failure to decide what matters most. Choose the one thing that moves life forward, protect an hour for it, and let the rest wait. Time obeys priorities, not wishes, so set the priority first and the hours will follow.
