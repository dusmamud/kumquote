Seneca challenges the common complaint that life is too short. We have enough time, he argues — we simply squander it on distractions, grudges, and the pursuit of things that do not matter. A life feels short only in hindsight, when we realise how much of it slipped away unnoticed. The remedy is not to live longer but to live more deliberately: guard your hours as you would guard money, spend them on what you truly value, and refuse to let others waste them for you. Life is long enough, if only we stop throwing it away.
