Lao Tzu looks at the natural world and sees a different relationship with time. Trees do not rush to grow, rivers do not hurry to the sea, and seasons arrive exactly when they should — yet everything is accomplished in the end. The quote invites us to trust a slower rhythm: to work steadily, to allow things their proper time, and to remember that rest is part of growth. Unhurried does not mean idle. It means moving at the pace of things done well.
