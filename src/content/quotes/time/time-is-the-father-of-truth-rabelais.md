Rabelais teaches that truth needs time to reveal itself, like a father watching over a slow birth. In the heat of the moment, lies can look convincing and rumors can pass for fact, because we judge too quickly and with too little evidence. But time tests every claim: falsehoods fade, contradictions surface, and what is real endures the passing of days and years. When we let time do its work, the truth emerges on its own, and no argument can hurry what only the years can prove.
