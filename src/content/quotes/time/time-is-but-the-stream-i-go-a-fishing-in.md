Thoreau fishes in the stream of time and finds it shallow. He drinks from it, yet sees the sandy bottom: our days are thin and brief compared with eternity. The image captures both the poverty and the wonder of time. It slips away even as we use it, and no effort can make it deeper. But eternity remains beyond the stream, vast and still. The quote invites us to drink deeply anyway, to notice the stars reflected in the water, and to live as if each shallow moment mattered.
