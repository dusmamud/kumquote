Boucicault catches the joke we play on ourselves. We speak of killing time as if time were the victim and we the victor, but the exchange runs the other way: every hour we kill is an hour of our own life, gone quietly and without ceremony. Time does not fight back or complain; it simply passes, and one day we notice how much of it we spent on nothing. The phrase is a warning disguised as wit. Spend time as if it were your own life, because that is exactly what it is.
