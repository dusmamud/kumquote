Most of us feel short of time, yet our days often leak away in small distractions, delays, and tasks that do not matter. Seneca points out that the real problem is not the length of life but how carelessly we spend it. A day can feel empty not because it was short, but because it was scattered. When we guard our hours the way we guard our money, time suddenly feels more generous. This thought invites us to look honestly at where our days go, cut what adds nothing, and treat each hour as something valuable that deserves respect.
