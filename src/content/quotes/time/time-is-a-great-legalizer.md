Mencken observes that time quietly overturns judgments we once thought permanent. Ideas condemned as scandalous become common sense, heroes become villains, and villains are forgiven, all without anyone passing a new law. Time legalizes by wearing down outrage and letting us see an old act with fresh eyes. This cuts both ways: it can excuse real wrongs, but it can also free us from rules that were never just. The lesson is humility about our own certainties, since time will probably revise them too.
