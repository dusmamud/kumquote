Delay feels harmless in the moment — a task can wait, a conversation can happen later, a decision can be made tomorrow. But time does not wait with us. It moves forward at the same steady pace whether we act or not, and the hours we postpone are gone for good. Franklin's warning is short because the truth is simple: procrastination costs the one resource we cannot earn back. The antidote is not panic but promptness — doing now what matters now, while the time to do it still belongs to us.
