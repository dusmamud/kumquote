Herrick urges us to gather rosebuds while we may, because old time is still flying. Youth, beauty, and opportunity are brief, and delay wastes them. The poem's advice is not reckless indulgence but timely action: do the good and joyful things while the season allows. Waiting for a perfect moment means watching the flowers fade ungathered. Time moves whether we act or not. The wise person picks the roses now, while they are in bloom. The roses will not wait for permission, and neither will the years; gather them while the season is yours.
