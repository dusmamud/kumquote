Thoreau pictures time as a stream, and himself as someone fishing in it — patient, unhurried, and content to wait. The image is gentle: time is not an enemy chasing us, but a natural flow in which we live, and its gifts come to those who are present and attentive. Fishing requires stillness and watching, not rushing, and Thoreau suggests that the best of life is caught in the same way. We do not need to conquer time or race against it; we need to sit beside it and pay attention.
