Borges reduces a lifetime of philosophy to a single line: we are not beings who merely live in time, we are made of it. Our memories, our hopes, our very selves are woven from passing moments, and there is no part of us that stands outside time's flow. That sounds melancholy, but it is also a kind of wonder — to be made of time is to be part of something vast and moving. We do not have time; in the deepest sense, we are time, briefly given a name.
