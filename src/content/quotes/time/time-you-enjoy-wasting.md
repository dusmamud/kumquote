We are often told to use every minute well, as if pleasure without a purpose were a fault. But rest, laughter, and idle hours are part of a good life, not a theft from it. An afternoon spent with friends, a slow morning with a book, or an evening doing nothing at all can restore us in ways that work never could. Enjoyment is not the opposite of a meaningful life; it is one of its ingredients. If the time brought joy, peace, or closeness, then it was used well, even if it produced nothing to show for it.
