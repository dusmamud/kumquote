Wright pictures the present as a moving shadow — a thin, shifting line between what has been and what will be. It cannot be held still or possessed; it passes even as we notice it. This image explains why presence is so difficult and so precious. Yesterday is fixed and tomorrow is imagined, but only the present is alive, and it is always moving. To live well is to stand in that moving shadow without clinging to the past or racing into the future, meeting each moment as it arrives and letting it go as it passes.
