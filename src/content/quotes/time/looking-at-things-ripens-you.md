Van Gogh defends slow attention in a world that prizes speed. Looking at something for a long time — really seeing it — changes the looker, not just the thing seen. Ripening is a slow process, like fruit maturing in sun; it cannot be forced. The deeper meaning van Gogh promises is not information but transformation: sustained attention teaches patience, reveals detail, and deepens feeling. Whether the object is a painting, a person, or a landscape, the reward of looking long is a richer inner life, ripened by time.
