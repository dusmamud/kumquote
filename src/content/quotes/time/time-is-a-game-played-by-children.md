Children live inside the present moment in a way adults often forget. They do not check the clock, worry about yesterday, or rush toward tomorrow; they are absorbed in whatever is before them, and that absorption is a kind of wisdom. Time, for them, is not a resource to manage but a game to enjoy. The quote invites us to recover something of that freedom: to play, to notice, to let an hour be full of itself rather than a step toward something else. Growing up should not mean losing the ability to be fully here.
