Of everything we own, time is the scarcest. Money can be earned, things can be bought, and even health can often be regained, but time, once spent, never returns. Hemingway's blunt line reminds us to treat it as the rarest resource, not the cheapest. We waste it casually on distraction and delay, as if the supply were endless, yet each of us holds only a finite number of days. Spend time like a miser spends gold: carefully, deliberately, on what truly matters. The people and purposes we give our hours to are the real measure of our lives.
