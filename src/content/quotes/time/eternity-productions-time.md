We often set eternity and time against each other, as though what lasts forever matters and what passes is worthless. Blake reverses that. Eternity, he says, loves what time produces: the poems, the paintings, the acts of kindness that only exist because someone made them in a brief moment. Our temporary works are not poor copies of something eternal; they are what eternity cherishes. That gives our days a surprising weight. The things we create in time, however small, are loved by something far larger than time itself.
