Steinbeck observes that time judges everything without wanting anything for itself. Critics have tastes, rivalries, and ambitions; time has none of these, and that is what makes its verdict so final. Fashions fade, reputations rise and fall, but what is genuinely good survives the slow test of years. The lesson is to build for time rather than for applause: do work that can withstand the only critic who cannot be flattered, bribed, or argued with. Aim for work that outlasts applause, and time will be your witness.
