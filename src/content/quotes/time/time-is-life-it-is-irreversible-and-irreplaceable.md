Alan Lakein states the equation plainly: time is life, and the two cannot be separated. Time is irreversible — no hour returns — and irreplaceable — no amount of money buys it back. That makes every wasted hour a small piece of life spent on nothing, and every well-used hour a piece of life claimed. But Lakein does not end with a warning; he ends with a promise. Mastering time is not about rigid schedules or constant busyness, but about choosing deliberately, and that choice is what turns time from a thief into a tool for making the most of life.
