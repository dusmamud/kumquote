Seneca catches us in a contradiction we live every day. We complain that life is short — that there is never enough time — and then act as if our days were endless, postponing what matters and filling hours with things we do not value. The complaint and the behavior cannot both be honest. If we truly believed our days were few, we would guard them fiercely. Seneca's point is that the feeling of a short life comes not from the calendar but from our own carelessness with it, and the remedy is to start living as though the count were real.
