Harris spots the trap in how we treat rest. We postpone it until the work is done, but the work is never done, so rest never comes. The moment we feel we cannot afford to relax is exactly when we need it most, because a tired mind makes poorer decisions and slower progress. Rest is not a reward for finishing; it is part of finishing well. A short pause when the pressure peaks often returns more time than it costs, in clarity, energy, and fewer mistakes made in haste.
