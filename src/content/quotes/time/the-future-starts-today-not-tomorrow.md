This sentence removes the comfortable excuse we have: tomorrow. It is easy to imagine a better future while postponing the first step, as if time itself will do the work for us. Pope John Paul II insists that the future is not a distant place we arrive at later; it is built by what we choose right now. Today is the only day we can act in, and every delay hands our plans to a version of ourselves that may never come. Starting today does not mean finishing today, only beginning, and that single choice turns hope into something real.
