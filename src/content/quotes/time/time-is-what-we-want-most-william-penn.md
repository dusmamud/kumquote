Penn wrote this more than three hundred years ago, before phones,
feeds, and notifications — which tells you the problem is not the
technology. We want more time more than almost anything, yet we
spend it as if it were the cheapest thing we own: on arguments
that do not matter, on worry about things already decided, on
scrolling that leaves nothing behind.

The quote stings because it is a mirror, not advice. You cannot
get more time, and you cannot save it for later. The only honest
response is to notice where today actually went, and to move one
hour tomorrow from the worst use to the best one. Wanting time and
using it well are two different skills — and only the second one
changes a life.
