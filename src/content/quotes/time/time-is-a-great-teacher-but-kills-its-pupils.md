Berlioz gives time two faces at once: teacher and executioner. The teaching part is familiar — experience slowly shows us what matters, what lasts, and what was never worth the worry. But the second half is the sting: the lessons arrive on a clock that never stops, and the pupil does not attend forever. The quote asks for honesty about the price of delay, not panic. If time teaches, then waiting is itself a lesson with a cost. Learn early, and act while the lesson is still useful.
