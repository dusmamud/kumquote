Douglas Adams, in his playful way, points at something real: our experience of time is slippery and subjective. An hour can drag or fly depending on attention, mood, and company, and lunchtime — that ordinary pause — somehow both anchors the day and disappears within it. The joke carries a gentle philosophy: time is not the rigid ruler we imagine but a lived, flexible thing. Taking the line lightly reminds us not to be tyrannized by the clock. Sometimes the wisest response to time's strangeness is simply to laugh, and to enjoy lunch.
