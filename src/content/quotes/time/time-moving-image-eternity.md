Eternity is hard for a human mind to grasp, because we live inside days and hours. Plato offers time as a moving picture of that timeless reality, like a film that lets us glimpse something too vast to see all at once. Each passing moment is one frame of a much larger whole. This idea gives our brief lives a quiet dignity: the days we live are not separate from eternity but small moving images of it. What we do with our time, then, is part of something far bigger than a calendar.
