Thomas Jefferson makes a bold claim: the feeling of never having enough time is mostly a problem of losing time, not lacking it. His remedy is a decision, not a technique — determine never to be idle. That does not mean never resting, but never drifting: filling the hours with intention instead of letting them scatter. Most of us complain about time while quietly spending it on things we would not choose if we thought about them. Jefferson's test is simple and uncomfortable. If we stop losing time, we may discover we had enough of it all along.
