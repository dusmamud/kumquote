Big stretches of time feel hard to manage, but minutes are small enough to handle. Chesterfield's advice works because it breaks a large problem into tiny pieces: use each minute well, and the hours add up on their own. A few wasted minutes here and there seem harmless, yet they quietly become lost days. On the other hand, minutes spent with care become hours of real progress. This thought teaches that good time management is not about grand plans, but about small, steady choices made again and again, until a well-spent life is simply the sum of well-spent minutes.
