Lao Tzu's blunt translation cuts through our favorite excuse. We all have the same twenty-four hours; what differs is what we choose to fill them with. Saying we have no time usually means the thing is not a priority, and admitting that is more honest — and more useful — than pretending time is the obstacle. Once we see time as something we shape through choices, we regain power over it. The question changes from "Where will I find time?" to "What do I actually want?" — and the answer tells us how to spend our days.
