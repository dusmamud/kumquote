Each day arrives with the same twenty-four hours, and the difference between people is rarely the time they have but how they command it. A day that runs you is a day of reactions — interruptions, demands, and distractions deciding where your attention goes. Running the day means choosing in advance what matters and protecting time for it. Rohn's line is a reminder that control is not about rigidity; it is about intention. When we direct our days, even small hours add up to real progress. When we do not, the days pass and we wonder where they went.
