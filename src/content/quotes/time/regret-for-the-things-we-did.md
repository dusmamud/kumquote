Harris draws a painful distinction between two kinds of regret. The mistakes we made fade with time: we learn, we apologise, we move on, and the sting softens year by year. But the things we never tried leave a different wound, because there is nothing to learn from and nothing to repair. The road not taken stays perfect in our imagination, and no amount of time can answer the question of what might have been. The lesson is clear: act, even imperfectly, because doing and failing heals, while never trying does not.
