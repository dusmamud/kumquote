Chaucer wrote centuries ago that time flees whether we sleep, wake, roam, or ride, and his warning has lost none of its force. Time does not pause for our hesitation, our fear, or our perfect moment. It moves at the same pace for the prepared and the procrastinating alike. The hours we spend waiting for better conditions are spent just the same as the hours we use well. This is not meant to frighten, but to free us from the habit of postponement. Begin the work, make the call, take the step. The day will pass either way, so let it pass with something done rather than something endlessly deferred.
