Atwood challenges the picture of time as a straight line carrying us from past to future. If time is a dimension, like height or depth, then the past is not behind us but around us, still present in the space we move through. Memories, old rooms, and former selves remain reachable, not lost, because time has volume rather than mere length. This view explains why a smell or a song can return us instantly to years ago: we are not traveling backward along a line but stepping sideways into a dimension that was always there.
