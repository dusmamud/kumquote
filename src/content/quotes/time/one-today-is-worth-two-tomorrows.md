Franklin's arithmetic is simple and ruthless: a single day used now outweighs two days promised later. Tomorrow is a comfortable fiction, always arriving but never quite here, and plans parked there have a habit of staying parked forever. Today is the only day that actually exists, the only one where work can be done, calls can be made, and changes can begin. Two tomorrows sound generous, but they are only promises. One today is real, and reality, however small, is worth more than any promise.
