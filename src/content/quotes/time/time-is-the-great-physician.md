Disraeli offers a quiet kind of hope: many wounds do not need solving, only time. Grief, embarrassment, and anger feel permanent in the moment, as if they will never loosen their grip. But time works like a slow medicine, dulling the sharp edges day by day until one morning we notice the pain has changed. This is not forgetting, and it is not a cure for everything, but it is a real force. When nothing else helps, the honest advice is often the simplest: give it time, and let the physician work.
