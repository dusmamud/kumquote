Marcus Aurelius, emperor and Stoic, reminds himself that life could end at any moment. This is not a morbid thought; it is a clarifying one. If today were your last, what would you do, say, and think? The question strips away the trivial — the grudges, the vanity, the endless postponement — and leaves only what matters. We act as if we have unlimited time, and so we waste it on things we would not choose if we knew the truth. Remembering our mortality is not fear; it is focus.
