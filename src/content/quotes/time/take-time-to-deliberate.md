Napoleon understood the two halves of good timing. Deliberation has its place: a hasty decision can ruin everything, and careful thought prevents costly mistakes. But thinking has a limit. Once the moment for action arrives, further delay becomes its own kind of failure, and the opportunity slips away while we are still weighing options. The wise person knows when the thinking is done and acts with full commitment. Do not confuse preparation with progress. Plan carefully, choose your moment, and then go in completely — half-hearted action is often worse than no action at all.
