Young calls procrastination a thief because it steals quietly, in small amounts that are easy to excuse. A postponed task rarely feels like a loss in the moment, yet the hours add up into days, and the days into chances that never return. The quote works because it names the crime plainly: delay is not harmless rest, it is time spent without consent. The remedy is not guilt but a smaller first step, taken now, which breaks the spell of waiting for a perfect moment. Guard your hours as you would your money, and the thief finds less to take.
