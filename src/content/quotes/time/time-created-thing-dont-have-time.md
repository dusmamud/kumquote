Lao Tzu's blunt translation cuts through our favourite excuse. When we say we have no time, we usually mean something else: that the thing is not important enough, or that we are afraid to begin, or that we prefer the comfort of a crowded schedule. Time is created, in the sense that we shape it by our choices. Admitting that we do not want to do something is uncomfortable, but it is also freeing. Once we see that time follows priority, we can stop blaming the clock and start choosing honestly.
