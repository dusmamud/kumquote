Horace wrote carpe diem more than two thousand years ago, and its advice has not aged a day. Seize the day, he says, and trust tomorrow as little as you can — not because tomorrow is evil, but because it is uncertain. We plan, we postpone, we assume there will be time later, yet the only day we can hold is this one. The quote is not a call to recklessness but to presence: to live today fully instead of lending it to a future that may never arrive. Two millennia later, the warning still lands exactly where it should.
