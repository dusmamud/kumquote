Gracián reminds us that time is the one possession no one can take from us and the only wealth everyone holds equally. Money, status, and property can be lost or given away, but our hours remain ours until they are spent. Even the poorest person owns this fortune, and even the richest cannot buy more of it. The quote reframes poverty and wealth entirely: the question is not how much we own but how we spend the time that belongs to us. It is the most democratic of all resources — and the most easily wasted.
