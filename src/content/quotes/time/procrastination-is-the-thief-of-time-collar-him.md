Charles Dickens turns procrastination into a criminal and gives us a direct order: catch him. The image works because delay really does steal — not in one dramatic robbery, but in small amounts, day after day, until years have quietly disappeared. We rarely notice the theft while it happens, which is exactly what makes it dangerous. Dickens does not ask for guilt or regret; he asks for action. Collaring the thief means starting now, before the moment passes. The quote is short, but its demand is urgent: the time we protect today is the only time we truly own.
