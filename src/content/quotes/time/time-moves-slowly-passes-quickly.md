Time feels contradictory: it crawls through a dull afternoon yet races through a happy year. Walker captures this paradox in a single line, reminding us that time's speed depends less on the clock than on how fully we inhabit each moment. Boredom stretches minutes into hours, while joy compresses hours into minutes. The lesson is gentle but firm: do not merely wait for time to pass, because it will pass either way. Fill your hours with attention and meaning, and even slow days will feel like they belonged to you.
