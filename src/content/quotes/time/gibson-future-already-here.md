Gibson points out that the future does not arrive everywhere at once. New ideas, tools and ways of living already exist somewhere — in a laboratory, a small community, or a single person's experiment — long before they reach the rest of us. What looks like science fiction today is often ordinary life for someone, somewhere, right now. That changes how we should look at change: instead of waiting for tomorrow to arrive, we can go and find where it is already happening. The future is uneven, and curiosity is how we catch up with it.
