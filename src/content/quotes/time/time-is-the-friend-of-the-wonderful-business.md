Buffett's line turns time from a neutral clock into a judge. A wonderful business — one with loyal customers, honest pricing, and real value — grows stronger the longer it runs, because every year adds reputation, habit, and trust. A mediocre business, by contrast, slowly leaks: customers drift away, costs creep up, and shortcuts catch up with it. The same test applies beyond investing. Good habits, honest work, and strong relationships compound quietly over years, while shallow efforts fade. Time does not treat everything equally; it rewards what is sound and exposes what is not.
