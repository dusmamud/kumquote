It is easy to blame the clock when a day feels too short, but the complaint often hides a deeper truth about how the day was spent. People who scatter their hours on trifles feel rushed precisely because nothing important got done. Time seems brief when it is wasted, and generous when it is used with focus. This idea turns the complaint around: instead of asking for more time, ask whether the time already given was used well. Owning our choices about time is the first step toward days that feel full rather than frantic.
