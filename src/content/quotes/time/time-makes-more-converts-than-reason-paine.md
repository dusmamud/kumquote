Paine notes that arguments rarely change minds as surely as time does. Reason can be resisted, debated, and dismissed, but lived experience over months and years quietly wears down old certainties. People who would never be persuaded by a speech slowly come around as events unfold and the truth of an idea becomes visible in daily life. This is why patience matters in any cause: the calendar is often a better advocate than the debater. Rather than forcing agreement now, the wise reformer plants the idea and lets time do its steady work of conversion.
