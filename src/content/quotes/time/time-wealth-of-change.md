Tagore draws a sharp line between time as lived and time as measured. Real time is rich with change: seasons turning, people growing, ideas ripening. The clock, however, reduces all of that to identical ticks, making every hour look the same and every day feel interchangeable. When we live by the clock alone, we count minutes instead of noticing what the minutes contain. The wealth of time is not in how much of it we have, but in how much change and meaning fills it. Slow down enough to see the change happening, and the same hours become richer. Time only feels poor when we stop paying attention to what it is doing.
