Delay feels harmless in the moment, but every postponed task quietly grows heavier. What could be finished today becomes tomorrow's burden, and tomorrow brings its own work besides. Franklin's advice is simple because it cuts through every excuse: if something can be done now, do it now. Acting promptly frees the mind from the weight of unfinished business and keeps small duties from piling into large problems. The habit of doing things today builds trust in ourselves, and that trust makes each new day lighter and more open to what matters most.
