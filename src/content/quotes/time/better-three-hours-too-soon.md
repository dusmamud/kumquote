Shakespeare gives punctuality a vivid defence: arriving far too early costs almost nothing, while arriving a minute late can cost everything. The extra hours are not wasted — they are a cushion against the unexpected, a chance to prepare, and a sign of respect for the people waiting. Lateness, by contrast, is a small theft of other people's time and a risk that cannot be undone once the moment has passed. The line is comic in its original play, but the wisdom is serious. Being early is cheap insurance; being late is a debt you can never repay.
