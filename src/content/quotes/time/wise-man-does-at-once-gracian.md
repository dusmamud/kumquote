Gracián observes that the wise and the foolish often end up doing the same thing; the difference is only timing. The fool delays, dithers, and waits for perfect conditions, finally acting when the moment has nearly passed. The wise person acts at once, while the action still matters, saving the time and worry that procrastination consumes. Doing it now does not mean rushing blindly; it means deciding promptly and moving while the opportunity is fresh. In the end, delay rarely improves a decision, it only shrinks the time left to enjoy its results.
