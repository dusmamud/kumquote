Earl Nightingale exposes an excuse: we avoid long goals because they take too long. A degree, a book, a new skill — the years stretch ahead and we decide it is not worth starting. But his second sentence dismantles the logic: the time will pass anyway. Five years from now will arrive whether we spend them building something or not. The question is never whether the time will pass, but what we will have to show for it when it does. Fear of the duration is really fear of the effort, and once we see that, starting becomes the obvious choice.
