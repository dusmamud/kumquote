Lukas, speaking from the racetrack, states a law that applies to every team: the group moves at the leader's pace. If the leader is sloppy, late, or half-hearted, the team will mirror it, no matter what the mission statement says. If the leader is disciplined, prepared, and hungry, the team rises to match. People watch what leaders do far more than what they say. Setting the pace is not about shouting or speed; it is about consistency, standards, and personal example. Want a faster pack? Start by running faster yourself, and watch everyone else find another gear.
