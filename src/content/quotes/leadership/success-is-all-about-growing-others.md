Welch describes how success changes when you become a leader. Before that point, progress is personal: your skills, your results, your reputation. Afterward, your results come through other people, so your job becomes helping them grow. A leader who keeps chasing personal credit holds the team back, while one who develops others multiplies the whole group's output. This shift is hard because it means measuring yourself by other people's achievements. But that is the point: leadership success is not about how far you rise, but about how many people you lift with you.
