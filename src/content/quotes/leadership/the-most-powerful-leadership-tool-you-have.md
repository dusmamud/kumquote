John Wooden, who won ten national championships as a coach, knew that speeches and slogans move no one for long. What people actually follow is the example in front of them: how a leader prepares, how they treat others, how they behave when nobody is watching. A personal example is powerful precisely because it cannot be faked for long — people see the gap between words and actions faster than any speech can cover it. Wooden's lesson is that leadership is lived before it is taught, and the standard we set for ourselves quietly becomes the standard of everyone around us.
