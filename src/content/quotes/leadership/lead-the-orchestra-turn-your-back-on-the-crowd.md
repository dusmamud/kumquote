Lucado's image is striking: the conductor faces away from the audience and toward the musicians. A leader who wants to lead must be willing to turn away from applause and face the people doing the work. Leadership is not about being seen; it is about seeing others clearly and guiding them. The crowd offers approval, but approval is not direction. The leader's attention belongs to the team — their needs, their timing, their harmony. Those who lead for the crowd's sake lose the orchestra. Those who serve the orchestra earn the crowd's respect in the end.
