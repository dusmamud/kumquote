Powell, a soldier and statesman, knew that confusion is the enemy of action. In any crisis, voices multiply — arguments, doubts, competing plans — and a group can freeze while it debates. The great leader's gift is simplification: seeing the heart of the problem and stating a solution so clearly that everyone understands it and can act on it. This is not dumbing things down; it is the hard work of thinking clearly enough to be brief. Clarity, by contrast, is a form of courage.
