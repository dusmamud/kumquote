Kennedy ties leadership to continuous learning. A leader who stops learning soon stops leading well, because the world changes and yesterday's knowledge goes stale. New challenges demand fresh understanding, and leaders must keep studying their field, their people, and themselves. Learning also keeps a leader humble and open to advice, instead of trapped by old certainties. The two qualities feed each other: learning improves judgement, and leadership creates new lessons to learn from. Neither can stand alone for long, which is why Kennedy calls them indispensable to each other.
