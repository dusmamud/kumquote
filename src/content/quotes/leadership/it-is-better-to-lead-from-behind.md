Leading from behind looks strange to those who think leadership means standing at the front and taking the spotlight. Mandela saw it differently. A leader who puts others in front gives them room to grow, to be seen, and to take pride in their work. When victory comes, the team celebrates because it was truly theirs. The leader stays behind, guiding quietly, stepping forward only when danger or difficulty requires it. This kind of leadership builds trust and confidence, because people know they will not be used as props for someone else's glory.
