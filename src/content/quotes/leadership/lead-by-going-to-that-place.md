Pointing at a destination and ordering people toward it is management, not leadership. Kesey argues that real leadership means going there first — stepping into the unknown yourself and showing, through your own actions, why the journey is worth making. People are rarely moved by instructions alone; they are moved by example. When a leader takes the first step, fear shrinks and the path becomes believable. Words can describe a place, but only a leader who has been there can make the case for going. Lead by going, not by pointing.
