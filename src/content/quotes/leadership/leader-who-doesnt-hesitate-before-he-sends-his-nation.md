Meir, who led a nation through war, insists that hesitation before grave decisions is not weakness but duty. A leader who sends others into danger without pausing to weigh the cost has not understood the weight of the role. Leadership is not about appearing decisive at all costs; it is about feeling the full responsibility of every choice that affects other lives. The pause before action is where wisdom lives, the moment to ask whether the sacrifice is necessary and just. Leaders who never hesitate are dangerous, because they have stopped seeing people and started seeing only objectives.
