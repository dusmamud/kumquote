This definition turns leadership into persuasion rather than command. Anyone can order people around, but a leader makes a hard task feel worth doing, so that people give their best instead of their minimum. Truman points to the skill of aligning duty with willingness: explaining why the work matters, sharing the burden, and treating people with respect. When followers feel ownership rather than compulsion, they bring energy and ideas that no order can extract. The test of a leader is not obedience on the day, but whether people would follow again tomorrow, willingly and with pride.
