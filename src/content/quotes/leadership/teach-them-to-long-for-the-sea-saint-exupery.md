Saint-Exupéry contrasts two ways of organizing people: the manager's way of assigning tasks and the leader's way of awakening longing. Wood, plans, and schedules matter, but they are means, not the mission, and workers who only see chores will do only chores. When people long for the sea, the work changes meaning; gathering wood becomes part of a voyage, and they bring their own energy, ingenuity, and care. Leaders therefore begin not with instructions but with inspiration, giving people something vast enough to be worth building toward.
