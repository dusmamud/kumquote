Harry Truman's definition of leadership is blunt and practical. Getting people to do what they want is easy; the real test is the hard, necessary work nobody volunteers for. A leader's job is not to make the work disappear but to give it meaning — to connect a difficult task to a purpose people care about, so they do it willingly and even with pride. That ability is rarer than authority or charisma. Orders can force compliance, but only leadership can turn reluctant effort into genuine commitment, and that difference decides whether a team merely functions or truly performs.
