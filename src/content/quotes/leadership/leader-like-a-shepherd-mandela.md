Mandela pictures leadership as quiet guidance from behind rather than loud command from the front. A shepherd does not drag the flock; he stays at the back, letting the boldest go ahead while gently steering the whole group toward good ground. The followers feel free, even believing the journey was their own idea, yet the leader's steady presence keeps them safe and on course. This is leadership without ego, where influence works through trust and patience rather than force. The greatest leaders often look invisible, because their people are moving well and need no shouting to find the way.
