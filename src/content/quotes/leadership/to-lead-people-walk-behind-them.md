Lao Tzu's paradox turns leadership upside down: the best leader does not march at the front demanding attention, but walks behind, supporting and guiding. When people feel the work is their own, they give it their best; when a leader constantly takes the spotlight, people become followers in the worst sense — passive and dependent. Walking behind is not weakness; it is trust, and it is the quiet confidence of a leader who does not need applause to know the direction is right.
