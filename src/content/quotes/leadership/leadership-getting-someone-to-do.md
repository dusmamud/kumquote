Landry captures the paradox at the heart of leadership: the work people resist is often the path to what they most want. Practice, discipline, and honest feedback are uncomfortable, yet they are how goals are reached. The leader's job is to connect the unwelcome task to the desired outcome, so effort feels meaningful rather than imposed. This requires trust, because people must believe the leader sees their goal clearly. When that link is strong, people do hard things willingly — not because they were forced, but because they understand why.
