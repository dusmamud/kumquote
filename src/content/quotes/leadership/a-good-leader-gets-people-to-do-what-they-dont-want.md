Truman offers a wry definition of leadership: getting people to do what they would rather not do, and like it. The secret is not force but persuasion, making the necessary feel meaningful. People resist orders they do not understand, but they embrace work they believe in. A good leader connects the task to a purpose people can own, so effort feels chosen rather than imposed. When people like the work, they do it better. Leadership is the art of turning reluctance into willing effort.
