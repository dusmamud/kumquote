Hopper draws a sharp line that many managers blur. Things — budgets, schedules, systems — can be managed with plans and checklists, but people cannot be managed the same way. People need direction, trust, and respect; they follow leaders who listen, explain the purpose, and stand beside them. Treating people like items on a spreadsheet breeds resentment, while treating them as partners builds loyalty. The best leaders know which tool to use where: manage the work, but lead the people doing it. When that distinction is clear, teams run better and people grow. Remember the difference every day.
