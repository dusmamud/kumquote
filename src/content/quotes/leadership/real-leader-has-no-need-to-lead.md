Miller describes a leader so secure that they do not need the spotlight. Needing to lead is often about ego: being seen in charge, hearing orders obeyed, collecting deference. The real leader is content with something quieter, pointing the way and letting others walk it, even if no one applauds the gesture. This is leadership without performance, where influence works through example and direction rather than command. The paradox is that the person who least needs to lead is often the one people most willingly follow.
