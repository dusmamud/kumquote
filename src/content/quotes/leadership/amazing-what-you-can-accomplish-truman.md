Truman points to the hidden tax that ego levies on every team: when people fight over credit, energy goes into rivalry instead of results. Letting go of the need to be recognized frees everyone to focus on the work itself, and paradoxically, far more gets done. Teams that share credit freely attract better ideas, because no one holds back for fear that someone else will shine. The leader's job is to make success the goal, not applause, and to trust that contribution matters more than applause.
