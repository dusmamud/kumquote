Sam Walton understood that leadership is less about giving orders and more about giving belief. People who doubt themselves hold back, wait for permission, and play safe; people who believe in themselves take initiative, solve problems, and stretch beyond what their job description asks. A leader who deliberately builds self-esteem is not being soft — they are unlocking capacity that no system or incentive can buy. Walton's point is practical as well as kind: confidence compounds. When each person grows a little braver, the whole organization becomes capable of things that once looked impossible.
