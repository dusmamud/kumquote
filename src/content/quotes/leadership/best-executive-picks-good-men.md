Great leadership has two halves, and Roosevelt names both. The first is choosing the right people — judgment in hiring matters more than any management technique. The second is harder: once you have chosen well, leave them alone. Meddling signals distrust, and distrust kills initiative. A leader who hovers over every detail becomes the bottleneck of the whole organization. The discipline of stepping back takes self-restraint, but it is what allows talented people to do their best work. Pick well, trust deeply, and get out of the way.
