Maxwell names the delicate distance of leadership. Too far ahead, and a leader becomes distant and unrelatable — people cannot follow someone they cannot reach. Too close, and the leader offers no direction, merely blending into the crowd. Good leadership lives in the tension: near enough to understand people's struggles, far enough ahead to show them the next step. This balance is not fixed; it shifts with each person and situation. The leader's art is constantly adjusting that distance, staying connected while still pointing forward.
