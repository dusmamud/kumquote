Sinek corrects a common misunderstanding about leadership: the leader is not the person who does the most work. Doing the work for others keeps them dependent and small. Real leadership is teaching, coaching, and clearing the path so people can figure things out themselves. That takes patience, because watching someone struggle is harder than fixing it for them. But the struggle is where growth lives. When people succeed beyond what they thought possible, the credit belongs to them, and the leader's quiet reward is a team that no longer needs rescuing.
