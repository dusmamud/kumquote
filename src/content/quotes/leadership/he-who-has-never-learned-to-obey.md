Good commanders are made, not born, and their training begins in following orders. Someone who has obeyed knows what a fair order feels like, how hard it is to carry one out, and what respect a leader owes the people they command. Without that experience, authority turns into arrogance: orders become unreasonable, and trust breaks down. Every great leader first learned to listen, to serve, and to put the mission above pride. Before you ask others to follow you, prove you can follow well. Obedience is not the opposite of leadership; it is its first school.
