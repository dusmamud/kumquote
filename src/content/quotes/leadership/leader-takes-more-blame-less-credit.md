Leadership is often judged by visible victories, but its truest test appears when things go wrong. A good leader steps forward to carry the blame, shielding the team from finger-pointing and fear, and steps back when praise is handed out, letting others take the credit they earned. This generosity builds trust faster than any speech. People follow a leader who protects them in failure and honours them in success, because such a leader proves the team matters more than their own reputation.
