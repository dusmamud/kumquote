Drucker points to the quietest skill in leadership: listening for what is left unsaid. Words in a meeting are only part of the message; hesitation, silence, and tone carry the rest. A leader who only hears spoken words misses fears, doubts, and ideas that people are afraid to voice. True communication means creating safety so the unsaid can surface, and paying attention when it does. The most important information in any room is often the thing nobody has said yet.
