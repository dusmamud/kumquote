Godin reframes leadership as hospitality, not command. Instead of issuing orders, a leader builds a platform, a stage where other people's ideas can be seen, tested, and spread. The best ideas often come from the edges of an organization, not the top, but they die without someone to amplify them. A leader's real work is spotting good ideas, removing obstacles, and giving credit loudly. When people feel their contributions matter, they bring their best thinking. Leadership, then, is less about having the answers and more about making room for everyone else's answers to shine.
