Drucker teaches that great leadership is not about fixing every weakness, but about arranging strengths so well that weaknesses stop mattering. Every person and every team has gaps, and a leader who obsesses over them wastes energy and morale. The wiser move is to align people so that one person's strength covers another's weakness, and the whole becomes stronger than its parts. When strengths are pointed in the same direction, the system works — not because it is flawless, but because its flaws no longer decide the outcome.
