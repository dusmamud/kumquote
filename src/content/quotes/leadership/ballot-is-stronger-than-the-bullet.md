Lincoln claims that the vote outweighs the gun. A bullet can win a battle, but a ballot can settle a question for a whole people without bloodshed, and its result carries a legitimacy that force can never claim. The strength of the ballot lies in numbers and consent: millions of quiet decisions that no army can overturn. Leaders who trust the vote are betting on reason over violence. The sentence is a reminder that the most powerful weapon in a free society is the one every citizen already holds.
