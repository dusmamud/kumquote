Walton treats encouragement as a leadership strategy, not a courtesy. People who believe in themselves take initiative, admit mistakes early, and attempt harder tasks, while people who feel small play safe and hide problems. A leader who notices good work and says so plainly changes what a team believes it can do. The cost is nothing but attention, yet many leaders skip it, assuming that pay is praise enough. Walton's point is practical: self-esteem is fuel, and the leader's job includes keeping the tank full.
