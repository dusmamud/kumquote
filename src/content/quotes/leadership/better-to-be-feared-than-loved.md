Machiavelli gives cold advice about power: love is fickle, held by obligation, while fear is held by the dread of punishment, which does not break so easily. A ruler who must choose should pick the bond that lasts longer, though he must avoid being hated, since hatred invites rebellion. The line is often quoted as cruelty, but its point is practical: a leader cannot build on affection alone. Authority needs a firm edge, or it will be tested the moment it is convenient to do so.
