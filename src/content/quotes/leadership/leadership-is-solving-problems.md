Powell strips leadership down to its simplest form. Titles, speeches, and strategy all matter less than one question: are problems getting solved? A leader who solves problems earns trust quickly, because people can see the results in their daily lives. The definition also clarifies priorities: when in doubt, find the obstacle and remove it. Leaders do not need to solve everything personally — often their job is to clear the way so others can solve it — but the focus stays on progress, not appearances.
