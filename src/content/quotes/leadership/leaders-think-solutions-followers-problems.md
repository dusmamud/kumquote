Tracy draws a simple line between two habits of mind. Talking about problems feels productive — it names what is wrong and assigns blame — but it rarely changes anything. Leaders redirect the conversation toward solutions: what can be done, by whom, and by when. This is not denial of difficulty but discipline of attention. Problems deserve honest acknowledgment, yet the energy of a team should flow toward fixing them. The person who consistently turns "here is what's wrong" into "here is what we can do" is already leading, whatever their title.
