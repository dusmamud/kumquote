Hoffer describes leadership as holding two opposite languages at once. The leader must be practical and realistic — budgets, deadlines, and facts cannot be wished away. Yet people are not moved by spreadsheets; they need a vision worth striving for. The leader therefore speaks the language of the idealist while working with the tools of the realist. This dual fluency is rare, and it is what separates managers from leaders. Vision without realism is fantasy, and realism without vision is drudgery; leadership joins the two.
