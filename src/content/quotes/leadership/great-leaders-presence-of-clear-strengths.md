Zenger rejects the idea that a leader must be flawless. Every person carries weaknesses, and the effort to erase them all leaves little energy for anything else. Great leaders are recognized instead by strengths so clear that people can name them: courage, fairness, vision, or steady judgment. Those strengths set the direction and carry the team through doubt. The practical lesson is to build on what you do well and let others cover the rest, rather than hiding every flaw and standing for nothing.
