Drucker strips leadership of its glamour. Speeches, charisma, and popularity are attributes, and attributes are not results. A leader may be eloquent and beloved yet achieve nothing, while a quiet, unpopular leader may transform an organization. Effectiveness is measured by what actually changes — goals reached, people grown, problems solved. This standard is demanding but fair: it judges leaders by their work, not their image. Anyone who wants to lead should ask not how they appear, but what their leadership produces.
