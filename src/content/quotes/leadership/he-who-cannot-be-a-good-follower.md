Authority is often mistaken for leadership, but Aristotle points to its quieter foundation: followership. A person who has never learned to listen, to serve, and to support another's direction cannot understand what followers need from a leader. Following teaches humility, patience, and the value of clear guidance — lessons that cannot be learned from above. The best leaders remember what it felt like to be led, and they lead in the way they once wished to be led. Leadership, in this view, is not a rank to seize but a responsibility earned by first learning to follow well.
