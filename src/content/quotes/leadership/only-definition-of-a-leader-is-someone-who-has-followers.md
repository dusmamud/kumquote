Drucker strips leadership down to a single test: does anyone actually follow? Titles, offices, and org charts prove nothing, because authority can be granted while influence must be earned. A leader is recognised not by the position held but by the people who choose to move in the direction set. That makes leadership a relationship rather than a rank, built on trust, clarity, and example. It sets a hard standard: if no one is following, the leadership is only imagined. The work is not to collect authority but to become worth following, in ways people can see and believe.
