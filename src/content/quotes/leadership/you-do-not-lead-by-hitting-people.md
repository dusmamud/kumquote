Real leadership persuades rather than compels. Hitting people over the head — through fear, threats, or sheer force — may produce obedience, but it never produces loyalty or genuine commitment. People who are coerced do the minimum and leave the moment they can. Eisenhower, a general who led vast armies, knew that lasting results come from willing cooperation. A leader earns followership by giving people a reason to act, not by forcing their hand. Authority that rests on respect outlasts authority that rests on fear, because people carry it with them willingly.
