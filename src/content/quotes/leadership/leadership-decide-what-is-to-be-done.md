Deciding what must be done is the first half, and it requires judgment, information, and the courage to choose. The second half is harder: getting others to want to do it. Orders can produce obedience, but they rarely produce enthusiasm. People give their best when they understand the purpose and feel ownership of the task. That is why the greatest leaders spend as much time communicating the why as they do deciding the what. A decision imposed is a burden; a decision shared becomes a mission.
