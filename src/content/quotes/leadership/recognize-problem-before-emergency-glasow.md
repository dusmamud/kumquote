Glasow defines leadership as foresight, the ability to spot trouble while it is still small and manageable. Anyone can react to an emergency once it is blazing, but reacting is not leading; it is damage control. The leader's real skill is attention to weak signals: a slipping deadline, a quiet complaint, a number drifting in the wrong direction. Catching these early takes vigilance and the courage to act before others see the need. Problems ignored grow expensive, while problems met early often dissolve with a small correction. True leadership, then, is largely the discipline of noticing soon enough.
