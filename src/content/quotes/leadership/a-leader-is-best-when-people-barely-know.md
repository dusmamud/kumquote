Lao Tzu praises a kind of leadership that is almost invisible. The best leader does not dominate the room or claim every success; instead, they create conditions where people do good work and feel it was their own doing. When the work is done, the team says, "We did it ourselves" — and that is the highest compliment a leader can receive. This stands against the image of leadership as loud command and constant visibility. True authority, Lao Tzu suggests, is quiet: it guides without forcing, supports without smothering, and trusts people enough to let them shine on their own.
