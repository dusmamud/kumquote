Plato warns that refusing to lead is not a neutral choice. Good people often step back from power, calling it dirty or beneath them, and the seat does not stay empty; someone else takes it, often someone worse. The penalty for that refusal falls on everyone, including the person who declined. Leadership, in this view, is a duty rather than an ambition: those with wisdom and character owe it to their communities to serve, precisely because they do not crave the role. Walking away feels clean, but it hands the future to whoever wants it most.
