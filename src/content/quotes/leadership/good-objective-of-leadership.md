Jim Rohn describes leadership as a double improvement. The leader's work is to lift those who are struggling and to stretch those who are already doing well. Both directions matter: neglecting the strugglers abandons people, while neglecting the strong wastes potential. Good leaders meet each person where they are and help them take the next step — a skill, a habit, a belief in themselves. Leadership is not a single speech or a grand gesture. It is the daily practice of noticing people, encouraging them, and helping each one become a little better than yesterday.
