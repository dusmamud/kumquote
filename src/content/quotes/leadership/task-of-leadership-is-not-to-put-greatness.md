Buchan reverses the usual idea of leadership. The leader's job is not to pour greatness into people, as if they were empty. Greatness is already there — in talent, in character, in the desire to do meaningful work. Leadership is the art of drawing it out: asking the right questions, giving people room, trusting them with real responsibility. People rise when they are expected to rise. The leader who tries to manufacture greatness creates dependence; the leader who elicits it creates other leaders. The best leadership leaves people larger than it found them.
