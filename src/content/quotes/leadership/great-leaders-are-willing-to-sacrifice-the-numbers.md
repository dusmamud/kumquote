Sinek draws a clear line between two kinds of leadership by asking what gets sacrificed when pressure comes. Numbers are easy to protect: cut jobs, squeeze hours, and the figures look better for a quarter. But the people who trusted the leader pay the price, and trust, once spent, is slow to return. Great leaders accept a harder quarter to protect their people, knowing that loyal people rebuild results again and again. Poor leaders do the reverse, and end up with fine numbers and an empty team.
