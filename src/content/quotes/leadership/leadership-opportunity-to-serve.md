Power tempts people to treat leadership as a reward: a title, a platform, a chance to be important. Walters warns that this is a misunderstanding of the role. Serving does not mean weakness or indecision. It means using one's position to remove obstacles, support others, and take responsibility when things go wrong. The trumpet call of self-importance is loud and flattering, which is why it is so dangerous. Quiet service rarely draws applause, but it is the foundation of trust, and trust is what makes leadership possible.
