Roosevelt describes leadership as choosing well and then stepping back. A great executive picks capable people, gives them clear purpose, and resists the urge to meddle. Trust is the heart of this approach: people do their best work when they are free to use their judgement. Micromanagement signals distrust and shrinks initiative. The discipline of self-restraint, letting good people do what they were chosen to do, is what separates a leader from a controller. Pick well, then let them work.
