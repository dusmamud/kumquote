Brown defines leadership as accountability for potential — not just for results but for what people and processes could become. A leader looks at a struggling team member or a broken system and asks what it could be, then takes responsibility for closing that gap. This is harder than assigning blame, because it makes the leader answerable for growth. But it is also more powerful: when leaders own the search for potential, people feel seen rather than judged, and organizations improve from the inside rather than by command alone.
