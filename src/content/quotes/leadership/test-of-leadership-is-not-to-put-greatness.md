Buchanan rejects the idea that leaders manufacture greatness in their people. The talent, courage, and goodness are already present, waiting for someone to notice and call them out. A poor leader assumes people are empty and tries to fill them with orders; a great leader assumes people are capable and creates the conditions where that capability appears. Eliciting greatness means asking the right question, giving the hard task, and trusting someone before they have fully proven themselves. Leadership, in this view, is less like building and more like uncovering.
