Roosevelt describes leadership as the art of choosing well and then stepping back. Picking good people matters more than doing everything yourself, because no leader can master every task. But the second half of the advice is harder: once good people are in place, the leader must resist meddling. Constant interference destroys confidence and slows work. Trust is what turns a group of talented individuals into a team that delivers. The best leaders, Roosevelt suggests, combine judgement in selection with discipline in restraint, letting capable people do the work they were chosen for.
