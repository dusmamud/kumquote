Drucker draws a clean line between management and leadership. Doing things right means efficiency: following procedures, meeting targets, and avoiding waste. Doing the right things means direction: choosing the goals that actually matter before worrying about speed. A team can work brilliantly on the wrong problem and still fail. Leaders must therefore decide what is worth doing, while managers make sure it is done well. Both roles matter, but they are not the same. Confusing them leads to busy organisations that move fast in the wrong direction.
