Patton's advice runs against the controlling instinct. Telling people exactly how to do something turns them into instruments and wastes their intelligence. Telling them what needs to be achieved — the goal, clearly — and trusting them to find the way, invites ingenuity. People who own the method bring creativity a commander could never have planned. The surprise Patton promises is real: teams trusted with the how often outperform the plan. Leadership, then, is less about control and more about clarity of purpose plus trust in execution.
