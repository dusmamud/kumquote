A group holds far more knowledge than any single member, but that knowledge is useless if it stays scattered. Follett saw the leader's role as gathering the group's scattered experience into something coherent and shared. When experience is organised, the group becomes smarter than the sum of its parts. Decisions improve, because they draw on everyone's lessons instead of only the loudest voice. The leader, in this view, is less a commander and more a weaver, bringing separate threads of experience into a single strong fabric.
