Hope is often dismissed as wishful thinking, but it is really a decision — a choice to believe that effort still matters even when the odds look poor. Before hope, every door seems closed; after it, we begin to notice openings we had walked past. Choosing hope does not guarantee success, yet it makes action possible, and action is what creates the chance of success. Despair, by contrast, guarantees nothing changes. The power of this line lies in its order: hope comes first, as a deliberate act of the will, and possibility follows from it.
