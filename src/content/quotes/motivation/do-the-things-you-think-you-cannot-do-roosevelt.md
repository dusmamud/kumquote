Roosevelt's rule is blunt because fear is persuasive: the voice that says you cannot do a thing sounds like wisdom, but it is usually only habit protecting itself. The only evidence that changes that voice is doing the thing once. Afterward, the impossible shrinks into something ordinary, and the boundary of what you believe about yourself moves outward. Pick one thing you have been calling impossible — the conversation, the application, the first lesson — and do it today. The fear will not vanish first; it fades after.
