Fear of mistakes is itself the most expensive mistake we can make. It keeps the letter unwritten, the business unstarted, the apology unsaid. Hubbard's warning is not that mistakes are harmless; they are real, and some of them hurt. It is that the fear of them does more damage than the mistakes themselves ever could. Everyone we admire has a long history of errors. The difference is that they made their mistakes in motion, learning as they went, instead of standing still to avoid them. A life without mistakes is a life without much living in it.
