This reframes how a day should be judged. A harvest is visible and final, but seeds are quiet work whose results arrive later, and most meaningful days are full of seeds rather than harvests. Preparing, practising, helping, learning and showing up may look like nothing on the day itself, yet they are the real work. Judging a day by what it produced tempts us to chase quick wins and neglect slow growth. Judging it by what was planted keeps attention on the effort we control. A well-planted day is a successful day, even when nothing is ready to gather yet.
