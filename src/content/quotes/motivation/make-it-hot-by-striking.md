Waiting for the perfect moment is a quiet way of never beginning. Conditions rarely arrive ready-made; they are shaped by the people who act first. Yeats turns the old proverb around: the heat you need is not found, it is made by the striking itself. Begin before you feel fully prepared, and the work will teach you what waiting never could. Momentum builds only once you move. The first imperfect attempt warms the iron, and the second strike lands better than the first ever would have from a standstill.
