Possibility is a roomier home than certainty, and Dickinson invites us to live there. When we dwell in possibility, we stop demanding that the future arrive fully formed before we dare to hope. A life built on possibility stays open to surprise, to second chances, and to doors we have not yet noticed. This is not naive optimism; it is a choice to keep the imagination working even when circumstances feel fixed. If we make our home in what might be, we give ourselves permission to try, to dream, and to begin again.
