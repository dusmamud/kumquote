Our limits are mostly guesses. We assume we know how far we can go, but that assumption is built on what we have tried so far — not on what is actually possible. The only way to find the real edge is to risk passing the imagined one. That risk is real: going too far can mean failing, looking foolish, or wasting effort. But staying safely inside the guessed boundary guarantees a smaller life. Eliot's point is that discovery requires the willingness to overshoot.
