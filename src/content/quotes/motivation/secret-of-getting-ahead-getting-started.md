Twain reduces every grand ambition to a single unglamorous step: begin. We wait for confidence, permission, or a perfect plan, but none of those arrive before we start — they arrive because we start. Getting ahead is not a mystery reserved for the talented; it is the natural result of motion. The first step is often small and unimpressive, yet it breaks the spell of waiting. Once we are moving, the next step becomes clearer, and the one after that. The whole journey, Twain suggests, is unlocked by the simple decision to start now.
