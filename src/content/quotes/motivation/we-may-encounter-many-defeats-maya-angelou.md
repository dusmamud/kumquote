Defeat is a fact of every ambitious life. We will be passed over, turned down, outplayed, and misunderstood, sometimes in the same week. Angelou does not deny any of that. Her line draws a distinction between encountering defeat and being defeated by it. The first is an event; the second is a surrender. To be defeated means to accept the loss as the final word about who we are. But a defeat can also teach, redirect, or end. What matters is not whether we fall, but whether we let the fall decide the rest of the story.
