Three words, and a whole philosophy of luck. Virgil does not promise that bravery always wins, but he observes that good fortune rarely finds the person hiding at home. The brave put themselves where fortune can reach them: they ask, they try, they risk being refused. Cautious people call that luck, but it is really exposure — more attempts, more chances, more doors opened. Fortune is not a lottery ticket; it is a companion that walks beside those willing to move first. Be brave, and give luck somewhere to land.
