Other people can criticize us, overlook us, or speak about us unfairly, and those things sting. But Roosevelt points to a truth: none of it becomes inferiority until we agree with it inside. Feeling inferior is not something handed to us; it is something we consent to, without noticing. We replay the comment, accept the judgment, and start living inside it. The moment we notice that consent, we can withdraw it. Our worth is not decided by a remark, a rejection, or a room that missed us. It rests with us, and no one can take it without our permission.
