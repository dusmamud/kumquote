Success is often measured from the outside: the title, the salary, the applause. Burns asks us to measure it from the inside instead. Failing at something you love still leaves you with the love itself, the craft, the curiosity, the days that felt alive. Succeeding at something you hate can look impressive while emptying you out. The work we choose fills most of our waking hours, and no amount of status makes up for dreading them. Better to stumble in the direction of what you love than to arrive, polished and praised, at a place you never wanted to be.
