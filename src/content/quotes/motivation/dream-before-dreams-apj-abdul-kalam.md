Kalam did not mean daydreaming. His point was sequence: the dream
comes first, and only then does the work get a direction. A student
who can say their dream in one sentence knows what to practise today,
what to ignore, and what to measure at the end of the week. Without
that sentence, hard work scatters — busy days, no progress.

So the practical version of this quote is small. Write the dream
down. Pick one task today that moves it forward. Do that task before
anything else. Motivation, in Kalam's sense, is not a feeling you
wait for. It is a dream remembered every morning, turned into one
concrete action before the day gets noisy.
