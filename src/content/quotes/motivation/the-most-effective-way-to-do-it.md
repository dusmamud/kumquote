Advice about doing things often becomes a substitute for doing them. We research, plan, discuss, and prepare, and the preparation itself starts to feel like progress. Earhart, who flew into skies no woman had crossed before, knew that no amount of thinking replaces the act itself. Doing is the only way to learn what a task truly requires, and every attempt teaches what planning cannot. The task that feels enormous from the outside shrinks once you begin. The most effective way is not the cleverest way; it is simply the doing.
