We treat difficulty as an interruption to a good life, yet the mind grows the way a muscle grows: through resistance. Easy days keep us comfortable but teach us little, while hard problems force us to think, adapt, and find resources we did not know we had. Just as labor builds the body, facing obstacles builds judgment, patience, and courage. The struggle itself is the training. When we stop asking why a challenge appeared and start asking what it is strengthening in us, hardship becomes useful, and we emerge from it more capable than before.
