Other people can take a great deal from us: our time, our comfort, even our freedom. But Douglass draws a line that no one else can cross without our permission. Dignity is not something handed out by the powerful; it lives inside the person who refuses to be made small. When we protect that inner ground, insults and injustice lose their power to define us. The sentence is a declaration of ownership over the self. Nobody can degrade what we refuse to surrender, and that refusal is where freedom truly begins.
