As the sun sets, shadows grow longer, and La Fontaine uses that image for the way friendship deepens with age. In youth, we are busy with ambition, and friendship is one pleasure among many. As life goes on, many things fade — strength, certainty, new beginnings — but friendship grows larger, stretching across the later years like an evening shadow. The line is tender rather than sad: it suggests that what matters most only becomes clearer as the day lengthens. In the evening of life, friends are the longest and warmest shadow we have.
