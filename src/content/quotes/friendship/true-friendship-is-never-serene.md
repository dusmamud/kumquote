A friendship with no friction at all is often a shallow one, because real friends care enough to disagree. They tell us hard truths, argue with us when we are wrong, and refuse to simply nod along. That honesty can make the relationship feel stormy at times, but the storms are a sign of life, not failure. Serenity belongs to polite acquaintances who have nothing at stake. True friends risk the occasional clash because the bond matters more than comfort. A friendship that has survived honest disagreement is stronger than one that never dared to have one.
