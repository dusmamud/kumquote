Friendship is at its best when it wants nothing from the other person except closeness itself. Gibran warns against treating friends as tools for advantage, networking, or gain; the moment friendship serves a purpose beyond itself, it stops being friendship. The true purpose is simpler and deeper: two spirits growing together, sharing thoughts, laughter, and silence, and becoming better for the company. When we stop asking what a friend can do for us and start enjoying who they are, friendship becomes its own reward. That is the only purpose it needs.
