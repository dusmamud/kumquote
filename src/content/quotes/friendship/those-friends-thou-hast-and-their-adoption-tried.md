Polonius advises his son to hold tested friends tightly, and the advice still holds. Real friends are not collected quickly; they are proven over time, through difficulty, honesty, and loyalty. Once someone has shown they will stay when it costs them something, that friendship deserves to be guarded like treasure. The image of hoops of steel suggests a bond that cannot be broken by distance, disagreement, or changing fortune. Choose carefully, test the bond, and then commit fully. A few friendships held this firmly are worth more than a crowd of shallow ones.
