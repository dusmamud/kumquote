We often feel helpless before a friend's troubles. We want to fix everything, to offer advice, to solve it. Thoreau offers a humbler answer: the most I can do for my friend is simply be his friend. Presence is not a small thing. Sitting beside someone, listening without judging, staying when there is nothing to say, these carry a friendship through its hardest hours. We do not need to have the answers. We only need to be there, steadily and without conditions. Sometimes the greatest help is not what we do, but simply that we stay.
