Wilde's wit hides a serious point about honesty between friends. An enemy attacks from behind, in secret, hoping you never see it. A true friend does the opposite: if they must hurt you, they do it to your face, openly. That bluntness is not cruelty but respect. They trust you enough to tell you a truth you may not want to hear, and care enough to risk your anger rather than let you walk into a mistake. A friend who criticises you openly is safer than one who flatters you in person and doubts you behind your back.
