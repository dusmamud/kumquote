We use the word friend lightly, for acquaintances of a week or companions of convenience, but Washington insists that real friendship takes time and testing. Like a plant, it grows slowly, putting down roots through shared seasons before it can stand strong. Adversity is the test: easy company proves little, but a friendship that survives disagreement, hardship, and distance has earned its name. The line warns us not to rush the label or expect instant intimacy. True friendship is grown, not found ready-made, and its slow growth is exactly what makes it last.
