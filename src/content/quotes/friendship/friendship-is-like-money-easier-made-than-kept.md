Samuel Butler compares friendship to money: earning it is the easy part, keeping it is the real work. A new friendship can spark in an afternoon, but lasting ones need steady attention — calls returned, promises kept, apologies made, time given. Many friendships do not end in a fight; they quietly fade because nobody tended them. Like savings, a friendship grows when you keep adding to it and shrinks when you only withdraw. The friends we still have in old age are rarely the ones we met most easily — they are the ones we refused to neglect.
