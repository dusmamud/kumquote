Lewis makes a surprising claim: friendship is unnecessary — and that is precisely why it matters. Food and shelter keep us alive, but friendship is what makes being alive worthwhile, just as art and philosophy give meaning beyond mere survival. A life reduced to necessities would be efficient and empty. Friendship belongs to the things we choose freely, not because we must but because they enrich us. Its lack of survival value is its glory: friends are not tools for getting ahead, but companions who make the journey itself the reward.
