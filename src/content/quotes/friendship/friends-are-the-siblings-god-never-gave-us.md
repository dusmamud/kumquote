Family is given by birth, but friendship is chosen, and that choice can create bonds as deep as any blood tie. Mencius captures this by calling friends the siblings we were never given: people who stand beside us like family, not because they must, but because they choose to. Chosen bonds carry a special warmth, since they are renewed by loyalty rather than obligation. A true friend knows your history, accepts your faults and stays through difficulty. In that sense, friendship is family by a different road, built freely and kept by care rather than by birth.
