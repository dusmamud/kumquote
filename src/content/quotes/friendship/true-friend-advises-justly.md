William Penn packs a whole philosophy of friendship into one long sentence, and every clause earns its place. A true friend gives without counting, tells the truth even when it stings, helps without being asked, and stands up for us when it costs something. Patience holds the friendship together through rough patches, and constancy keeps it alive through the years. Notice what is missing: there is nothing here about convenience or advantage. True friendship is a character test — for both people. The friend who does all of this is rare, and being that friend is rarer still.
