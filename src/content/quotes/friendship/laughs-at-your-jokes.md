Loyalty is often described in grand terms, but Glasow finds it in small, generous acts. A loyal friend laughs at a weak joke because your delight matters more than the quality of the punchline. The same friend takes a small problem seriously because your worry matters more than the size of the trouble. This is the quiet kindness at the heart of friendship: treating another person's feelings as important, even when the occasion seems minor. True friends do not grade your joys or your sorrows. They simply share them.
