New relationships often need constant talk to avoid awkwardness, but true friendship does not. David Tyson points to a quiet test: can you sit together in silence and still feel at ease? Comfortable silence means trust, acceptance, and a bond that does not need performance. You do not have to entertain each other or fill every pause. Simply being present is enough. Such friendships grow slowly, through shared experiences and honest moments, until words become optional. If you can share silence peacefully with someone, you have found a rare kind of closeness that many people spend a lifetime seeking.
