Friendship works in both directions of fortune. In good times, a joy shared with friends shines brighter than one enjoyed alone; celebration needs witnesses to feel complete. In hard times, friendship divides the burden, so that no one carries the whole weight by themselves. The same friend who doubles our happiness also halves our sorrow. Cicero's line captures the full economy of friendship: it multiplies the good and divides the bad. That is why a friend's presence matters in every season — prosperity is sweeter with company, and adversity is lighter when shared.
