Human friendship always carries a measure of judgement. Even the kindest friend forms opinions, asks awkward questions, and sometimes misunderstands. Animals offer a different kind of companionship: they accept us as we are, without demanding explanations or improvements. A dog does not care about our failures, and a cat does not lecture us about our choices. That is why their friendship feels so restful — it is acceptance without conditions. Eliot's line is not really about animals at all; it is about what we wish all friendship could be.
