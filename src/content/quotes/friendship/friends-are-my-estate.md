An estate is what a person owns and leaves behind. Emily Dickinson owned very little of that. What she owned was friends, and she counted them as her true wealth. It is a striking measure: not what we accumulate, but whom we love and who loves us. Bank accounts do not visit us when we are ill; friends do. Possessions do not remember us; friends do. If we counted wealth the way Dickinson did, most of us would find we are richer than we think — and poorer in the ways that matter if we have neglected our friendships.
