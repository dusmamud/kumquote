An enemy's offense is expected, so forgiving it costs little. A friend's offense cuts deeper, because it breaks trust, and trust was the whole foundation. We expect enemies to hurt us; we expect friends to protect us, so their betrayal feels personal in a way an enemy's never could. That is why forgiving a friend is the harder and more important act. It asks us to weigh years of loyalty against one wound, and to choose the friendship over the hurt. Forgiveness here is not weakness. It is the strength to save something worth saving.
