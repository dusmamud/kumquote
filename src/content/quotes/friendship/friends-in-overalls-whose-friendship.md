Titles and wealth impress strangers, but they rarely reveal character. Edison reminds us that the truest friendships often grow far from ballrooms and boardrooms, among people who work with their hands and speak plainly. A friend in overalls may have little to offer in money or influence, yet offer something far rarer: honesty, loyalty, and a heart without pretence. Judge friends by their steadiness, not their status. The people who stand by you in ordinary clothes on ordinary days are the ones whose friendship no king's favour could ever replace.
