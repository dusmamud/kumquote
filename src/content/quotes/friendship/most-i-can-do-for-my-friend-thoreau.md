Thoreau strips friendship down to its simplest duty: be there. We often feel we must fix a friend's problems, offer brilliant advice, or rescue them from trouble — and when we cannot, we feel useless. But Thoreau says the greatest gift is not the solution; it is the steady presence of someone who stays. Being a friend means showing up, listening without rushing to fix, and standing beside them while they find their own way. If a friend is struggling today, do not worry about having the right answer. Just be their friend. That is enough.
