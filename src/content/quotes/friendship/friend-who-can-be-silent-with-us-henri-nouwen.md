Nouwen describes the rarest kind of friendship: the one that does not try to fix anything. In grief or confusion, most people rush to offer answers, solutions, or cheerful distractions — and the sufferer feels more alone, not less. The true friend stays, silent, tolerating the not-knowing and the powerlessness alongside you. That presence asks for real courage, because doing nothing feels like failing. But it is the deepest care there is: to sit with someone in the dark without demanding they come into the light. If you can be that friend, you have given the greatest gift friendship holds.
