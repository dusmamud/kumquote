Lewis noticed that friendship does not start with introductions or
favours. It starts with recognition — the shock of discovering that
someone else carries the same odd interest, the same private worry,
the same unspoken thought. In that moment, loneliness ends, because
the thing you believed was only yours turns out to be shared.

So if you want more friendship, the move is not to be more
impressive. It is to be more honest about what you actually care
about, even the unfashionable parts. Say the real thing out loud.
The people who answer "You too?" are the ones worth keeping, and
they can only find you if you stop hiding the parts of yourself
that make you findable.
