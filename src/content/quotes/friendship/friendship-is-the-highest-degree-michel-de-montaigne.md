We often rank relationships by intensity: the passionate romance, the dramatic reunion, the grand gesture. Montaigne ranks them differently. Friendship is the highest degree of perfection in society, not because it is dramatic, but because it is steady, honest, and free. Friends choose each other without need or obligation, and that free choice makes the bond the purest form of human connection. A society built on such friendships, on trust freely given and freely returned, is a society at its best. Perfection, in Montaigne's sense, is not grandeur. It is the quiet, daily practice of being good to one another.
