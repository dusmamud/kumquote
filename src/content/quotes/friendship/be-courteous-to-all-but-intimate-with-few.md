Kindness should be generous, but intimacy should be careful. Courtesy costs little and improves every encounter, so there is no reason to withhold it from anyone. Trust, however, is different: it is given in full, and once broken it is hard to repair. Washington's advice is to separate the two — be warm to all, but reserve your confidence for the few who have earned it over time. A friend who has been tested through difficulty, disagreement, and distance is worth more than a dozen charming newcomers. Choose slowly, test honestly, and then trust completely.
