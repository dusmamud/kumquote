Friendship, like everything else, moves with time. Some friends drift away, some pass from our lives, and new ones arrive in their place — not as replacements but as new chapters. The comparison to days is gentle and wise: we do not mourn yesterday forever, because today has its own light. Grieving a lost friendship is natural, yet clinging to what has passed blinds us to the people arriving now. The line invites acceptance rather than bitterness: honour the old friends, welcome the new ones, and trust that each day of friendship, old or new, has its own gift.
