Goldsmith draws a sharp contrast between friendship and love as he sees them. Friendship, at its best, is a fair exchange between equals, where neither person rules and neither begs, and each gives freely without counting the cost. Love, in his darker view, too often becomes a struggle for power, with one side commanding and the other submitting. Whether or not we agree with his view of love, the praise of friendship stands. True friendship needs no master and no servant. It thrives on equality, honesty, and the quiet respect of two people who meet as equals.
