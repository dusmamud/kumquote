Laws, treaties, and institutions can organise society, but they cannot create trust on their own. Woodrow Wilson points to friendship as the deeper bond that holds people, communities, and nations together. When people know each other as friends, they listen more patiently, forgive more easily, and work together more willingly. Friendship crosses borders, cultures, and differences in a way that rules cannot. It begins simply: a shared meal, an honest conversation, a helping hand. If we want a more peaceful world, we should start by building genuine friendships, one person at a time, with patience and respect.
