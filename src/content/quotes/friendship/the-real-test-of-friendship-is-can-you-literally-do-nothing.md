Kennedy's test is delightfully simple: can you do nothing together? Many relationships survive only on activity — dinners, trips, constant entertainment — and collapse into awkwardness when the activity stops. But the deepest friendships need no performance. Two people can sit in a room, read separate books, say almost nothing, and feel completely at ease. That comfort is real trust: neither person fears silence or being boring. If your friendship only works when something is happening, it may be more about the happening than the friend. The best friendships are enjoyable even when nothing is happening at all.
