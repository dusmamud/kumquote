A broken heart feels like a wound that no advice can reach, and the world often offers little more than platitudes. Austen points to the remedy that helps: a friend who stays close. Friendship does not fix a disappointment in love, but it softens the pain by reminding us that we are still known, valued, and wanted. A friend's steady presence restores the sense of worth that rejection takes away. Time heals, but friends make waiting bearable. In the aftermath of lost love, it is usually not a new romance that saves us, but an old friendship that holds us.
