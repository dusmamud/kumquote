The people around us shape us more than we admit. Their habits, values, and standards rub off quietly, day after day, until they become our own. That is why the company we keep is not a small matter — it is one of the main forces forming our character. Good company lifts us; bad company drags us down, often without us noticing. Washington's advice is blunt: solitude is better than corrupting company. Being alone gives us space to think and choose; bad company takes that space away and replaces it with pressure.
