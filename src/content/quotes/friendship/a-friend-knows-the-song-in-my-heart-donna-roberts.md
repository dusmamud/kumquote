Memory fades. We forget birthdays, promises, even the names of songs we once loved. Roberts imagines a friend as someone who keeps the song of our heart when we cannot. A friend knows us so deeply they can remind us who we are when grief, illness, or age has made us forget. That is a rare kind of love: not the loud kind, but the quiet, faithful kind that holds our story for us. We all need someone who remembers our song. And we all, in turn, can be that person for someone else, singing gently when their memory fails.
