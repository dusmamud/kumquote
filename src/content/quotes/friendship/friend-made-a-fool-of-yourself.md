Everyone has moments they wish they could erase: the foolish remark, the bad decision, the public embarrassment. A fair-weather acquaintance will quietly file that moment away as your permanent reputation. A real friend refuses to do that. They saw you at your worst and still know who you are underneath it. Their memory is longer than your mistake, and their judgement is kinder than the crowd's. That is one of friendship's greatest gifts: the freedom to fail without being reduced to the failure. With a true friend, a foolish moment stays a moment, not a verdict.
