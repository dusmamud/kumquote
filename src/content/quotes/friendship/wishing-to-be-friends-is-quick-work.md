Wanting a friend takes a moment; becoming one takes time. Aristotle knew that real friendship cannot be rushed, because trust grows slowly through shared experiences, kept promises, and seasons spent together. Like fruit, friendship needs time to ripen: early enthusiasm is sweet but fragile, while a mature friendship has depth, resilience, and flavour that only years can produce. We should not be discouraged when new friendships feel shallow; ripening is the natural process. Give friendship time, attention, and patience, and it will grow into something lasting and nourishing.
