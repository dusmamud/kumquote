Gardner reminds us that life, unlike a sketch, offers no clean way to undo a stroke. Every decision leaves its mark, and wishing for an eraser only wastes the time you could spend drawing the next line well. But the image is not as bleak as it sounds: artists who cannot erase learn to make each stroke count, and they also learn that a wrong line can become part of a better picture. Draw carefully, yes — but draw boldly too, because hesitation leaves nothing on the page at all.
