Twain connects two fears we usually treat as separate. People who dread death, he suggests, often dread life first — they avoid risk, postpone joy, and keep themselves safe at the cost of never really living. The fear of dying is then just the fear of a life left unused, and no amount of caution can fix that. A full life does not remove death, but it removes the regret that makes death frightening. Live well now, and the end loses its sting.
