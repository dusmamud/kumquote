Schweitzer points to a quiet tragedy that never makes headlines: the slow death of curiosity, courage, and hope inside a person who is still breathing. Bodies age, but the spirit can wither much earlier, worn down by routine, fear, or disappointment. A life can look full on the outside while something essential has gone silent within. The warning is also an invitation. What dies inside us usually dies from neglect, not from age. Tend to the inner life — read, wonder, risk, care — and the years keep their meaning. Staying alive is not the same as staying awake.
