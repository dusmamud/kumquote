Most of our suffering happens before anything actually happens. The mind rehearses disasters, replays embarrassments, and invents outcomes far worse than reality ever delivers. Seneca's point is not that real hardship does not exist, but that imagined hardship usually outweighs it. We pay the pain twice: once in fear, once in fact, when once would have been enough. Learning to separate what is happening from what we fear might happen is one of life's great skills. Face the real problem when it arrives, and refuse to suffer its imaginary versions in advance.
