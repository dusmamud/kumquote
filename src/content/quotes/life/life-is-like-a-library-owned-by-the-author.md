Each of us owns a library, but few of the books in it are ours. Our parents, teachers, friends, and the age we live in wrote most of the volumes on our shelves — the beliefs we never chose, the habits we copied, the fears we inherited. Only a few books carry our own handwriting: the choices we made deliberately, the values we tested ourselves. Fosdick's image asks us to look at the shelves and ask which books we actually wrote. A life becomes our own only when we start writing more of it ourselves.
