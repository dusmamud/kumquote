Anger feels like strength, but it usually makes us weaker. An angry fighter reacts instead of thinking, wastes energy, and makes mistakes an opponent can exploit. The best fighter stays calm, watches clearly, and acts only when the moment is right. This applies far beyond combat: in arguments, negotiations, and difficult decisions, the calm person sees more and chooses better. Strength is not the loudest emotion in the room. It is the quiet control that lets us respond with precision instead of reacting with heat.
