Kierkegaard names the strange condition of being human: we only understand our lives in hindsight, yet we must live them without a preview. Looking back, the detours and disasters often make sense — they taught us, redirected us, or brought us where we needed to be. But in the moment we cannot see that pattern; we can only choose and step forward. The quote asks for two things at once: the courage to act without certainty, and the patience to trust that meaning will arrive later, when we finally look back.
