Change arrives whether we welcome it or not, like weather moving across a landscape. Lao Tzu's counsel is not passivity but clear sight: resisting what is already happening only adds a second layer of suffering to the first. The sorrow we feel often comes less from the change itself than from our argument with it, the insistence that things should have stayed as they were. Letting reality be reality means meeting each moment as it actually is, not as we wish it were. This acceptance frees the energy we spent fighting the inevitable, so we can respond wisely instead.
