We save celebration for big occasions, the wedding, the promotion, the milestone birthday, as if ordinary days do not deserve it. Rivers disagrees. Life is what you celebrate, all of it, even its end. The ordinary Tuesday, the imperfect meal, the friend who makes you laugh, these are the material of a life, and they deserve a toast. Celebration is not denial of the hard parts; it refuses to let them be the whole story. Celebrating the small and the ordinary trains our eyes to notice what is good. A life noticed that way becomes, quietly, a life worth celebrating.
