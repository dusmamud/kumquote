Sandburg's onion is a gentle metaphor for how life reveals itself. We do not understand it all at once; we peel it layer by layer, year by year, and each layer brings something new, sometimes sweet, sometimes stinging. The weeping is part of the process, not a failure of it. Growth often arrives disguised as discomfort, and understanding often costs a few tears. But the peeling is also how we get closer to the heart of things, to what is real beneath the surface. Life asks for patience with its layers.
