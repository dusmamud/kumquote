We inherit stories about who we are — from family, culture, and the expectations of others. These stories can guide us, but they can also confine us, telling us what is possible before we have had a chance to decide for ourselves. Rumi's advice is to refuse the ready-made version and unfold our own myth instead: to live in a way that writes a story only we could have written. That does not mean rejecting everything we were given; it means choosing consciously what to keep and what to outgrow. A life lived from someone else's script is only borrowed.
