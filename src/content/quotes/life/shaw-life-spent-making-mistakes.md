Playing it safe feels honorable because it avoids error, but Shaw argues the opposite: a life of mistakes is the more honorable one. Mistakes are evidence of trying, of reaching beyond what is easy and learning from the result. The person who does nothing makes no mistakes, yet also contributes nothing and learns nothing. Every error carries a lesson that a cautious life never receives, and those lessons, shared with others, become genuinely useful. Better to stumble forward, gathering wisdom as you go, than to stand still in perfect, empty safety.
