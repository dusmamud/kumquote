Seven words, and a warning against a small life. Disraeli does not mean that life is short in years, though it is; he means that a life shrunk by fear, routine, and low expectations wastes whatever time it has. To be small is to play safe, to want little, to avoid the risk of wanting more. But the days pass at the same speed whether we live boldly or timidly. Since the time is limited either way, the only sensible choice is to live large: aim higher, love deeper, and refuse to shrink.
