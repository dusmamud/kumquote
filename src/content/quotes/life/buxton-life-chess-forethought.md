Chess rewards the player who thinks several moves ahead, and life works much the same way. The person who pauses to consider consequences before acting avoids many of the traps that catch the impulsive. Forethought is not fear or overthinking; it is the quiet habit of looking ahead before committing. A few minutes of reflection can save months of repair. Like a good chess player, we do not need to predict everything — we only need to think one or two moves further than our impulses would. That small edge, repeated over a lifetime, decides a great deal.
