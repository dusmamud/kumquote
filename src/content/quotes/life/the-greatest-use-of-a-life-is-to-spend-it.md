A life spent only on itself ends with its owner, but a life spent on something larger keeps working long after. James measures a life by what survives it: the child raised well, the idea that spreads, the kindness that gets passed on. This is not about fame; most lasting things are quiet. The teacher's influence, the parent's example, the neighbour's generosity — all outlive the people who gave them. Spend your years on something that will still be standing when you are gone, and your life becomes larger than its length.
