A new language feels impossible at first: the sounds are strange, the rules are confusing, and every sentence comes out wrong. Life is like that, Morley suggests. We arrive without a phrasebook, mispronouncing everything, misunderstanding the customs, and learning mostly by embarrassment. The temptation is to stop speaking, to stay quiet and safe. But no one learns a language by silence. Fluency comes only from trying, failing, and trying again, until the strange sounds slowly become our own. Life rewards the same courage. Speak it badly, speak it often, and one day you will find you are speaking it well.
