Lao Tzu describes change as the natural weather of life, not an interruption to it. Seasons turn, people grow, plans break — none of this is a mistake in the design. Sorrow arrives, he argues, not from change itself but from our resistance to it: gripping the old season, demanding the past return, refusing the new shape of things. When change comes, let it move through you like weather. The sorrow fades the moment you stop demanding that life stay still.
