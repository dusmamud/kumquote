The ideal life is often pictured as wealth, fame, or constant excitement, but Twain reduces it to three quiet things. Good friends give us belonging and laughter, the kind of company that makes ordinary days feel full. Good books give us new worlds, ideas, and the comfort of a mind at work. And a sleepy conscience — the peace of knowing we have lived honestly — lets us rest without regret. None of these can be bought in a hurry, and none of them shout. Together they describe a life that is rich in the ways that actually last.
