Cocteau turns life sideways with a single strange image. A fall is usually vertical, a sudden drop, but a horizontal fall is slow, continuous, and easy to ignore. We are falling all our lives, from birth toward death, but because the motion is level and steady, we mistake it for standing still. The quote wakes us to that quiet descent. Time passes even on the most ordinary days, and the fall does not pause while we decide how to live. There is melancholy in the image, but also urgency: if life is a fall, then every moment matters.
