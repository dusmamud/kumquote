These two sentences hold a tension that makes a whole philosophy. Living as if we might die tomorrow pushes us to stop postponing what matters — the apology, the dream, the kindness, the honest conversation — because tomorrow is never guaranteed. Learning as if we might live forever pulls the other way, urging patience, curiosity, and steady growth without the panic of a deadline. Together they balance urgency with depth: act now on what matters, and keep learning as though there is always more time to grow. A life lived both ways is full and unhurried at once.
