Capote jokes that life starts well enough — childhood and youth carry energy, romance, and surprise — but the final act often disappoints: ageing, loss, and the slow fading of old dreams. The humour hides a warning. If we expect the ending to write itself, it probably will, and not kindly. The third act is ours to shape while we still can: forgiving old quarrels, passing on what we know, and finding new pleasures in smaller things. A play cannot be rewritten, but a life can be redirected at any age. Make the last act worth staying for.
