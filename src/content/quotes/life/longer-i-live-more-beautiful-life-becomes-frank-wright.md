Wright, who designed boldly into old age, answers the common fear that life fades as the years pile up. Beauty, for him, was not the freshness of youth but something that deepens with attention: the more you live, the more you notice — light, craft, kindness, the small patterns of a day. The quote is an invitation to keep looking rather than keep counting years. If life seems less beautiful than it once did, the problem may not be the years but the attention. Look longer, and the beauty returns.
