Twain traces the fear of death to its real source: a life only half lived. The person who has held back, postponed joy, avoided risk, and left dreams unattempted clings to the future because the past feels unfinished. But the person who has lived fully, who has loved, tried, failed, laughed, and used their days, can meet the end without panic, because there is nothing essential left undone. The quote is not morbid; it is a call to live now, with both hands. Death frightens us less when life has been spent rather than hoarded.
