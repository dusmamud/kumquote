Injustice is easy to ignore when it happens to someone else, somewhere else. It feels distant, and minding our own business feels reasonable. But justice is not a local possession; it is a shared condition. When unfairness is tolerated in one place, it sets a precedent that can reach everywhere, including our own door. We are caught in a single fabric of mutuality, as King put it, and what affects one directly affects all indirectly. Standing against injustice elsewhere is therefore not charity but self-interest of the wisest kind.
