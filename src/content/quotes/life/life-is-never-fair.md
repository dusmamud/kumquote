Fairness is a hope, not a guarantee, and Wilde finds a strange mercy in that. If life gave everyone exactly what they deserved, many of us would have less to be grateful for, because our blessings often exceed our merits. Accepting that life is uneven frees us from the exhausting work of keeping score and comparing lots. Instead of demanding a fair world, we can practise gratitude for what we have and kindness toward those with less. Life's unfairness, seen this way, becomes a reason for generosity rather than complaint.
