Kierkegaard corrects a modern habit: treating life like a puzzle with one correct answer. A problem demands a solution, a deadline, and a feeling of being finished — and life offers none of those. It offers instead moments to be lived: conversations, meals, walks, work done well, grief endured honestly. Approached as a problem, every unsolved part feels like failure; approached as an experience, the same parts enrich the story. Stop waiting for the solution that will finally make everything clear. The meaning is not at the end of the puzzle. It is in the living itself.
