Yeats captures the quiet irony of a life spent waiting. We prepare for the perfect job, the perfect moment, the perfect version of ourselves — and the arrival keeps slipping away. Preparation feels safe because it promises a future payoff, but years of rehearsal can become a substitute for living. The line is not an argument against planning; it is a warning against postponing life itself. The thing we are preparing for may never come, but today is already here. Live in the rehearsal too, or the performance may never begin.
