Mencken's cynical wit cuts through romantic idealism with a grin. He suggests that love makes us believe our beloved is uniquely different from everyone else, when in truth the feeling is universal and the person is simply the one we have chosen. Yet the delusion is part of love's charm: believing someone is one of a kind makes devotion feel meaningful. Whether we take the line as a joke or a warning, it reminds us that love is as much about what we project onto another person as about who they actually are. The delusion, happily, is one we enjoy.
