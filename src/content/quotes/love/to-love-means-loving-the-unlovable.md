Easy love is not the test. The real measure of love is what happens when the other person is difficult, hurtful, or undeserving — when there is no reward for staying. Chesterton extends the same logic to forgiveness, faith, and hope: each virtue only proves itself in the hardest case. A love that stops at the unlovable was never really love, only preference. The point is not to excuse harm or pretend difficulty away, but to recognise that love, forgiveness, faith, and hope earn their names precisely when they cost us something. That is when they are real.
