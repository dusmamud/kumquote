Disappointment hurts in proportion to how much we care. A stranger's failure barely touches us, but the failure of someone we love cuts deep, because love raises the stakes. King turns that pain into proof: deep disappointment can only exist where deep love exists first. The hurt is not a sign that love was a mistake; it is the measure of how real the love was. To avoid disappointment entirely, we would have to stop loving deeply, and that would cost far more. Love deeply, and accept that the depth of caring is also the depth of the wound.
