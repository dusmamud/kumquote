Barrymore jokes about the brief, golden period when love is all imagination. Meeting someone beautiful fills the mind with perfection, and for a delightful interval, the beloved can do no wrong. Then reality arrives: habits, flaws, and the ordinary truth of another human being, here compared, unkindly and hilariously, to a haddock. The humor hides a real point. Early love is partly invention, and lasting love begins when the interval ends and we choose the real person anyway. The haddock is not the end of love; it is where love gets honest.
