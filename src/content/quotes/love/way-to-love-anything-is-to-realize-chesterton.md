Chesterton points out a strange habit of the heart: we treasure things most clearly when we imagine them gone. While something feels permanent, we rush past it, assume it, and postpone our attention to it. The thought of losing it snaps us awake — suddenly the ordinary morning, the familiar voice, the everyday presence looks precious. Loss does not have to happen for the lesson to work; imagining it is enough. Hold what you love as though it were borrowed, not owned, and your attention will sharpen on its own. Love grows brightest in the light of its own fragility.
