Angelou compares love to a virus, and the comparison is truer than it sounds. Love arrives uninvited, ignores our plans, and spreads through a life before we notice the symptoms. It can happen to anybody — the young, the old, the careful, the cynical — at any time, including the times we swore it never would. We cannot vaccinate ourselves against it, and we cannot schedule it. The only choice is what we do once it arrives. Love is democratic in the most literal sense: nobody is immune, and nobody is excluded.
