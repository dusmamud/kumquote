Johnson enjoys the paradox: love makes fools wise and wise men foolish, and both are right. The fool in love suddenly understands things no lecture could teach — patience, sacrifice, attention to another's happiness. The wise person, meanwhile, finds their careful reasoning undone by a feeling that refuses to be argued away. Love does not respect the categories we build for it. It humbles the clever and enlightens the simple, often in the same week. Both belong to it, and both are worth honouring.
