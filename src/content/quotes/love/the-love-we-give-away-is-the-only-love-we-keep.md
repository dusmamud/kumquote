Love behaves unlike anything else we own: hoarding it makes it shrink, and giving it away makes it grow. Hubbard's paradox is that the love we keep is exactly the love we give. A love guarded out of fear withers into possessiveness, while a love offered freely returns in forms we never expected — in loyalty, in warmth, in the quiet knowledge that we mattered to someone. Giving does not empty the giver; it proves the love was real. What we give away is the only part that stays with us.
