The deepest happiness is not wealth or success but the quiet certainty that someone loves you as you truly are. Not for your achievements or your appearance, but for yourself, flaws included. To be loved in spite of your faults is to be fully known and still accepted, and there is no greater comfort than that. It frees you from pretending and lets you rest in the knowledge that you are enough. Hugo names this conviction as life's greatest happiness, because once you have it, everything else in life feels lighter and safer.
