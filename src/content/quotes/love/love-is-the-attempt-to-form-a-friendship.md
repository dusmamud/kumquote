Cicero's definition is quietly radical: love begins not with passion but with friendship. Beauty may catch the eye, but friendship — shared jokes, honest talk, trust built over time — turns attraction into something lasting. Passion alone burns brightly and fades; friendship gives love a foundation that survives bad days and boredom. This is why the strongest romances grow from genuine liking: two people who enjoy each other's company and choose each other daily. Love inspired by beauty may start the story, but only friendship can keep it going. Seek first a friend, and love has somewhere solid to stand.
