Cummings refuses to measure love in parts. It is not one good thing among many, not a slice of a well-rounded life; it is the whole, and then more than the whole, overflowing every boundary we try to set for it. Love changes the arithmetic of a life: everything added to it becomes more, and everything without it feels less. The line is short because it needs no argument. Anyone who has loved deeply knows the feeling of a world reorganized around a single person, where the ordinary suddenly glows. Love is not an ingredient.
