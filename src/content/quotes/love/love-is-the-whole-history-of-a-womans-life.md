De Staël's observation, written in a very different century, still stings because it names a real imbalance. For many women of her time, love was the central story — the event around which a whole life was arranged — while for men it was one chapter among many. Her line asks us to notice whose life gets organized around love and whose does not. A fair relationship should not make one person's story smaller than the other's. Love, at its best, is a shared history — a whole life written together, not an episode in someone else's plot.
