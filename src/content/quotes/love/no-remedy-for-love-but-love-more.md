Thoreau offers the only cure he trusts for the ache of love: more love. When love wounds us — through loss, disappointment, or distance — our instinct is to guard ourselves and love less. He argues the opposite: the remedy is not withdrawal but a deeper, braver love, directed at life, at others, and at ourselves. Loving more does not erase the pain, but it keeps the heart open and moving. A closed heart may avoid new hurt, yet it also loses the very thing that makes life worth living.
