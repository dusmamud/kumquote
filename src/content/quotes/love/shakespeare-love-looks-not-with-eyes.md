We imagine love as something we see — a face, a gesture, a moment — but Shakespeare insists it happens deeper than sight. The eyes can be fooled by beauty or blinded by flaws, while the mind sees the whole person: their character and kindness. That is why love often surprises onlookers, who judge by appearances, while the lovers themselves understand exactly what they see in each other. True love is a way of perceiving, not merely a reaction to looks. It looks with attention, memory, and imagination, and finds beauty where the eyes alone would miss it.
