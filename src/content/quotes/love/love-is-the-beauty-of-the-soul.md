Physical beauty fades and changes, but Augustine points to a beauty that grows with character: the beauty of a soul shaped by love. A loving soul shows itself in patience, generosity, mercy and attention to others, qualities that make a person radiant in a way appearance cannot. This beauty is not given at birth; it is cultivated through choices, especially loving when it is difficult. The line also reverses how beauty is usually judged. Instead of asking whether someone looks beautiful, it asks whether their soul is beautiful.
