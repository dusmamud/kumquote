Shaw's wit hides a real insight: love is not a calm, rational ranking of qualities, but a magnificent overstatement. To the lover, one person stands apart from the whole world, and no list of virtues can explain why. The exaggeration is the point — love makes the ordinary extraordinary, and the beloved becomes unlike anyone else, not because they are flawless but because they are seen through devoted eyes. There is honesty in the joke, too: love is partly a delightful illusion, and its power comes from how fully we believe in the difference it creates.
