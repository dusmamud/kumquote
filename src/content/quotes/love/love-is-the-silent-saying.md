Love does not always announce itself with grand speeches. Sometimes it lives quietly in the way one name keeps returning to the mind, unbidden, through the day. McLaughlin captures the intimacy of that repetition: to love someone is to carry their name like a small prayer, spoken silently in thought and aloud in hope. The saying and saying is not obsession but devotion, a rhythm of attention that turns an ordinary name into something sacred. Real love often works this way, in quiet constancy rather than loud declaration.
