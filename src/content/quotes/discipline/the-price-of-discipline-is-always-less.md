Discipline asks for payment in advance: time, effort and discomfort, spent before any reward appears. Regret collects its payment later, with interest, in the form of missed chances and what might have been. Qubein weighs the two costs and finds discipline cheaper every time. The price of discipline is known and limited; the pain of regret is open-ended and grows with time. This comparison turns discipline from a burden into a bargain. Paying a small, certain price now is wiser than risking a large, uncertain one later. The wise choose the cost they can measure.
