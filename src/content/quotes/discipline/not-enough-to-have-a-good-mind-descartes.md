Descartes, who trusted reason above all, warns that a good mind is only raw material. Plenty of clever people waste their gifts on distraction, argument for its own sake, or clever excuses — the mind is sharp, but it is pointed nowhere. Using it well is the discipline: choosing what to think about, questioning your own conclusions, and directing thought toward what matters instead of what merely entertains. The main thing, as he says, is the use. Point your mind at a worthy problem today, and keep it there until something real comes of it.
