No teacher, book, or lecture can replace the lessons learned by doing. Practice reveals what theory hides: where our understanding is shaky, where our habits fail, and what actually works under real conditions. Each repetition teaches something new, correcting small errors and building confidence that reading alone cannot provide. Publilius Syrus, writing two thousand years ago, understood a truth that modern science confirms: we learn best by trying, failing, and trying again. Instruction points the way, but only practice walks it, and the doing itself is the greatest teacher of all.
