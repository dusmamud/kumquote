Talent on its own is raw material, full of promise but unfinished. Smith compares discipline to fire, because fire does not merely warm metal; it changes what the metal is. Regular practice, honest feedback, and the willingness to repeat unglamorous work transform natural ability into reliable skill. Without that refining process, talent stays impressive in flashes and unreliable when it matters. With it, ability becomes something others can count on. The image reminds us that discipline is not punishment; it is the process that turns what we could do into what we actually do, consistently and well.
