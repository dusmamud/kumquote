We usually treat sorrow as something that happens to us and pleasure as something we chase, but Wilde gives the self-mastered person command over both. The one who governs their own mind is not a prisoner of moods: they can let a sorrow go, and create joy rather than waiting for it to arrive. Self-mastery is not cold control but freedom — choosing our response instead of being dragged by every feeling. That power is available to anyone willing to practice it, one choice at a time, until the mind becomes a servant rather than a master.
