Conquering others wins applause, but conquering ourselves wins freedom. Our worst opponent is rarely an external rival; it is the impulse to quit, the appetite for comfort, and the fear that talks us out of trying. These enemies live inside us, and no one else can defeat them for us. Every other victory depends on this one, because a person who cannot govern their own desires will eventually lose whatever they win. Self-conquest is quiet and unglamorous — it happens in small daily choices, not on a stage.
