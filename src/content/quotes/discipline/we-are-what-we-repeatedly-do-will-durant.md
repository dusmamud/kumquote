Durant wrote this as a summary of Aristotle, and it has outlived
most self-help books because it removes the drama from excellence.
You do not become excellent by one heroic effort, a single morning
of motivation, or a perfect week. You become what you repeat on
ordinary days, when nobody is watching and nothing feels special.

That is both bad news and good news. Bad, because a single good day
changes nothing. Good, because the fix is small and boring: choose
one action, shrink it until it is embarrassingly easy, and do it
every day. Excellence is not a personality trait. It is a calendar
full of small repetitions, and the person who repeats them becomes
the person the quote describes.
