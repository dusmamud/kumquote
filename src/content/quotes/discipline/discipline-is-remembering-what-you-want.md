Discipline is often pictured as iron willpower, but Campbell offers a gentler and truer definition: it is simply remembering what you want. Temptation wins when the goal fades from view; a craving, a distraction, or a shortcut looks appealing only in the moment the larger purpose is forgotten. Keep your goal vivid and close, and the small choices become easier, because you can see what each one costs. Discipline is not a battle against yourself. It is a memory exercise: remember what you want most, and let that memory decide.
