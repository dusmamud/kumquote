A bad habit is easy to start and hard to stop, because each repetition makes it stronger. Franklin's advice is to act early, before the habit takes root. Refusing the first cigarette or the first skipped workout costs little, but breaking the pattern later costs far more willpower and pain. Prevention is a quiet form of discipline: noticing the small choices that lead somewhere you do not want to go, and stepping aside before the path gets steep. The wise person does not test their strength against a habit; they simply refuse to build it.
