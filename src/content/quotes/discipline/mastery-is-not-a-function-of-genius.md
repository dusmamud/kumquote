It is comforting to believe that masters are born different, because that belief excuses us from the long work they did. Greene removes that excuse: mastery is not a gift handed to a lucky few, but the result of time and intense focus applied to one field over years. Talent may set the starting point, but attention decides the destination. Anyone willing to stay with a subject long enough, studying it deeply instead of skimming, can reach a level most people assume is reserved for geniuses. The secret of mastery is unglamorous and available to all: sustained focus, repeated daily.
