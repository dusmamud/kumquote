Greatness of character is rarely the result of a single dramatic decision. Kleiser attributes it to constant self-discipline and self-control, practised quietly over time. Character is built in the small moments when nobody is watching: keeping a promise, resisting a shortcut, doing the right thing when the easy thing is tempting. Each act of self-control strengthens the next one, like a muscle growing with use. The word constant matters, because occasional discipline produces occasional results. Steady practice, day after day, is what turns good intentions into a trustworthy character that others can rely on.
