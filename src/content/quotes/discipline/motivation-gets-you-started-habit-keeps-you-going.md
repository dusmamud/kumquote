Motivation is useful, but it is unreliable. It rises with excitement and fades with tiredness, stress, or boredom. Jim Ryun, an Olympic runner, knew that races are not won on motivated days alone; they are won by habits built on ordinary days. A habit removes the daily debate about whether to act. You simply do the work because it is what you do at that time. Build systems that make good choices easy: a fixed time, a prepared space, a small first step. Motivation may start the journey, but habit is what carries you faithfully to the finish.
