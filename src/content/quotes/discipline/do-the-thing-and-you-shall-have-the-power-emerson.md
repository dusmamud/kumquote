We wait to feel ready before we act, as if power must come before we begin. Emerson reverses the order. Do the thing, and you shall have the power. The power does not come first; it comes from doing. The first step is clumsy, the second steadier, and by the tenth we wonder why we waited. Action is the teacher that no amount of thinking can replace. We do not need permission, preparation, or perfect conditions. We need only to begin. Do the thing, and the power to do it well will follow, as surely as morning follows night.
