Lao Tzu draws a line between two kinds of power. Mastering others — winning arguments, giving orders, holding authority — is a kind of strength, but it depends on other people and fades when they resist. Mastering yourself is deeper and more lasting: governing your anger, your impulses, and your fears. No one can take that power from you, and it works in every situation, not only when you are in charge. True power, the quote suggests, is not control over the world but command over oneself.
