Emmons warns that habit is a powerful servant that quietly becomes a master. Good habits — early rising, honest work, regular exercise — serve us without asking for thanks; they carry us through days when willpower is low. But bad habits demand the same obedience, and they collect their payment in wasted years. The danger is that habits form silently, one small repetition at a time, until one day we obey them without choosing. The choice is never whether habits will rule us, only which habits get the job. Choose them deliberately, or they will choose themselves.
