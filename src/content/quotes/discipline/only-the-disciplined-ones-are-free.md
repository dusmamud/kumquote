Eliud Kipchoge, the marathon great, turns the usual idea of freedom upside down. We often think freedom means doing whatever we feel like, whenever we feel like it. But moods are unreliable masters: they tell us to skip training, quit early, and choose comfort. The disciplined person is free because their choices are their own, not dictated by the feeling of the moment. Kipchoge's own career proves it — years of early mornings and strict routines bought him the freedom to run faster than anyone in history. Discipline is not a cage; it is the key.
