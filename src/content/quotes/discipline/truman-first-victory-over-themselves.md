Truman noticed a pattern across the lives of people who achieved great things: before they won any public battle, they first won a private one. They mastered their temper, their appetites, their fear and their urge to quit. Nobody applauds that first victory, because it happens quietly, inside a single person, long before any medal or headline. Yet without it, every later success rests on shaky ground. The lesson is practical rather than grand: if you want to lead, build or endure, start by governing yourself. Conquer the small daily battles first.
