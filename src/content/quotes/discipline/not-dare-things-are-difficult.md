We usually get the order wrong. We tell ourselves a task is too hard, so we avoid it — and the avoidance makes it feel even harder. Seneca reverses the logic: things seem difficult because we refuse to dare, not the other way around. The moment we take the first step, the difficulty shrinks. A feared conversation, a hard workout, a project we keep postponing — all of them are smaller once we begin. Courage does not wait for difficulty to disappear. It acts first, and difficulty follows it down to size.
