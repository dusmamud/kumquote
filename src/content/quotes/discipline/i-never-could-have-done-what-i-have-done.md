Charles Dickens credits his vast body of work not to flashes of genius, but to unglamorous habits. Punctuality kept his days from leaking away; order kept his plots, papers, and promises straight; diligence kept him writing when inspiration was absent. Talent opens the door, yet it is routine that walks through it every single day. We often admire great achievements and forget the boring machinery behind them — the same desk, the same hour, the same steady effort. Dickens's secret was no secret at all: show up on time, keep things in order, and keep working.
