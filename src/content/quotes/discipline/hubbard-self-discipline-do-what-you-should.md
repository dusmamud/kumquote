Most of us know what we should do; the hard part is doing it when we do not feel like it. Hubbard's definition strips discipline of mystery: it is simply the ability to act on what we know, on schedule, regardless of mood. Feelings are unreliable guides — they change with weather, sleep, and circumstance — but a decision made in advance can hold steady. Discipline is the bridge between knowing and doing. When we stop waiting to feel motivated and start acting because it is time, we discover that action itself often creates the motivation we were waiting for.
