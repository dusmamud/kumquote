Discipline looks hard from the outside, yet Pavlina claims it makes life easier, and experience supports him. Undisciplined living creates constant small crises: missed deadlines, broken promises, cluttered schedules, and regrets that pile up. Discipline replaces that chaos with habits that run smoothly once established. The effort is front-loaded; the ease arrives later. A disciplined person spends less energy fighting fires and more energy on what matters. Over time, good habits become automatic, and the life they produce feels lighter. Ease is not the absence of effort, but the reward of effort invested early.
