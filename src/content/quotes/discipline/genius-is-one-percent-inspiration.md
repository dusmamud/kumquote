Great ideas are common; the work to realise them is rare. Edison's famous ratio reminds us that inspiration is only the beginning, a single percent of the journey, while the other ninety-nine percent is the sweat of testing, failing, revising, and trying again. His own light bulb took thousands of attempts, and each one was perspiration, not inspiration. People who wait for a brilliant idea miss the point: the idea matters far less than the discipline to develop it. Genius is not a gift of sudden insight but the habit of working long after the inspiration has faded.
