Grand plans can be seductive because they feel important without requiring action. Peter Marshall reminds us that a small deed actually completed matters more than a great deed only imagined. One phone call made, one page written, one apology offered, these quiet actions change real lives. Planning has its place, but it should serve action, not replace it. If a task feels too large, shrink it until you can do it today. Done is better than perfect, and small deeds repeated daily build a life of genuine usefulness. Start small, finish what you start, and let the results speak.
