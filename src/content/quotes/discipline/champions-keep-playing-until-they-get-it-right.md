The difference between a champion and everyone else is not talent but the willingness to keep practising after others have stopped. Most people practise until they get something right once; champions practise until they cannot get it wrong. That extra repetition, done when no one is watching, is what turns skill into reliability under pressure. Billie Jean King knew that mastery is built in the unglamorous hours of repetition, not in the spotlight of competition. The lesson applies far beyond sport: whatever we want to master, we must keep going until getting it right becomes our habit.
