Henley wrote these lines while battling illness and amputation, which is why they carry such weight. Circumstance can take almost everything — health, comfort, freedom — but it cannot take the inner decision about how we respond. To be master of our fate is not to control what happens to us; it is to refuse to let what happens decide who we are. The captain does not calm the sea; he steers through it. That distinction matters, because it places responsibility where it belongs: not on the storm, but on the person holding the wheel.
