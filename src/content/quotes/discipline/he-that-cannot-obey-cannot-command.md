Leadership is often imagined as giving orders, but Franklin knew it begins with taking them. A person who has never learned to follow instructions, respect rules, or submit to a discipline they did not choose will not know how to lead others fairly. Obedience teaches humility, patience, and the reality that every role has limits. Those lessons are what make authority trustworthy. The best commanders, managers, and parents are usually people who first learned to serve well. If you cannot obey, your commands will be hollow, because you are asking others to do what you yourself refused to learn.
