Dickens wrote some of the longest, most beloved novels in English — and he credits the achievement not to genius but to three plain habits: punctuality, order, and diligence. Be on time. Keep things in order. Work steadily. Add one more habit: concentrate on one subject at a time. It is an unglamorous formula, and that is the point. Great work is rarely the product of inspiration alone; it is the product of ordinary habits practiced for years. Anyone can adopt them. The habits are simple. Keeping them is the hard, rewarding part.
