John Dryden describes a quiet handover of power. At first, every habit is a choice — we decide to run in the morning, to read before bed, to check our phone at dinner. Repeated often enough, the choice disappears and the habit decides for us. That is why small routines matter so much: the person who trains daily does not wrestle with willpower each morning; the habit carries them. The danger runs the other way too — careless habits, once formed, quietly shape a life. Choose your habits carefully, because sooner or later they will be choosing for you.
