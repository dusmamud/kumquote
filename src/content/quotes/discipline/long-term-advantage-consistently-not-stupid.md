Most people chase brilliance, trying to be smarter than everyone else. Munger suggests the opposite strategy: simply avoid being stupid, consistently, over a long time. That means refusing obvious mistakes, resisting impulses, checking your work, and staying away from risks you do not understand. It is far less glamorous than genius, but it compounds. A person who rarely makes big mistakes will outperform a brilliant person who makes them regularly. Discipline is not about shining; it is about not stumbling. Be consistently not stupid, and the long term will take care of itself.
