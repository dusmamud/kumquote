Failure is information, but only if we are willing to receive it. Many people avoid it by playing safe, blaming others, or pretending it did not happen — and in doing so, they protect their pride while blocking their own growth. Accepting failure means looking at it honestly: what went wrong, what was in our control, and what must change next time. That honesty is uncomfortable, which is why so few practise it. But every great athlete, artist, and builder has the same habit: they treat failure as a coach, not an enemy.
