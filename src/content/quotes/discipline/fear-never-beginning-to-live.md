Death is certain and largely out of our hands, yet it is what we fear most. Marcus Aurelius points to a quieter fear we should take seriously instead: the fear of reaching the end having never truly lived. A life spent only avoiding risk, pleasing others, and postponing dreams is a life never begun. Beginning to live takes discipline — choosing what matters, acting on it, and refusing to drift. The years will pass either way. The question is whether we will have used them. Do not fear the end; fear the unlived middle.
