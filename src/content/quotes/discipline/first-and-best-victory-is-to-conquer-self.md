We often measure victory by defeating others, winning competitions, or gaining recognition. Plato points to a harder and more important victory: conquering yourself. Mastering your impulses, fears, laziness, and anger is the foundation for every other achievement. A person who cannot control themselves will be controlled by moods, habits, and circumstances. Self-conquest does not mean harshness; it means honest awareness and steady practice. Each time you choose patience over anger, effort over comfort, or truth over convenience, you win that first victory. Conquer yourself first, and the outer victories will follow more easily.
