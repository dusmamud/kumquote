Commanding others looks like strength, but it is the easier victory: armies, titles, and wealth can all be won without ever facing yourself. Mastering yourself is the harder conquest, because the enemy is inside — your temper, your appetites, your fears, your excuses. No one else can fight that battle for you, and no applause rewards it. Yet the person who rules their own impulses is freer than any ruler: they cannot be provoked, bribed, or frightened into acting against their judgement. Power over others is borrowed and temporary.
