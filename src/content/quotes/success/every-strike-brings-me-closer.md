Ruth reframes the strikeout, the most visible failure in baseball, as progress rather than defeat. Every swing that misses still teaches the batter something: the speed of the pitch, the movement of the ball, the timing that needs adjusting. Success, in this view, is not the absence of failure but the accumulation of attempts, each one narrowing the distance to the next breakthrough. The strike does not push the goal further away; it pulls the hitter closer, because every miss carries information that a cautious non-swing never provides. The failed launch and the rejected proposal are not wasted motion.
