Mistakes feel like proof of failure, but Carnegie turns them into raw material for success. A setback reveals what did not work, and that knowledge is worth more than a lucky win. The successful person does not deny the error or repeat it blindly; they study it, adjust their approach, and try again differently. Each attempt becomes smarter because the last one taught something. Progress is rarely a straight line. It is a cycle of trying, learning, and improving, and those who keep learning from their mistakes eventually move further than those who never risk one.
