Morley offers a definition of success that ignores money and applause. If you can spend your days as you choose — doing work you care about, with people you love — then you have already succeeded, whatever your bank balance says. Most definitions hand the verdict to other people: the boss, the neighbours, the audience. His version hands it back to you. The question is not how much you have earned, but how much of your life is actually yours. A modest life lived on your own terms is a richer success than a grand life lived to please everyone else.
