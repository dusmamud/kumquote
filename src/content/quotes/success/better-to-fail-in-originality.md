Copying others may bring easy success, but it is a hollow kind, borrowed rather than earned. Melville argues that an original failure is worth more than an imitated success, because the failure belongs to us: it carries our ideas, our risks, and our learning. Imitation teaches us only how to follow, while originality teaches us how to think, create, and grow. The world remembers original voices long after imitators are forgotten. Even when originality fails, it fails forward, leaving us wiser and more ourselves than any safe copy ever could.
