Missing a high target feels like failure, so we protect ourselves by aiming low — and then we hit the small target and call it success. The real danger is subtler: a low aim guarantees a small life. We spend our best years achieving things that never truly challenged us, mistaking comfort for accomplishment. Aiming high carries the risk of falling short, but even the miss stretches us further than a safe hit ever would. Better to reach for something great and land partway there than to succeed completely at something that asked nothing of us.
