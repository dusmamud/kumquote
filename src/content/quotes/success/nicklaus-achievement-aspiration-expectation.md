We tend to credit achievement to talent or luck, but this line points to something quieter: the standard we set for ourselves. People who keep raising what they expect of themselves also keep raising what they can do, because expectations shape effort and practice. A low bar produces comfortable results; a higher bar forces new learning. The key word is steadily — not one reckless leap, but a gradual lifting of the standard as each level is mastered. Achievement, then, is less about a single breakthrough and more about refusing to settle at the level we have already reached.
