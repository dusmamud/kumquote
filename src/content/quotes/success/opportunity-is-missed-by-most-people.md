Edison punctures the fantasy that opportunity arrives wearing a suit and carrying a formal invitation. Real opportunity usually shows up looking like effort: a difficult task, an unglamorous job, a problem nobody else wants. Most people walk past it because they were expecting something easier, something that announces itself as a golden chance. But the disguise is the point. The work itself is the opportunity, and those willing to put on the overalls get to keep what others overlooked. Success often belongs not to the most talented but to the most willing to do the unglamorous part.
