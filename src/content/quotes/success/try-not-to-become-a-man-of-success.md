Success measured only by wealth, status, or applause can leave a person empty. Einstein asks for a deeper measure: value. A person of value solves real problems, helps others grow, keeps promises, and leaves things better than they found them. Ironically, such people often achieve lasting success, because trust and usefulness attract opportunities that self-promotion cannot. Chasing success first can lead to shortcuts and compromise, while serving with integrity builds a reputation that endures. Ask yourself each day not only what you gained, but what you contributed. That question points toward a richer and more lasting kind of success.
