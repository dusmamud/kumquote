We are taught to measure success by what we get — the title, the money, the prize. But the getting is brief, and once it arrives, it quickly becomes ordinary. The doing is where life actually happens: the long effort, the learning, the daily discipline of trying. A person who only values the triumph will feel empty between prizes, always waiting for the next one. A person who values the doing finds meaning in the work itself, whether or not it wins. That is a steadier kind of success, one that cannot be taken away by a single result.
