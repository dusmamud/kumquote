Wells measures success not against other people but against our own possibility. The ratio he describes compares what we actually did with what we might have been, and that gap is the honest score. A person who achieved modest things from difficult beginnings may score higher than someone who achieved great things with every advantage. The quote refuses the league tables and the applause, asking instead whether we lived up to our own capacity. It is a private, demanding standard, but also a forgiving one: it honors effort against circumstance.
