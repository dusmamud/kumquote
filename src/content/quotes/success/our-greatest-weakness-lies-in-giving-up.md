Edison, whose laboratory was built on thousands of failed experiments, names giving up as the only weakness that truly ends a pursuit. Failure is recoverable; surrender is not. The quote's power lies in its modesty: success may require only one more attempt, the one made after the moment when quitting feels most reasonable. Nobody can know in advance which attempt will be the one that works, and that uncertainty is precisely why persistence matters. The person who tries once more has a chance; the person who stops has none.
