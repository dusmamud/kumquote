Every discovery begins as a guess that has not yet been proven. Before the evidence exists, someone has to imagine what might be true and risk being wrong. Caution alone never finds anything new; it only protects what is already known. The bold guess is the first step — a hypothesis, a hunch, a leap that says the world might work differently than we assumed. Most guesses fail, and that is expected. But without the willingness to guess boldly, there is nothing to test, nothing to refine, and nothing to discover.
