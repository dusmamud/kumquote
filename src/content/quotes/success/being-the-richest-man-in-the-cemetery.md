Wealth is a poor measure of a life well lived, because no one carries it beyond the grave. What remains is the work — what you made, whom you helped, and whether it was wonderful enough to be proud of. This reframes success entirely: not as accumulation but as contribution, not as the size of the fortune but as the quality of the day's work. Chasing money alone leaves a hollow feeling even when it succeeds, while doing something wonderful fills the day with purpose.
