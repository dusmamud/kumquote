Wilson's punchline works because it catches a familiar excuse in the act. When others succeed, it is tempting to call it luck, which quietly protects our pride: if it was luck, our own failure was not our fault. But the second sentence turns the joke around — ask a failure, and of course they will say it was luck, because that is the story failures tell. Successful people usually tell a different story, about work, timing, and persistence. Listen to the people who made their own luck, and you will hear far less about fortune.
