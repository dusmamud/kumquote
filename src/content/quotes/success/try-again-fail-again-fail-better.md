Three short sentences, and a complete philosophy of persistence. Try, fail, and then try again, but better. The quote refuses to treat failure as a reason to stop; it treats failure as the material of progress. Failing better means each attempt carries a lesson from the last one, so the next failure is a smarter, more interesting failure. Over time, those improved failures add up to something real. This is how artists, scientists, and builders actually work: not by avoiding mistakes, but by making better ones until the work finally succeeds.
