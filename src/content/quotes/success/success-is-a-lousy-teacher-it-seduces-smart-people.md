Winning feels like proof that every decision was right, and that feeling is dangerous. Gates warns that success teaches badly because it hides mistakes, flatters judgment and quietly suggests that failure is something that happens to other people. Smart people are especially vulnerable, since a strong record makes overconfidence feel reasonable. The antidote is to study victories as carefully as defeats, asking what nearly went wrong and what luck contributed. Staying curious after a win keeps learning alive. Success should be enjoyed, but it should never be trusted as a teacher without questioning.
