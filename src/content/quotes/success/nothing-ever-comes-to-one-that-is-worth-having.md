Nothing worth having arrives by accident. Washington's plain truth is that the valuable things in life — skill, trust, freedom, a good name — are earned through sustained effort, not wished into being. Hard work is not a punishment but the price of admission to a meaningful life. It also protects what we gain: what is earned slowly is understood deeply and held carefully. The next time something feels out of reach, ask what steady work it would take, and begin. Worthwhile things are rarely free, and that is exactly why they matter.
