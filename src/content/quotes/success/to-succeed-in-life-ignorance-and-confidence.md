Knowing too much about the obstacles ahead can stop us before we start. Twain's wit carries a real insight: a little ignorance of the difficulties lets us begin with boldness, while confidence carries us through the challenges we discover along the way. Experts sometimes talk themselves out of trying because they see every risk; beginners, unaware of the odds, simply try and often succeed. The lesson is not to stay uninformed, but to avoid letting knowledge of difficulty become an excuse. Begin with confidence, and learn the rest as you go.
