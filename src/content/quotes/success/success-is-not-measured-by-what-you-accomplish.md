Trophies and titles tell only part of the story. Two people may reach the same result, yet one faced far greater obstacles to get there, and that struggle is the truer measure of success. Marden reminds us that achievement means most when it is earned against resistance: doubt, hardship, poverty, or failure. Easy victories teach little, while hard-won ones build character that lasts far beyond the prize itself. When we judge success, we should ask not only what someone achieved, but what they overcame to achieve it. That is where real greatness lives.
