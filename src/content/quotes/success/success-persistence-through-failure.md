Tyler corrects a common misunderstanding: successful people are not those who never fail, but those who refuse to stop at failure. Every meaningful achievement carries a trail of mistakes, rejections, and false starts behind it. The difference is not talent or luck, but persistence — the willingness to learn from the fall and try again. Failure is not the opposite of success; it is part of its raw material. If we stop treating setbacks as verdicts and start treating them as lessons, success becomes a matter of endurance rather than perfection.
