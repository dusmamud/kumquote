Sartre finds an unexpected gift in the passage of time: as life's sand runs out, our vision sharpens. Youth sees the world through thick glass, clouded by ambition, illusion, and the belief that time is endless. But as the years pass and the hourglass empties, the glass grows clearer, and we see what truly matters with a starkness that youth cannot afford. If we use it well, the narrowing of our remaining time becomes a lens, focusing us on what is essential and freeing us from what never was.
