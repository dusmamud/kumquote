Jordan separates three qualities that are often confused. Wisdom is judgment about the right next step, skill is the ability to carry it out, and virtue is the will to do it. A person can possess any one of them and still fall short: the wise person who never acts, the skilled person who works toward the wrong goal, or the well-meaning person who lacks the ability. Real effectiveness needs all three, in that order. The quote is a useful check before any decision: do I know what to do, can I do it, and will I follow through?
