It is useful to understand other people — their motives, their needs, their fears — and that understanding is a kind of intelligence. But Lao Tzu draws a clear line: the deeper achievement is knowing yourself. Self-knowledge means facing your own weaknesses honestly, recognising your patterns, and understanding what truly drives you. Without it, even great skill at reading others leads us astray, because we act from blind spots we cannot see. Wisdom begins at home. The person who understands their own heart makes better choices, keeps calmer in conflict, and grows steadily instead of repeating the same mistakes.
