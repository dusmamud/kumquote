Rumi contrasts two ambitions: changing the world and changing oneself. The first is grand, dramatic, and mostly out of our control; the second is humble, difficult, and entirely within it. Cleverness sees faults everywhere else and imagines fixing them from outside. Wisdom turns the gaze inward, recognising that our own habits, judgements, and reactions are the one territory we can truly govern. Ironically, changing ourselves is often what changes the world around us, because people respond to who we are more than to what we demand.
