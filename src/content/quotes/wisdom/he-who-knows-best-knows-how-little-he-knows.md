Jefferson captures a paradox of learning: the more we truly know, the more aware we become of what we do not know. Beginners often feel certain because they have not yet seen the edges of a subject. Experts, having explored deeply, see how vast the unknown remains. This is why genuine knowledge produces humility rather than arrogance. The person who knows best is the one most honest about their limits, most open to correction, and most willing to keep learning. Real wisdom shows itself as quiet modesty about the size of what remains unknown.
