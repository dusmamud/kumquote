Cicero defines wisdom not as cleverness, but as judgment. Knowledge can tell us how things work; only wisdom tells us whether a thing is good or harmful, right or wrong. Most of life's hard moments are not tests of information, but of discrimination — seeing the difference between a shortcut and a compromise, between loyalty and obedience, between ambition and greed. The wise person pauses where others rush, asking not "Can I do this?" but "Should I?" That single question, asked honestly, is the whole function of wisdom.
