Holmes draws a line between two habits: knowledge performs, wisdom receives. Knowing something makes us eager to announce it, to prove ourselves, and to fill the silence with facts. Listening asks for the opposite discipline, to stay quiet while someone else speaks, and to let a new idea land before judging it. The privilege is real: listeners learn what talkers never hear, because every conversation contains something the speaker knows and we do not. Speaking displays what we already have; listening is how we gain what we lack.
