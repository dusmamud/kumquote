Socrates traces wisdom to a simple starting point: wonder. The person who stops asking questions stops learning, because they assume the world is already understood. Wonder keeps the mind open; it turns ordinary things into mysteries worth exploring. A child asks why the sky is blue, and a wise adult never fully loses that curiosity. Without wonder, knowledge becomes a closed book, repeated but never refreshed. With it, every answer leads to a better question. Wisdom, then, is not a destination but a habit of remaining curious about life.
