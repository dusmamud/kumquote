Ignorance is honest: it admits what it does not know and stays open to learning. The illusion of knowledge is far more dangerous, because it closes the door before the lesson begins. A person convinced they already understand will not ask questions, test assumptions, or listen to correction. Progress depends on the humility to admit uncertainty, even about familiar subjects. The wisest people are those who hold their conclusions lightly, ready to revise them when better evidence arrives. That openness is the beginning of all real learning.
