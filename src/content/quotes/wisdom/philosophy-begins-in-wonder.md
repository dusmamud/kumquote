Wonder is the spark that starts all thinking. A child who asks why the sky is blue is already a philosopher, because the question matters more than any quick answer. When we stop wondering, we stop learning; the world becomes familiar, flat, and taken for granted. Philosophy begins in that moment of surprise when the ordinary suddenly seems strange and worth examining. Keeping wonder alive means refusing to pretend we already know everything. It keeps the mind open, curious, and humble. Every great discovery starts the same way: someone paused, looked closely, and asked why.
