Gibran warns us not to confuse eloquence with wisdom. A person can speak beautifully and say very little, while another may speak plainly and carry great depth. Words are only vessels; what matters is the meaning they hold, the lived truth behind them. Real wisdom is measured not by how impressively we talk but by how deeply we understand. When we listen, we should listen past the surface of sentences to the experience and reflection they contain. Meaning, not volume or polish, is the real test.
