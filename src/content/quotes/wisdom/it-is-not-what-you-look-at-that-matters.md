Thoreau draws a line between looking and seeing. Two people can stand before the same scene, the same person, or the same problem, and walk away with entirely different understandings. The eyes take in the surface, but seeing is the work of attention, patience, and an open mind. A hurried glance finds only what it expected, while careful seeing notices detail, motive, and meaning. Wisdom begins here: not in gathering more sights, but in learning to truly see the ones already in front of us.
