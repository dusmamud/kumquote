George Bernard Shaw warns that being wrong with confidence is worse than not knowing at all. An ignorant person knows they need to learn, so they stay open and cautious. A person armed with false knowledge feels certain, argues loudly, and acts on mistakes as if they were facts — spreading error faster than ignorance ever could. History is full of confident wrong ideas defended as truth. The remedy is humility about what we believe: check the source, question the slogan, and prefer honest uncertainty over a pleasing falsehood.
