Thoreau humbles every generation, including our own. We congratulate ourselves on what we know and forget that we also inherit our era's blind spots. The wisest person of any age is still shaped by the assumptions of that age — some true, some later exposed as false. Recognizing this should make us modest about our own certainty and generous toward the past. Wisdom includes knowing that future generations will see our ignorance clearly. The best we can do is question our own time as honestly as we judge others.
