Cooper separates two things we often confuse. Knowledge looks backward: it records what happened, what worked, and what others discovered. Wisdom looks forward: it asks what this knowledge means for the choice in front of us now. A person can be full of facts and still make foolish decisions, because facts alone do not tell us what to do next. Wisdom is knowledge put to work in an uncertain future, guided by judgement, values, and care for consequences. The past informs us, but only wisdom can lead us.
