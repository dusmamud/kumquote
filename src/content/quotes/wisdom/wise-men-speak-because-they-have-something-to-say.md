In a noisy world, speaking often feels like proof of importance. Yet the wisest people speak sparingly, because they wait until they have something true, useful, or kind to offer. Fools speak to fill silence, to impress others, or to avoid feeling left out, and their words add little. The difference is not talent but motive. Wise speech serves understanding; foolish speech serves the speaker. Learning to pause before speaking is a quiet discipline. It asks us to listen first, think clearly, and choose words that help rather than harm.
