Shakespeare warns that confidence is a poor measure of wisdom. The fool feels certain because he never examines his own ignorance, mistaking a few facts for full understanding. The wise person, having learned more, sees more clearly how much remains unknown, and that awareness brings humility. This is why the loudest voice in a discussion is rarely the wisest one. Wisdom grows from doubt and questioning, not from self-satisfaction. To admit we might be fools is the first step toward becoming wiser, because only an open mind can still be taught anything.
