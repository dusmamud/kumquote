Larson's definition is delightfully practical. We usually think of wisdom as the skill of escaping trouble, but the wiser skill is never entering trouble at all. Foresight — pausing before the risky commitment, the angry reply, the unnecessary debt — prevents most crises before they begin. Wisdom shows itself quietly, in the problems that never happened. The person who seems lucky is often simply prudent. Learning to see one step ahead, and to choose the boring safe path when it matters, is wisdom in its most useful form.
