Open-mindedness is often confused with agreeing to everything, but true thinking requires the opposite skill: holding an idea up to the light without letting it move in. We can examine a belief, understand why others hold it, and weigh its evidence, all while reserving judgement. This takes patience, because a new idea can feel persuasive before it has been tested. The educated mind stays curious without becoming credulous, asking questions instead of rushing to adopt or reject. Entertaining a thought is how we learn; accepting it too quickly is how we stop learning.
