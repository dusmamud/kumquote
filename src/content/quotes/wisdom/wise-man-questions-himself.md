Arnold points to where each person aims their questions. The fool interrogates everyone else, collecting flaws, motives, and excuses, because blaming others is easier than changing. The wise person turns the same sharp questions inward: What did I miss? What was my part? What should I do differently? This self-questioning is uncomfortable, which is exactly why it works. Other people cannot be fixed by our criticism, but our own thinking can be corrected by honest inquiry. Wisdom begins the moment we stop cross-examining the world and start examining ourselves.
