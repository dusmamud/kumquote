Lee describes wisdom as the quiet result of surviving difficulty. Pain itself teaches nothing while it is raw; it is the healing, the reflection, and the slow understanding afterward that turn it into insight. A person who has suffered and made sense of it carries a steadiness that no book can give. That is why the wise often speak gently about hard things: they know the cost firsthand. Wisdom is not the wound but the scar, proof that the hurt was faced and something lasting was learned.
