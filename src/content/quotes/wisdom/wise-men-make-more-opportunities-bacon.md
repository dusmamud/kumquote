Bacon contrasts two attitudes toward opportunity: waiting for it or making it. The passive person scans the horizon for an open door, complaining when none appears, while the wise person builds doors, creating chances through effort, skill, and bold initiative. Opportunities are rarely found lying in the road; they are usually made by someone who prepared, acted, and asked. This is not luck but craftsmanship, the habit of turning small openings into larger ones. Fortune favors those who move first, because action itself generates the very chances that waiting can never produce.
