Shakespeare, through Troilus, names a painful truth: wisdom and love pull in different directions. Love is blind, impulsive, and certain; wisdom is cautious, clear-eyed, and patient. To love wisely seems almost impossible, because love asks us to surrender the very judgement that wisdom protects. Yet this tension is part of being human. We cannot live by reason alone, nor by feeling alone. The line does not solve the problem — it honours it. Some of life's deepest truths are not puzzles to solve but tensions to hold.
