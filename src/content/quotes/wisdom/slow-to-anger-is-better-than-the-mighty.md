This ancient proverb measures strength differently from the world around it. Conquering a city takes armies, but ruling your own temper takes a steadier kind of power, one that must be won again every single day. Anger promises quick victory and usually delivers lasting regret, while patience looks weak in the moment and proves strong over time. The person who can pause before reacting holds the real advantage: they choose their response instead of being chosen by it. Self-command, the proverb says, outranks every outward conquest.
