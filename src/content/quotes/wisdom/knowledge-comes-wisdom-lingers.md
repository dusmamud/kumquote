Knowledge arrives quickly — a fact learned, a skill picked up, a book finished. Wisdom moves more slowly. It lingers, settling into character over years of experience, mistakes, and reflection. Tennyson's contrast explains why the well-informed are not always wise: information can be gathered in an afternoon, but judgment takes a lifetime. Wisdom is what remains after knowledge has been tested against reality. It cannot be rushed or downloaded. We should respect its slow pace and give our experiences time to teach us what facts alone cannot.
