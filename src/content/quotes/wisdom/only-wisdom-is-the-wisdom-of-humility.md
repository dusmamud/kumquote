Eliot names the rarest kind of wisdom: humility. Most of us chase knowledge as if collecting more facts will finally make us wise. But facts without humility harden into arrogance, and arrogance stops learning cold. True wisdom begins with admitting how little we know, how often we are wrong, and how much others can teach us. That admission is not weakness; it is the door through which real understanding enters. The humble mind stays curious, listens longer, and changes its views when evidence demands it. In the end, knowing our limits is the surest sign we are growing.
