Lao Tzu draws a line between two kinds of knowledge. Understanding other people — their motives, their fears, their hopes — is genuine wisdom, and it serves us well in the world. But knowing ourselves is something deeper: it is enlightenment, because the self is the one subject we can never escape and the one we most often avoid. We study others eagerly and ourselves reluctantly, yet the inner life shapes everything we do. True clarity begins at home, in honest self-knowledge.
