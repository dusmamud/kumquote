Gandhi turns wisdom back on itself: even wisdom must doubt itself. The moment we become sure of our own wisdom, we stop learning and start defending. Certainty about our judgment closes the very openness that made us wise. True wisdom keeps a margin for error, listens to correction, and treats its own conclusions as provisional. This is not indecision but honesty. The wisest people are those who hold their views firmly enough to act, yet loosely enough to change when better reasons arrive.
