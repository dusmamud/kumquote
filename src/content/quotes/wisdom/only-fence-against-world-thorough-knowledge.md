Locke compares ignorance to an open field, where anything can wander in unchallenged. Knowledge is the fence: it marks what is real, keeps out rumor and fear, and lets a person stand on firm ground. A thorough knowledge does not mean knowing everything, but knowing enough to judge what you hear and to test what you are told. Without that protection, the mind is at the mercy of every passing voice. Learning, in this sense, is not a luxury but a form of self-defense.
