Hugo calls wisdom a sacred communion — something shared and reverent, not owned. Wisdom is not a trophy to display; it is a quiet meeting between a person and the truth. Like communion, it requires openness, humility, and a willingness to receive. We do not conquer wisdom; we enter into it, slowly, the way we enter a friendship. It changes us from within rather than arming us from without. Those who treat wisdom as sacred guard it carefully and share it gently, knowing it is a gift received, not a possession earned by force.
