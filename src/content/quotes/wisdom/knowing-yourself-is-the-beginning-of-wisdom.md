Aristotle places self-knowledge at the very start of wisdom, before any study of the world. Without it, we mistake our biases for facts, our fears for good judgement, and our habits for choices. Knowing yourself means understanding your strengths and weaknesses, your triggers, your values, and the stories you tell yourself. That knowledge changes everything else: it makes our decisions clearer, our relationships kinder, and our ambitions more honest. It is also a lifelong task, because we change as we grow. Wisdom does not begin with a book or a teacher. It begins with looking inward, honestly.
