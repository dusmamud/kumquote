David Hume gives a simple rule for clear thinking: believe only as strongly as the evidence allows. A claim supported by solid proof deserves confidence; a claim with thin support deserves only a tentative maybe. The fool believes fully on a rumour and doubts stubbornly in the face of facts. The wise person keeps their certainty matched to their reasons, ready to change their mind when better evidence arrives. In an age of loud opinions, this quiet habit is wisdom itself — letting what is true decide what we believe, rather than letting what we believe decide what is true.
