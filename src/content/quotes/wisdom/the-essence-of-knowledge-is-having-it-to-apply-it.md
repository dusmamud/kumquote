Confucius separates knowing from pretending. Real knowledge has two honest moves: use what you know, and admit what you do not. The danger is the middle ground, where people bluff their way through ignorance and make confident errors. Applying knowledge turns learning into something useful; confessing ignorance turns it into the beginning of more learning. Both require humility. The wisest person is not the one who knows most, but the one who is clearest about the boundary between what they know and what they do not.
