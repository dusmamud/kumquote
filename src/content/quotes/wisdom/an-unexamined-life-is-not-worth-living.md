Socrates said these words at his trial, defending a life spent questioning everything, including himself. To live without examination is to drift — accepting beliefs because they are popular, following habits because they are familiar, and never asking whether our life is truly our own. Examination means pausing to ask why we believe what we believe and whether our actions match our values. It is uncomfortable, and it can disturb easy certainties, but it makes life deliberate rather than automatic. A life worth living is one we have thought about, chosen, and understood, not merely endured from day to day.
