Wisdom is often imagined as knowing more, but James turns that idea around: wisdom is knowing what to ignore. Life throws endless details, opinions, and provocations at us, and we cannot attend to them all. The wise person chooses carefully, letting small slights pass, refusing pointless arguments, and focusing energy on what truly matters. Overlooking is not carelessness; it is discipline. In the end, a good life is shaped as much by what we refuse to notice as by what we choose to see.
