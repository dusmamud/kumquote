La Rochefoucauld exposes a flattering illusion: we are brilliant advisers and poor patients. From a distance, someone else's problem looks simple, and the right answer is obvious. Our own problems are tangled in pride, fear, and habit, so the same clear thinking suddenly fails. Distance gives objectivity; closeness gives excuses. The cure is to treat our own situation as if it belonged to a friend: what would we honestly tell them to do? Wisdom is not knowing the right answer, it is being able to apply it when the problem is ours.
