Seneca redefines poverty: it is not a small bank balance but an endless appetite. A person with modest means who is content is richer than a wealthy person who always craves more, because craving has no finish line. Each new possession only raises the bar, and the wanting never stops. This is why the pursuit of more can feel like running on a treadmill — exhausting and going nowhere. True wealth, Seneca suggests, is wanting what you already have. When desire is mastered, even simple things feel abundant, and freedom arrives not from having everything but from needing little.
