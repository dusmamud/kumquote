Lao Tzu separates two kinds of understanding. Knowing others — reading people, judging character, seeing motives — is genuine wisdom, and it helps us navigate the world. But knowing ourselves is something deeper: enlightenment. It is harder, too, because we are the one person we cannot observe from the outside, and our own blind spots hide from us. Self-knowledge means facing our fears, our desires, and our habits without flattery or excuses. Those who achieve it are steadier, because they are not ruled by impulses they refuse to see. Wisdom begins with others; enlightenment begins at home.
