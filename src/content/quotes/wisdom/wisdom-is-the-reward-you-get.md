Wisdom is rarely the reward of speaking well or winning arguments. Larson suggests it comes from the opposite habit: listening, especially in the moments when talking feels more satisfying. Listening requires patience, humility, and the willingness to be changed by what you hear, which is why it is so difficult and so valuable. Over a lifetime, those quiet hours of attention accumulate into understanding that no book or lecture can give. The quote reframes wisdom as a practice rather than a possession, earned slowly by anyone willing to close their mouth, open their ears, and let other people teach them.
