Horace warns that reading alone does not make us wise. Books can fill the mind with facts and fine sentences, yet wisdom lives in how we act — in the choices we make under pressure, in the way we treat people, in what we do when no one is watching. Knowledge from books is a beginning, not an arrival. It must be tested against life, corrected by experience, and proven in conduct. A library is a tool, not a substitute for living. True wisdom is what remains after knowledge has been tried, kept, or quietly discarded.
