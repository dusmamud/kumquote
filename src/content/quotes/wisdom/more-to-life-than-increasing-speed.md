Gandhi's warning fits our hurried age perfectly. We measure progress by speed — faster work, faster messages, faster lives — and mistake velocity for meaning. But a faster life is not necessarily a better one. There is more to life than increasing its speed: depth, attention, relationships, and rest. Slowing down is not falling behind; it is making room for what speed crowds out. Wisdom chooses direction over pace, and asks not how fast we are moving but where we are going, and whether the journey is worth taking.
