Enemies are usually seen only as threats, but Gracián finds a surprising use for them. A critic watches us closely, notices our weaknesses, and speaks bluntly where friends may stay polite. That uncomfortable honesty can teach us more than comfortable praise ever will. A fool, by contrast, may have loyal friends yet learn nothing from them, because flattery asks for no change. Wisdom means listening even to voices we dislike and asking what truth hides inside them. The lesson is not to seek out enemies, but to learn from every honest mirror, whoever holds it.
