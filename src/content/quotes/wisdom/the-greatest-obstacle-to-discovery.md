Ignorance is honest — it knows it does not know, and so it keeps asking questions. The illusion of knowledge is far more dangerous, because it closes the mind while pretending to be open. When we believe we already understand something, we stop looking, stop listening, and stop learning. Boorstin warns that this false certainty blocks discovery more effectively than any lack of information. True wisdom begins with admitting the limits of what we know. Only a mind willing to be wrong can notice what it has missed and grow beyond it.
