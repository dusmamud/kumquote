Montaigne places self-possession above all other achievements, greater than wealth, fame, or power. To belong to oneself means to be at home in one's own mind, guided by one's own judgment rather than borrowed opinions or the crowd's applause. It is the quiet freedom of knowing what you believe, what you value, and where you stand, even when others disagree. Without this inner ownership, every success is rented from the approval of others and can be repossessed at any moment. The greatest thing, then, is not to conquer the world but to keep command of oneself within it.
