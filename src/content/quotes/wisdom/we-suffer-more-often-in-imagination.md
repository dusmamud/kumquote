Seneca notices that most of our suffering happens before anything bad actually occurs. We rehearse disasters in our minds, worry over outcomes that may never come, and endure the pain of events that exist only in imagination. The real difficulties of life, when they arrive, are often smaller and more manageable than the versions we feared. Our minds, trying to protect us, can become our tormentors. The remedy is to separate fact from fantasy: ask what is actually happening now, not what might happen later.
