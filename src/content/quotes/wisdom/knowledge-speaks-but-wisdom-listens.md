Hendrix draws a sharp line between knowledge and wisdom. Knowledge wants to be heard: it collects facts, forms opinions, and rushes to speak. Wisdom, by contrast, pauses and listens, because it knows how much it still does not understand. The quiet person in the room is often the one learning the most, noticing what others miss in their hurry to talk. Speaking proves what we already know; listening teaches us something new. True wisdom, then, is less about having answers and more about staying open enough to keep receiving them.
