Sartre finds a strange gift in the passing years. As sand leaves the hourglass, less remains, but the glass itself grows clearer; we begin to see our life without the fog of endless possibility and petty distraction. Youth imagines infinite time and chases everything at once. Age, with its narrower horizon, can finally tell what mattered from what merely glittered. The point is not to mourn the sand but to use the clarity it brings, choosing the few things worth our remaining hours.
