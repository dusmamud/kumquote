Vos Savant draws a clean line between two kinds of learning. Knowledge comes from study: reading, attending classes, memorizing facts. It is a deliberate, structured effort, and it works. Wisdom comes from observation: watching how people behave, noticing patterns, and seeing what happens when choices meet consequences. You cannot memorize wisdom from a book; you have to see it in the world. That is why some highly educated people make foolish choices, while some people with little schooling show remarkable judgement. Study fills the mind. Observation trains it. Both matter, but only one of them makes you wise.
