Montaigne draws a sharp line between borrowing facts and owning understanding. Knowledge can be handed over: we can memorise another person's findings, repeat their arguments, and pass their exams. Wisdom cannot be transferred so easily, because it is knowledge that has been tested against our own life, our mistakes, and our choices. Reading about patience is not the same as practising it under pressure. Advice can point the way, but wisdom only grows when we walk it ourselves and pay attention to what happens.
