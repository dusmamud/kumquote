Rathbone offers a refreshingly honest view of how wisdom actually arrives. It rarely comes from being clever; it comes from living through our own foolish choices and learning from them. Everyone has made decisions they later regret, and the difference between people is not whether they stumble but whether they study the fall. Surviving stupidity is not the achievement; extracting the lesson is. When we can look back at our errors without denial or self-pity, we convert embarrassment into understanding. Wisdom, in this sense, is the interest earned on the price we paid for our mistakes.
