Disraeli celebrates quotation as a kind of time travel. A single sharp sentence can carry a lifetime of experience across centuries, letting us borrow the judgment of people we will never meet. Without quotations, each generation would have to repeat every mistake from scratch; with them, wisdom compounds like interest. That is why great lines survive: they are compact, tested, and portable. To read and remember them is not decoration but inheritance. We stand on the experience of ages whenever we let an old sentence interrupt a new mistake.
