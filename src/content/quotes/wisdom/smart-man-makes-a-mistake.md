Williams describes wisdom as secondhand learning, the cheapest kind. The smart person pays for each lesson with their own failure, which is honourable but expensive. The wise person watches, asks questions, and borrows the lesson without paying the price. This requires humility, because it means admitting that someone else's experience counts as much as our own. Pride insists on learning everything firsthand; wisdom is content to learn from the bruises of others. The smartest move is often not to make the mistake at all.
