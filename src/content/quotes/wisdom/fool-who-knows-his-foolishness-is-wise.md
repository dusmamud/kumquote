The Buddha draws a sharp line between two kinds of fool. The first knows his limits and can therefore learn; the second is certain he has none and therefore cannot. Admitting foolishness is not self-pity but the first act of wisdom, because it opens the door to correction. The truly dangerous person is not the one who knows little but the one who believes he knows everything and stops listening. Growth begins the moment we can say, plainly, that we might be wrong.
