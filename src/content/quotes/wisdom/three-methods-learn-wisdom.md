Confucius maps out three roads to wisdom, and each has its price. Reflection is the noblest: sitting quietly with our thoughts and learning before we act. Imitation is the easiest: watching those who know more and following their example. Experience is the bitterest: learning through our own mistakes, which always costs more than we expect. Most of us rely on experience because we skip the first two. The wise person uses all three — reflecting first, imitating the best, and letting experience teach only what the other methods could not.
