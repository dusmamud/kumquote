Franklin, a printer's apprentice who became a statesman and inventor, knew the value of learning first-hand. Money invested can be lost, stolen, or spent, but knowledge stays with you and keeps paying returns for a lifetime. A skill learned, a language mastered, or a book absorbed changes how you think and what you can do, and no one can take it away. The interest Franklin speaks of is not only financial: knowledge opens doors, sharpens judgement, and enriches every conversation. Of all the investments available to us, learning is the safest and the most rewarding.
