Gibran warns that wisdom can curdle into something cold when it loses its heart. A person may know a great deal and still be unable to weep with a friend, laugh at a joke, or care about anyone beyond themselves. That is not wisdom; it is knowledge wearing a disguise. Real wisdom stays tender enough to feel, light enough to laugh, and generous enough to seek more than itself. Tears, laughter, and selflessness are not weaknesses that wisdom outgrows; they are proof that wisdom is still alive. The moment wisdom stops feeling, it has already stopped being wise.
