Confucius defines true knowledge with a simple test: know what you know, and know what you do not know. Most of our errors come from confusing the two — pretending certainty where we are ignorant, or doubting what we actually understand. Honest knowledge begins with an honest inventory. Admitting ignorance is not a failure; it is the first act of learning. The person who can say 'I do not know' clearly is far wiser than the one who speaks confidently about everything. True knowledge is humble, precise, and always aware of its own edges.
