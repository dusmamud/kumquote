Lincoln's advice turns our impatience on its head: most of the work should happen before the work begins. We love to swing the axe immediately, mistaking motion for progress, but a dull axe wastes every stroke and exhausts the worker. Sharpening first, planning, learning, gathering the right tools, looks like delay, yet it makes the actual task swift and sure. The four hours of preparation are not lost time but multiplied time, since each careful minute saves many careless ones later. Wisdom is knowing that the best way to work hard is often to prepare harder.
