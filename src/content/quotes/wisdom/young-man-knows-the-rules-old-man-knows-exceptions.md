Rules are useful, especially for the young, because they offer a clear map of how the world is supposed to work. But life is messier than any rulebook, and experience teaches that every rule has its exceptions, moments when judgment must override the letter of the law. The old have seen enough to know when to follow the rule and when to break it wisely. This is the difference between knowledge and wisdom: knowledge memorizes the rules, while wisdom understands their limits. Age does not guarantee this insight, but long, thoughtful experience is the surest way to earn it.
