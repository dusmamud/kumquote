Wilde's wit carries a serious point: we dress up our errors with a dignified name, calling them experience. The label is comforting, but the value is real. Mistakes teach what success cannot, because they show us plainly what does not work and force us to change. A person who has never failed may sound polished, yet their judgement is untested. Experience is not simply the passage of time; it is the lessons extracted from things that went wrong. The wise response to a mistake is not shame but study.
