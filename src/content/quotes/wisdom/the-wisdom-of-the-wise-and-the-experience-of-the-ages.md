Disraeli celebrates the humble quotation as the vessel that carries wisdom across centuries. A great idea might die with its thinker, but once it is compressed into a memorable sentence, it travels — copied into books, repeated in speeches, passed from parent to child. Quotations are how the experience of the ages survives the ages themselves. Every time we repeat a wise line, we keep a distant mind alive, and we hand its hard-won lesson to someone who never met its author.
