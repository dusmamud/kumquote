Jefferson warns that a wrong belief is worse than no belief at all. An empty mind can still learn, but a mind crowded with confident errors has to unlearn before it can begin. Falsehoods feel like knowledge; they answer our questions quickly and flatter our certainty, which is exactly why they are hard to give up. Wisdom starts with clearing space: admitting what we do not know, questioning what we were told, and testing ideas against evidence. Ignorance is a starting point, but confident error is a wall.
