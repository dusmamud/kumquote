Johnson offers a practical view of knowledge. Some things we know ourselves, and the rest we need only know where to find. Both kinds are useful, and pretending to know everything is unnecessary. A wise person keeps good sources, good friends, and good references close, and is not ashamed to look something up. Memory is limited, but knowing how to find reliable information is a skill that never runs out. Humility about the second kind of knowledge is what makes the first kind trustworthy.
