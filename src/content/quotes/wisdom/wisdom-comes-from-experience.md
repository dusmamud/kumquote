Pratchett delivers a wry circle: wisdom grows from experience, and experience often arrives precisely because we lacked wisdom. Every mistake becomes tuition, and the tuition is rarely cheap. The foolish part is not making the error, but refusing to learn from it. A wise person treats each stumble as data, asking what went wrong and what to change next time. The fool repeats the same lesson at full price. So experience is not automatically wisdom; it only becomes wisdom when we pause, reflect, and adjust. Learn the lesson once, and the next experience can be a kinder teacher.
