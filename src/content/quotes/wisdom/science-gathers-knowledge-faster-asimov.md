Science hands us new tools at a dizzying pace, yet society's wisdom, our ability to use those tools well, lags behind. Knowledge tells us what we can do; wisdom must decide what we should do, and that slower work of reflection, ethics, and foresight is easily crowded out by excitement. The sadness lies not in progress itself but in progress without guidance, like giving a child ever sharper instruments. Closing the gap means valuing the humanities, debate, and moral reflection as much as discovery itself, so that our wisdom can catch up with our knowledge.
