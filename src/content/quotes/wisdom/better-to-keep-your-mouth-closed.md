Twain's advice is practical and comic at once. Speaking less protects us from exposing what we do not know. Silence leaves room for others to imagine us wise; a hasty opinion can remove all doubt in the wrong direction. This is not a call to never speak — honest speech matters — but a reminder that words are permanent and cheap talk is costly. The wise pause before they speak, ask whether their words add anything, and often choose silence. Listening more and speaking less rarely makes anyone look foolish. It usually makes them look thoughtful.
