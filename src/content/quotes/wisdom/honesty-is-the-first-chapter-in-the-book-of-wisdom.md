Wisdom is often pictured as cleverness, but Jefferson places honesty at its foundation. Before we can understand the world clearly, we must be truthful with ourselves about what we know, what we do not know, and what we have done. Self-deception makes every later judgment unreliable, while honesty keeps our thinking clear and our choices sound. A wise person does not hide behind flattery or excuses; they face facts plainly and speak truthfully to others. Honesty is not only a moral virtue. It is the first skill of clear thinking, and without it, no deeper wisdom can be built.
