Hermann Hesse draws a line between what can be taught and what must be lived. Facts, formulas, and instructions pass easily from one person to another — that is knowledge, and it can be written in books. Wisdom is different: it is knowledge that has passed through experience, failure, and reflection until it changes how a person acts. No one can hand you their wisdom like a parcel; they can only share the stories, and you must walk the road yourself. That is why the wise often speak in few words — they know the lesson cannot be given, only earned.
