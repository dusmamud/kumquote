Plato separates speech driven by thought from speech driven by the need to be heard. The wise person waits until there is something worth saying, and silence costs them nothing, because their worth does not depend on holding the floor. The fool speaks to fill the quiet, to impress, or to prove they belong, and the words arrive before the thinking does. In meetings, arguments, and everyday talk, the difference is easy to hear. Wisdom often sounds like restraint: saying less, meaning more, and letting quiet do its work.
