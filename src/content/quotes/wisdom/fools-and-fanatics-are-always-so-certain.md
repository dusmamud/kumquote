Russell observes a strange imbalance in the world: those who know least are often the most certain, while those who know most are full of doubt. Certainty feels strong, but it is often a sign of a narrow mind that has stopped questioning. Doubt, by contrast, is not weakness — it is the mark of a mind still learning. Wisdom begins when we admit how much we do not know. The wiser we become, the more we see the complexity of things. True confidence is quiet and open; false confidence is loud and closed.
