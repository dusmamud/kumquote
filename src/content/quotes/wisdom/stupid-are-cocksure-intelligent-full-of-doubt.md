Russell captures a painful pattern: the least informed are often the most certain, while those who know the most are cautious and doubtful. Certainty feels strong, so it spreads easily, while doubt feels weak, so it stays quiet. The trouble is that confident error moves faster than careful truth. Wisdom therefore requires a particular courage — the willingness to say "I might be wrong" in a world that rewards loud certainty. Doubt is not weakness; it is the honest recognition that reality is complicated, and the beginning of better judgment.
