Roosevelt corrects a common misunderstanding: brave people are not fearless. They feel fear just as we do, but they have weighed it against something that matters more — a duty, a loved one, a principle — and found fear wanting. Courage is therefore a judgement, not a temperament. It is the moment we decide that the thing we value is worth the risk of acting. That is good news for the rest of us, because it means courage can be learned. We do not need to eliminate fear; we only need something we care about more than being afraid.
