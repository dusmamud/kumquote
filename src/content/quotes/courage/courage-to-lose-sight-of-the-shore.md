Faulkner uses the image of a swimmer leaving the shore to describe every great change. New horizons — a new career, a new country, a new way of life — cannot be reached while we cling to the familiar. At some point we must let go of the solid ground we know and swim into open water, where the shore is no longer visible and there is no turning back. Courage is not the absence of that fear but the willingness to lose sight of safety in exchange for something greater.
