In hard times, fear itself becomes the greatest obstacle. It freezes action, clouds judgment, and turns small problems into paralysing threats. Roosevelt spoke these words when the nation faced deep economic hardship, and his point was practical, not just comforting. The real danger was not only the crisis, but the fear that would stop people from working, helping, and rebuilding together. Fear, left unchecked, creates its own disaster. When we face fear directly and refuse to let it rule us, we free ourselves to act with hope, reason, and courage.
