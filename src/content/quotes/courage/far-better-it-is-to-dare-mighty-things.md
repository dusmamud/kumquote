Roosevelt praises those who dare mighty things, even when failure follows. The alternative is to live safely among poor spirits who neither enjoy much nor suffer much, a life of small comforts and no triumphs. Daring means accepting that glorious attempts may be checkered by failure, and choosing them anyway. The quote is a call to ambition with honour: better to strive and fall than to never strive at all. Greatness belongs to those willing to risk it. The poor spirits keep their safety and lose everything else; the daring lose only their fear.
