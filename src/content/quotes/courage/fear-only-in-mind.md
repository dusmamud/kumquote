Fear feels like a wall in the world, solid and real, but it lives only in our thoughts about what might happen. The danger we imagine is often far larger than the danger that actually exists. Once we see fear as a mental event rather than a fact, it becomes something we can work with. We can question it, prepare for it, and act in spite of it. The mind that creates fear can also be trained to meet it calmly. Courage begins with that shift: fear is not the world stopping us, it is only a thought passing through.
