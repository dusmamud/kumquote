Dying for a cause can look like the ultimate courage, yet Lao Tzu suggests it is the easier kind, because it ends quickly and asks nothing more. Living, by contrast, demands courage every single day. It means facing disappointment, admitting mistakes, forgiving others, and carrying on when no one is watching or applauding. Inner courage is quiet and unglamorous, but it is the braver path, since it must be renewed morning after morning. The line asks us to admire not the dramatic gesture, but the steady person who keeps choosing life, with all its difficulty, again and again.
