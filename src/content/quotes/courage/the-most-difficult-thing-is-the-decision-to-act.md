Earhart, who flew alone across the Atlantic, knew that the hardest part of any bold act is the decision to begin. Once the decision is made, what remains is tenacity — steady, unglamorous persistence — and that is a skill most of us already have. The decision is hard because it means accepting risk, leaving comfort, and facing the possibility of failure. We delay it by planning endlessly, waiting for certainty that never arrives. But certainty is not the price of action; the decision itself is.
