Shakespeare, through Caesar, argues that fear kills us long before danger does. The coward dies a thousand small deaths, rehearsing every disaster in advance, suffering in imagination what never happens in life. The valiant person faces danger once, in reality, and is done with it. Fear multiplies suffering by making us live through the worst outcome again and again. Courage does not promise safety; it promises something better, that we suffer only what actually happens, once, instead of endlessly in advance.
