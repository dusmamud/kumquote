Aesop's short line exposes a common self-deception. From a safe distance, anyone can be bold — criticizing, advising, and promising courage they never have to prove. Real bravery begins when safety ends, when the risk is personal and the cost is real. The quote invites humility about our own imagined courage and skepticism toward loud bravery that costs nothing. It asks a simple question of any bold claim: would you still say it up close? True courage is measured at close range, where it counts.
