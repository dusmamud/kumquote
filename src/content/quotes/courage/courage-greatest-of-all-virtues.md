Telling the truth when a lie would be easier, standing up for someone when silence would be safer, giving when keeping would be simpler — each of these requires courage first. Without it, our virtues remain private intentions, never put to use. Johnson calls courage the greatest virtue not because it matters more than the others, but because it unlocks them. It is the gate through which every other good quality must pass to become real. A person of courage may fail, but they will at least have lived their values.
