Shaw's joke stings because it reverses what we think courage is. We admire the lion tamer facing a wild animal, yet Shaw suggests the braver act is facing other people, with their judgments, moods, and cruelty. A lion's danger is honest and predictable; human danger is complicated, personal, and endless. The line reminds us that physical courage is often easier than social courage: speaking up in a room, telling the truth to a friend, or standing alone against a crowd. Those are the cages that truly frighten us.
