Disappointments are real, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Plans fail, people let us down, and justice moves slower than we wish. But disappointment is finite: it has edges, a beginning and an end. Hope, by contrast, has no boundary if we refuse to let it die. This line pairs clear-eyed realism with stubborn courage. We can accept the loss of this battle without surrendering the belief that a better future is possible. Hope is not naivety here; it is the discipline of holding on when disappointment says to let go.
