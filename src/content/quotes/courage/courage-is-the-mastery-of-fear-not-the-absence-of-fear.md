Twain corrects a common misunderstanding: courage is not the absence of fear. Fearless people are not brave; they simply feel nothing. Courage is the mastery of fear, the ability to act well while afraid. The frightened person who stands up anyway shows more courage than the fearless one who never had to choose. The quote frees us from shame about fear. Feeling afraid does not disqualify you from being brave. Mastery, not absence, is the true measure. Fear is the material courage works with, and mastering it is the whole of the art.
