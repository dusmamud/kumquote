Real courage rarely looks heroic. Most of the time it begins with someone who feels afraid, uncertain, and far from brave, yet decides to act as if courage were already theirs. Harris points out that professing courage is not pretending or lying; it is a commitment that pulls behaviour forward. When you declare you will face something and then take the first step, the feeling often follows the action. Bravery, in this view, is less a quality you possess than a decision you make repeatedly, especially on the days when fear is loudest and confidence is nowhere to be found.
