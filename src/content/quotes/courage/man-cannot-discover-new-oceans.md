New horizons can only be reached by leaving the familiar behind, and that departure is frightening. The shore represents safety: known routines, certain outcomes, and the comfort of solid ground. Gide reminds us that discovery demands letting go of that security, at least for a while. No sailor finds new lands while clinging to the coastline. The courage to lose sight of the shore is the willingness to trust ourselves in open water, uncertain of what lies ahead. Growth begins at the moment we accept that we cannot see the destination and sail anyway.
