Regret comes in two kinds, and Ball argues they are not equal. The regrets of inaction are emptier and harder to bear, because they are haunted by what might have been. We can never know whether the chance we avoided would have worked out, and that uncertainty lingers for years. Choosing action does not guarantee success, but it guarantees experience, and experience can be lived with. A life of attempts, even failed ones, feels fuller than a life of careful avoidance. Better to regret the leap than to wonder forever about the edge.
