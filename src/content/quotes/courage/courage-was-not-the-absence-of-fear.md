Fear is not the enemy of courage; it is its raw material. The brave feel fear just as keenly as anyone else, perhaps more so, because they understand the stakes. What sets them apart is what they do next: they act anyway, step by step, until fear loosens its grip. Mandela learned this in prison, where fear was a daily companion and courage a daily choice. Triumph over fear rarely comes in one dramatic moment; it comes from repeated small victories. Each time we face what frightens us, fear shrinks a little, and our freedom grows a little larger.
