McLaughlin shrinks courage down to its smallest, truest size: not the grand heroic gesture, but the quiet act of surviving one more moment. We imagine courage as a single dramatic leap, yet most of life's bravery is ordinary endurance — getting through the next hour, the next conversation, the next hard morning. That is the only courage that matters, because it is the only kind real life ever asks of us. Moment by moment, it carries us through. Each small step forward is a victory worth counting.
