Carlyle repeats the word three times because once is not enough to capture what courage does to a life. Courage raises ordinary existence into something vivid and splendid, like blood rising to the surface and coloring everything it touches. Without it, days stay pale: safe, predictable, and forgettable. With it, the same life gains color, because we attempt things, risk failure, and feel fully alive. Carlyle's image is physical on purpose: courage is not an idea but a force in the body. Let it rise, and watch a dull life turn crimson with meaning.
