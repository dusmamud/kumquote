Bravery is not the absence of fear, but the ability to do your job while fear is screaming in your ears. A soldier on a battlefield, a surgeon in an emergency, or a speaker before a crowd all feel the same trembling; the difference is training and purpose. Performing properly under fear means focusing on the next small action. Fear narrows the mind, but discipline widens it again, one step at a time. Anyone can learn this: rehearse, breathe, and act. Courage, in the end, is a skill of doing what must be done, even while scared half to death.
