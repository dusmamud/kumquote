Burke warns that evil rarely needs active help to win; it needs only the silence of good people. When decent men and women look away, stay quiet, or decide that someone else will act, wrongdoing spreads unopposed. The quote places responsibility squarely on the bystander, since inaction is itself a choice with consequences. Courage, in this light, is not always dramatic; often it is the simple act of speaking up, refusing to comply, or doing the small right thing when it would be easier to do nothing. Evil triumphs not because it is strong, but because goodness stayed silent.
