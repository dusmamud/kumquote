Fear does not disappear when we face it; it changes form. Prayer, in this sense, is not only a religious act but any honest turning toward something larger than ourselves, a moment of stillness, a call for help, a decision to trust. When fear is spoken aloud, it loses some of its power to paralyze. Courage is born in that exchange: fear remains, but it is no longer in charge. We act not because we are unafraid, but because we have placed our fear somewhere safe and chosen to move forward anyway. That is the quiet alchemy of bravery.
