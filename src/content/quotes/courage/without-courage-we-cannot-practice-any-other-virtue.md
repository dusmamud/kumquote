Maya Angelou places courage at the foundation of every other virtue. Kindness, honesty, mercy, and generosity are easy to praise when they cost nothing — but the moment they require standing up, speaking out, or giving when it hurts, they demand courage first. Without it, our values become wishes we abandon under pressure. Angelou is not saying courage matters more than the rest; she is saying it is what makes the rest possible. A virtue that collapses at the first sign of risk was never really a virtue, only a preference, and courage is what turns preference into practice.
