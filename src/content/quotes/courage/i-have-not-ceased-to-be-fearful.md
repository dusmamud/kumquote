Fear does not simply disappear once we become brave; it stays with us, whispering warnings and urging us to hold back. The difference is not the absence of fear but the refusal to let it make the decisions. We can feel our hands shake and still sign the letter, feel our voice waver and still say the words. Fear is a passenger, not the driver, and it loses power the moment we stop obeying it. Real courage is ordinary and repeatable: notice the fear, thank it for its warning, and then choose to act anyway.
