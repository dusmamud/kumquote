Ingersoll argues that the hardest test of courage is not facing danger but facing defeat. Anyone can be brave in victory, when the crowd is cheering and the outcome is certain. Bearing defeat — a failed plan, a lost love, a shattered hope — without losing heart is far harder. The courage to keep your heart open after a loss, to believe that effort still matters, is the rarest kind. It is also the most necessary, because a life without defeat is impossible, and only those who endure it can ever reach the victories beyond it.
