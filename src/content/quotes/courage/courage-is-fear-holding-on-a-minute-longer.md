Patton reduces courage to a single minute of endurance. Fear tells us to quit now, and the moment we obey it, the story ends. Courage does not remove the fear or make the danger smaller; it simply postpones surrender by sixty seconds, and then by another sixty. Most of what we call bravery is this quiet refusal to stop one minute early. The battle, the speech, the hard conversation, all of them are won by people who held on a minute longer than they thought they could.
