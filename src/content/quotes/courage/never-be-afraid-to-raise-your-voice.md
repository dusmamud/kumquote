Speaking up is often harder than staying silent, because silence feels safe and speech carries a cost. Yet every injustice survives partly because good people say nothing. Faulkner reminds us that courage is not only found in dramatic moments; it lives in the ordinary choice to tell the truth when a lie would be easier. Raising our voice for honesty and compassion does not require loudness or anger, only clarity and steadiness. One honest voice can give others permission to speak too, and that is how quiet rooms slowly begin to change.
