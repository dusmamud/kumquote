The coward's real punishment is not death itself but the thousand small deaths that come before it: every imagined disaster, every sleepless night, every chance avoided out of fear. Fear makes us rehearse our ending again and again, so we suffer it many times over. The brave person faces the same single end but lives freely until it comes, tasting death only once. The line is a challenge to stop dying in advance. Fear cannot protect us from the end; it can only steal the life we have before it arrives.
