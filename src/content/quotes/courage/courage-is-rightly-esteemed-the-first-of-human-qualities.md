Winston Churchill, who led Britain through its darkest hours, calls courage the first of human qualities — not the loudest or the most glamorous, but the one that guarantees all the others. Wisdom without courage stays silent. Honesty without courage bends under pressure. Compassion without courage looks away. Churchill understood this from experience: in a crisis, every other virtue depends on someone being brave enough to act on it. That is why courage is rightly esteemed first. It is the foundation on which character is built, and the quality that turns good intentions into real deeds.
