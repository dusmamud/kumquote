No law, shelter, or guardian can protect a person the way her own courage can. Courage lets a woman speak when she is told to be quiet, leave when she is told to stay, and demand what is fair when she is told to accept less. It is not loud or aggressive; it is the steady refusal to shrink herself to fit other people's comfort. Protection from others can be taken away, but courage travels with its owner. To be brave is not to be fearless, but to trust one's own voice more than the voices urging silence.
