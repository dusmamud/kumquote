Courage is often imagined as something people are simply born with, as if bravery were a trait handed out at birth. Angelou rejects that idea and replaces it with something more generous: everyone is born with potential, and courage is what that potential becomes when it is practised. Nobody needs to wait for fearlessness to arrive before acting. Courage grows the way any skill grows, through small acts of standing up, speaking honestly, and trying again. The point is hopeful: bravery is not a gift for the few, but a capacity inside every person, waiting to be used.
