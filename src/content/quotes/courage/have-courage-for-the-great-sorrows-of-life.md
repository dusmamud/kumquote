Hugo separates the two kinds of hardship we face and gives each its own answer. Great sorrows cannot be solved or hurried; they ask for courage, the willingness to endure what cannot be changed. Small troubles need only patience, a refusal to let minor irritations grow into crises. Then comes the practical part: do the day's work well, without borrowing tomorrow's worries. A day lived this way earns its rest. Peace at night is no accident; it is the reward of meeting the day with courage where it mattered and patience where it did not.
