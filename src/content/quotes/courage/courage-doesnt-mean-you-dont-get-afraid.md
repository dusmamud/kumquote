Many people wait for fear to vanish before they act, as if courage meant feeling nothing. Hamilton, who returned to surfing after losing an arm to a shark, knows better. Fear is a normal signal, not a stop sign; it tells us something matters, not that we must quit. Courage is the decision to move forward with fear still present, one stroke at a time. This view makes bravery available to everyone, because it does not ask us to be fearless. It asks only that fear not be given the final word over what we do next.
