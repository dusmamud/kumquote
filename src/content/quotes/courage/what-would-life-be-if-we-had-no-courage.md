Van Gogh, who painted in obscurity and doubt, asks a question that answers itself. A life without the courage to attempt anything would be a life of watching from the sidelines — safe, perhaps, but hollow. Every meaningful thing begins as an attempt: a painting, a business, a friendship, a change of direction. Attempts carry the risk of failure, and that risk is what keeps most people from trying. But the greater risk is never attempting at all, and reaching the end of life with nothing ventured.
