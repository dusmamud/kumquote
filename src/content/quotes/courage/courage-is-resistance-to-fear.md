Twain corrects a common misunderstanding: brave people are not fearless. They feel fear just as keenly as anyone else, but they refuse to let it decide for them. Courage is the act of resisting fear — standing steady while the fear is still there — and over time, mastering it. True courage is known by the presence of fear, and the choice made in spite of it. When we accept that fear will come along, we stop waiting to feel fearless before acting, and we discover that we were braver than we thought.
