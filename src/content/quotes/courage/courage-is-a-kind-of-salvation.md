Plato calls courage a kind of salvation because it rescues us from the fear that would otherwise rule our lives. Fear shrinks the world: it keeps us silent when we should speak, still when we should move, and small when we could grow. Courage does not erase fear; it saves us from obeying it. Each brave act is a small rescue, pulling one more part of life back from fear's control. That is why courage feels like salvation, not one dramatic moment, but a steady deliverance, choice by choice, from a life governed by fear into one governed by purpose.
