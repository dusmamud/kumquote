Emerson offers a practical cure for fear: do the feared thing, and the fear dies. Fear feeds on avoidance; every time we step around it, it grows a little larger and more convincing. Action breaks the spell, because most fears shrink the moment we touch them. The speech is less terrifying than the week spent dreading it; the conversation is easier than the months of silence. Fear promises that doing the thing will destroy us, but experience usually shows the opposite. The only certain way to kill a fear is to walk straight through it.
