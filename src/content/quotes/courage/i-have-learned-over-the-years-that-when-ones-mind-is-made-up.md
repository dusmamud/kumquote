Rosa Parks speaks from a life of determined bravery. She learned that fear shrinks not when danger disappears, but when the mind is made up. Once you know what must be done, the question changes from whether to act to how to act, and that clarity leaves little room for fear to grow. Her words carry the weight of someone who sat down on a bus knowing the consequences and did it anyway. Courage, in her account, is not a feeling that arrives before the act; it is the result of a decision, that the right thing is worth doing.
