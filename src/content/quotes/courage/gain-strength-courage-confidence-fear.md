Roosevelt explains how courage is built: not by avoiding fear but by stopping to look it in the face. Each time we face what frightens us — the difficult conversation, the public failure, the honest admission — we discover we can survive it, and that discovery becomes strength. Confidence is not a gift but a record of fears faced and survived. The experiences that scare us most are therefore the most valuable, because they are the ones that expand what we believe we can do. Courage grows one faced fear at a time.
