Imagination is a double-edged gift: it lets us dream of better futures, but it also lets us picture every disaster in vivid detail. Fear grows in that imagined space, larger than any real threat. Courage is the capacity to face those imagined terrors directly, to look at the worst case and still choose to act. The brave do not lack imagination; they master it, using it to plan rather than to panic. Confronting what we fear in our minds first makes the real thing lose its mystery. Courage begins as an inner argument, won one imagined fear at a time.
