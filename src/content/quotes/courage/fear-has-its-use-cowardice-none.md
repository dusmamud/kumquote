Fear is often treated as an enemy, but Gandhi defends it. Fear warns us of danger, sharpens our senses, and keeps us from foolish risks. It has a genuine purpose. The distinction matters, because it removes the shame from being afraid. Feeling fear is human and useful. Surrendering to it is the failure. Gandhi, who faced prison and violence without raising a weapon, knew this difference intimately. Courage is not the absence of fear; it is fear kept in its proper place.
