Krause strips courage down to its honest core: trying while knowing you might fail. It is easy to act when victory is assured, but that is not courage, it is arithmetic. Real courage begins with the clear-eyed discovery that losing is possible, even likely, and the decision to try anyway. This kind of bravery has nothing to do with confidence or swagger; it is quieter, the choice to enter the contest without the comfort of a promised outcome. Win or lose, the attempt itself is the victory, because it proves that fear of failure no longer decides what we do.
