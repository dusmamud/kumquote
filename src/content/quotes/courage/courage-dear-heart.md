These three words, spoken by Aslan in The Chronicles of Narnia, are among the gentlest encouragements in literature. They do not command or lecture; they address the heart directly, as a friend would. Courage is presented not as a heroic feat but as something tender, personal, and close — a quiet word to someone who is afraid. The brevity is the point: when fear is real, long speeches do not help, but a simple, kind reminder can steady us. Lewis knew that courage often needs only a small voice saying, go on.
