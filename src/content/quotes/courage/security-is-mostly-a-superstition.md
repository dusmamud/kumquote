Helen Keller, who lived with deafness and blindness, rejected the idea that safety is the purpose of life. Security, she argues, is largely an illusion: no amount of caution can guarantee that nothing bad will happen, and chasing safety above all else leaves life small and fearful. The alternative is to treat life as a daring adventure — to accept risk as the price of a life fully lived. Adventure does not mean recklessness; it means choosing growth over comfort and experience over worry. A life without daring may feel safe, but Keller warns it is also empty.
