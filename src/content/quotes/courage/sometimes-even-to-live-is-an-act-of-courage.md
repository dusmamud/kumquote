Courage is usually pictured as a bold deed, but Seneca points to a quieter kind: simply continuing to live through hard times. After loss, failure, or long sadness, getting up each morning and facing another day can demand more strength than any single heroic act. This idea honours the people who keep going without applause, who endure what cannot be fixed quickly and still choose life. It also reframes our own struggles: if merely living through a difficult season takes courage, then we are braver than we think, and each day we endure is a small victory worth noticing.
