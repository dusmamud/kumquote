We usually blame power itself when leaders become cruel, but the deeper poison is fear: the fear of losing control, of being challenged, of being exposed. A ruler who fears losing power clings to it ever more tightly, silencing critics and punishing dissent. Meanwhile, ordinary people who fear punishment learn to stay silent, and their silence lets the cruelty continue. Fear corrupts both sides of the same bargain. The way out is to face fear openly, because a society that names its fears can finally begin to free itself.
