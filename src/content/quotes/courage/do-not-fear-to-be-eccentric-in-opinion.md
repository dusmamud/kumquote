Every idea that society now takes for granted was once strange to someone. The round earth, equal rights, and countless scientific truths all began as eccentric opinions held by people willing to look foolish. Russell reminds us that the fear of standing out is the enemy of progress: if everyone waited for approval before thinking differently, nothing new would ever be believed. Holding an unpopular view takes courage, but it also takes honesty, because we owe our real thoughts more than silent agreement. Today's odd opinion may be tomorrow's common sense, and someone has to voice it first.
