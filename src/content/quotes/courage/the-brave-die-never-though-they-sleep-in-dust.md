Byron argues that courage outlives the courageous. A brave person's body may return to dust, but the example they set does not die with them; it nerves a thousand living men, giving others the strength to act. This is why we remember heroes long after their time: their courage becomes a resource for the rest of us. To live bravely is therefore to give a gift that keeps working after we are gone — an inheritance measured not in wealth, but in the courage it lends to others.
