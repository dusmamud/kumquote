Theodore Roosevelt redefines courage as endurance without resources. Anyone can continue while strong; the test comes when strength is gone and stopping feels justified. Going on then is not powered by energy but by will — by the decision that the task matters more than comfort. This is the courage of the last mile, the final round, the difficult year. It reassures us that feeling exhausted does not mean we have failed; it means we have reached the place where courage actually begins, and where it matters most.
