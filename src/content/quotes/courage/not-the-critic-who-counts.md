Every bold attempt attracts critics, people who stand safely outside the arena and explain what the doer did wrong. Their words can sting, but they cost them nothing. The person who counts is the one in the arena, trying, stumbling, and trying again. Criticism from the sidelines is cheap because it risks nothing. This line frees us from giving critics more weight than they deserve. If someone has never risked failure themselves, their judgment of our efforts matters far less than our own willingness to keep acting.
