Aristotle argues that courage makes every other virtue possible. Honesty, generosity, and justice all require moments when standing up costs something. Without courage, good intentions stay private and untested. A person may know the right thing and still fail to do it for fear of the consequences. Courage is the bridge between knowing and doing. It does not replace the other virtues; it enables them. That is why Aristotle places it first: everything else depends on it. Build courage first, and the other virtues will have a foundation strong enough to stand on.
