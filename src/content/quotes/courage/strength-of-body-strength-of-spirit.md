Tolkien, who wrote of small heroes facing great darkness, reminds us that the decisive strength is inward. Bodies fail, age, and tire; spirit endures, chooses, and persists. A person of modest physical strength but firm spirit can outlast far stronger opponents, because courage is a matter of will rather than muscle. The quote dignifies those who feel physically outmatched: what counts is the spirit that refuses to quit. In every real trial, it is the inner resolve that carries the day.
