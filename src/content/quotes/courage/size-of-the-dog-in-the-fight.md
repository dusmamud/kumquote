Twain's joke carries a serious truth about competition: outcomes are decided by spirit, not by size. The bigger opponent, the richer company, or the louder rival looks unbeatable on paper, yet history is full of underdogs who won because they refused to accept the odds. Fight means persistence, the willingness to keep pushing when talent and resources run short. Size is a fact; fight is a choice. And in any contest that lasts long enough, the choice matters more than the fact.
