Angelou ranks courage first among the virtues, and for a practical reason: every other virtue depends on it. Honesty requires the courage to tell the truth when it is costly. Kindness requires the courage to act when others look away. Justice requires the courage to stand up when standing is dangerous. Without courage, our good intentions stay private, and our values collapse under pressure. Courage is therefore not one virtue among many; it is the foundation that makes all the others possible. Practise courage first, Angelou suggests, and the rest of character has something solid to stand on.
