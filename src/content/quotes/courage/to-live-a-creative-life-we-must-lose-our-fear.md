Every new idea begins as a risk, because it might fail, look foolish, or be rejected. If we demand certainty before we begin, we never begin at all, and the creative life stays a daydream. Losing the fear of being wrong does not mean pretending mistakes do not matter; it means treating them as the price of making anything original. The painter, the writer, and the inventor all work through wrong turns on the way to something true. Once we accept that error is part of the process, the fear loosens its grip and the work can finally start.
