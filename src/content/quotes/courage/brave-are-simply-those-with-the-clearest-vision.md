Thucydides strips away the romance of bravery. The brave are not blind to danger or drunk on glory; they see both with unusual clarity, and they go forward anyway. Ignorance can look like courage, but it is only accident, because a person who cannot see the risk has made no choice. Real courage requires open eyes: knowing exactly what is at stake, counting the cost honestly, and still stepping forward. That clear-eyed decision, made with full knowledge of glory and danger alike, is what separates the brave from the reckless.
