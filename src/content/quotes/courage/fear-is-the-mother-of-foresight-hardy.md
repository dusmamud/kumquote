Hardy offers a surprising defense of fear, calling it the mother of foresight. Fear makes us look ahead, imagine what could go wrong, and prepare for dangers we might otherwise ignore. Without it, we would blunder blindly into trouble, careless and unready. The point is not to let fear rule us, but to recognize that it carries useful information, a warning that sharpens our attention and our planning. Courage, then, is not the absence of fear but its proper use: listening to what fear teaches, preparing wisely, and then acting anyway. Fear rightly understood becomes a guardian, not a jailer.
