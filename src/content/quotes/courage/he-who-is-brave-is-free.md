Seneca links courage and freedom in a single stroke: the brave person is free because fear no longer rules them. A coward may live in comfort, but every choice is made by fear — what to say, where to go, what to risk — and that is a kind of prison. The brave person may face danger, but their choices are their own. Freedom is not the absence of threat; it is the absence of fear's control over us. To be brave, in Seneca's sense, is to be free.
