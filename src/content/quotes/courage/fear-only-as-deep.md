Fear has no fixed size. The same situation can terrify one person and barely trouble another, because fear grows or shrinks with the thoughts we feed it. When we dwell on the worst outcome, fear deepens until it feels bottomless. When we limit it to the facts and take one step at a time, it becomes shallow enough to cross. The proverb hands us back control: we cannot always choose what frightens us, but we can choose how much room we give it in our minds. Fear is only as deep as we allow it to be.
