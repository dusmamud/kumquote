Roosevelt offers a stoic kind of courage: acceptance. Life will bring things we did not choose — loss, illness, disappointment — and resisting reality only adds suffering. Acceptance is not surrender but clarity: this is what has come, and the question is how we meet it. Meeting it with the best we have to give turns hardship into a measure of character rather than a verdict on our worth. We cannot control what arrives, but we can control the quality of our response, and that is where courage lives.
