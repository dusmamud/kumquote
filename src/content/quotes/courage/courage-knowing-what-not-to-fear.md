Fear itself is not the problem; misplaced fear is. We fear embarrassment, rejection, or failure, while ignoring the quieter dangers of a life never attempted. Plato's definition turns courage into a matter of judgment rather than boldness. The courageous person is not the one who fears nothing, but the one who has learned which fears deserve attention and which can be set aside. Knowing what not to fear frees an enormous amount of energy. Courage, then, is less about suppressing fear and more about sorting it wisely.
