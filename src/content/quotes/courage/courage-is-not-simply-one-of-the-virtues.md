Virtues are easy to praise when life is comfortable. Honesty, loyalty, and kindness cost little when no one is testing them. The testing point is where a virtue becomes real: when telling the truth risks punishment, when loyalty demands sacrifice, when kindness requires standing alone. At that moment, every virtue takes the form of courage. Lewis shows us that courage is not separate from goodness; it is goodness under pressure. Without it, our virtues are only intentions. With it, they become actions that hold firm when they matter most.
