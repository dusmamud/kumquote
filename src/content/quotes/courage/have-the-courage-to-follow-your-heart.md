Logic and other people's expectations are loud, but the heart often knows the direction before the mind can explain it. Jobs urges us to trust that quieter voice — the intuition that points toward the work, the place, or the life that feels genuinely ours. Following it takes courage, because it may mean disappointing others or leaving a safe path. Yet the greatest regrets rarely come from listening to the heart; they come from ignoring it. Courage, here, is not recklessness but fidelity to what we know matters most, even when the reasons are hard to put into words.
