Brave people are not fearless; they simply weigh their fear against something that matters more. A parent runs into danger for a child, a witness speaks up despite the risk, and a patient faces treatment because health is worth the fear. Courage is therefore a judgment, a quiet decision about what counts. Fear is still present, loud as ever, but it is no longer the deciding voice. When we know what we value most, fear becomes information rather than a command, and acting in spite of it becomes possible.
