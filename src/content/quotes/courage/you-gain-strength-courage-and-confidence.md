Roosevelt describes courage as something that grows through use. Each time we face a fear directly instead of avoiding it, we collect evidence that we can survive hard things — and that evidence becomes confidence for the next challenge. Fear shrinks when it is faced, and grows when it is avoided. The advice to do the thing you think you cannot do sounds demanding, but it is really an invitation: the thing you fear is often the door to your own strength. Every frightening experience survived adds to a quiet inner record.
