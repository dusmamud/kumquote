Aristotle links courage directly to freedom: as long as fear rules us, we are not free, whatever our circumstances. Fear narrows choices, silences opinions, and keeps us in safe harbors. Overcoming fear does not mean never feeling it, but refusing to let it decide. The person who has faced their fears discovers a new kind of liberty — the freedom to act according to judgment rather than anxiety. True freedom, then, is not external but internal, won in the quiet victories over our own fears, one at a time.
