The fear of failing can be so strong that it keeps us from trying, and that is the one failure that is certain. Ruth struck out often, yet he kept stepping up to the plate, because the chance of a hit was worth the risk. The same is true beyond baseball: every attempt carries the possibility of embarrassment, but refusing to play guarantees nothing good happens. This idea asks us to measure the real cost of trying against the hidden cost of never trying. Playing the game, win or lose, is how we learn, grow, and sometimes surprise ourselves.
